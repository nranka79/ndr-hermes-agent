# School Medical Absence Communication

Recurring pattern: a parent (NDR) needs to email a school (Aditi) about a child's (Ruhaan) absence due to a known recurring medical condition. Two distinct sub-patterns.

---

## Pattern A — Reply to a Doctor Certificate Demand

The school demands a medical certificate for 3+ days of absence. The child has a known, recurring medical condition (e.g. viral → asthmatic flare-up → persistent cough) and the parent doesn't want an unnecessary hospital visit.

### Tone Doctrine
- **Polite and cooperative** — never confrontational
- **Make the case** — explain the known condition, the pattern, why a hospital visit is counterproductive
- **Offer compliance** — if the certificate is absolutely mandatory (SOP, board requirement), agree to get one
- **Seek understanding** — ask for the basis of the requirement to find an alternative
- **Key phrase:** "We would, however, appreciate understanding the basis for this requirement"

### Email Structure

1. **Thank and acknowledge** — "Thank you for your response and for accommodating [child]'s test schedule."
2. **Provide medical context** — "[Child] has an established medical pattern — whenever he contracts a viral infection, it triggers an asthmatic flare-up that results in a persistent, long-running cough. This is a known, recurring issue."
3. **Explain the current course of action** — "Following our doctor's standing advice, we have started him on azithromycin without a hospital visit this time."
4. **Explain why a hospital visit is not ideal** — "Taking him to a hospital simply to obtain a certificate would mean an unnecessary visit — putting him under additional strain and exposing him to further risk of infection."
5. **Offer to comply if mandatory** — "If the school absolutely requires a doctor's certificate as a mandatory formality, we are happy to take him and obtain one."
6. **Ask for the rationale gently** — "Is it a board requirement? A school policy? Perhaps we can find an alternative that meets your requirement without necessitating a medical visit."
7. **Close with gratitude** — "We would be grateful for your guidance."

### Known School SOP (confirmed Aug 2026 — Aditi)
- For Grade 9+ students registered with a Board (IGCSE/ICSE): absence of 3+ days requires a medical certificate
- This is communicated through the High School Processes and Procedures document
- The request is NOT questioning the authenticity — it's an established SOP the class teachers must follow
- Response should acknowledge this understanding: "We fully understand the school's need to maintain proper records"

## Pattern B — Fresh Notification of Exam Absence

The child is sick and will likely miss a scheduled exam. Notify in advance and ask for accommodation.

### Email Structure

1. **Inform directly** — "Given [child]'s condition today, it is unlikely he will be able to attend school tomorrow."
2. **Name the exam** — "We understand there is a [subject] test scheduled."
3. **Propose options** — "Would it be possible for [child] to take the exam on an alternate day? Alternatively, if permissible, could he come in solely for the exam, sit separately, and leave immediately after?"
4. **Mention medical updates** — "We are also in the process of trying to reach his doctor. Depending on what the doctor advises, we will confirm whether [child] is up to date on the material."
5. **Ask for guidance** — "Please let us know your recommendation. We are very happy to cooperate with whatever arrangement works best for the school."

### Recipient Selection

- **To:** The class teachers (both, if there are two)
- **Cc:** HOD/coordinator (usually CC'd on the original chain), parent (Roshni), the child
- **Bcc:** School administrator (if applicable, e.g. Joel)

## Pattern C — Doctor Certificate Request via WhatsApp (No Physical Visit)

When a parent needs a doctor's certificate for school but the child cannot be taken to hospital (risk of infection, known recurring condition, unnecessary strain). Request the certificate via WhatsApp.

### Message Structure

1. **Establish relationship** — "Doctor, as you know, last time also you have seen [child]."
2. **Describe current illness** — "Unfortunately he's again got a flu and the cough episodes have started again."
3. **Mention current treatment** — "We started him on azithromycin based on Dr. [previous doctor]'s recommendation to control the neutrophilic flare-up of asthma."
4. **Explain the school situation** — "The school is absolutely insisting that we get a doctor's certificate. We want to avoid taking him to the hospital with his current condition."
5. **Make the specific request** — "Can you help me with a doctor's certificate that says:"
   - This is a repeat/known condition
   - Whenever he develops a flu, he will unfortunately be out for a week to ten days
   - He's currently down with flu and cough
   - Advised rest until fully settled (may need another week)
   - Sick since [specific date]
6. **Urgency** — "I would need it urgently because he has a test tomorrow."
7. **Offer to draft (light/jovial close)** — "If you want, I can get my AI agent to draft a quick one for you if that helps. But I don't want to overstep." (Add a smiley to keep it light.)

### Key Details to Include
- Child's name
- Previous doctor's name (who established the diagnosis)
- Date when illness began (sick since Monday, DD Month YYYY)
- Expected recovery timeline (another week from today)
- Known condition description: "flu → asthmatic flare-up → persistent cough for 7-10 days"

## Pitfalls

- **Don't conflate siblings' class teachers.** Neetu Shrivastava and Subath Senan are Rivan's (younger child) Std 07 class teachers, not Ruhaan's (Grade 9). Verify which child's school is being discussed.
- **Old vs new academic year.** Class teacher assignments change yearly. A welcome PDF from the previous academic year may reference different teachers. Always check the date and academic year on the document.
- **Grade 9 = IGCSE Year 2.** The school's SOP requiring certificates kicks in when the child is registered with a Board. This is noted in the High School Processes document.
- **Bcc vs Cc.** School administrators often need to be BCC'd, not CC'd (per NDR's preference — Joel was BCC'd to keep him informed without making it visible to the reply-all chain).
- **Don't create WhatsApp certificate messages before confirming the doctor's number exists.** This session: Dr. Nishant Hirebat was not found in any contact source (not in contacts sheet, not in Gmail across all three accounts, not in People API). Always confirm the recipient exists before composing.
