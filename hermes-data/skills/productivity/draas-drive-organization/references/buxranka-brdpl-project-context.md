## Pattern: "get <third party> to write to <counterparty>"

When NDR says *"write to Anbu asking him to get Manju to simply write to them that…"*, the deliverable is
**an internal instruction email to our own colleague** — NOT an email to the counterparty, and NOT an email to
the third party. Anbu is not a party to the Godrej thread; sending this to Harsimran or Manju would be wrong.

What worked (verified 2026-09-17):
1. Resolve the strand first — Gmail search by sender domain / person name, read the whole thread, and pull the
   quotation PDF attachment (it carries the amounts, the bank details, and the conditions to quote back).
2. Draft a **reply on the existing thread** (threadId + In-Reply-To/References on the NDR→Anbu forward) so Anbu
   sees Harsimran's authorization directly below. **To: the colleague only, Cc: none** — an internal instruction
   must not leak to the counterparty or the consultant.
3. Body shape: (a) one line of context — who authorized what and that they asked what's needed; (b) the numbered
   asks, anchored to the third party's OWN quotation terms; (c) **a ready-to-use verbatim block the third party
   can copy** — this is the highest-value part, it removes all ambiguity; (d) a nudge to send it immediately and
   who to keep in copy.
4. Anchor every number to the source document, not to voice. NDR said "3.5 lakhs advance" — the quotation
   confirms ₹3,50,000 out of ₹5,90,000 and makes the DWG + advance a joint starting condition.
5. Create the DRAFT only, verify To/Cc/Subject/threadId by reading it back, then deliver it to NDR in the same
   turn (drafts are inert — see `confirm-before-actions`).
6. Flag anomalies separately to NDR rather than in the email: the Excel Consulting Services vs Manju DS
   mismatch, the GSTIN typo, and the second address `anbarasan@draas.com` in case he wants it cc'd.
