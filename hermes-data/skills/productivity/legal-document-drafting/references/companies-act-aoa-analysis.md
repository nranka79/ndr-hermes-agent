# Companies Act 2013 — AoA & Shorter-Notice EGM Analysis

Reference for analyzing AoA amendments, Section 101 shorter-notice provisions, minority shareholder risk, and NCD/public deposit questions for DRA Group private companies.

## Core Legal Framework

### Section 101 — Notice of General Meetings
- Default: **21 clear days** notice for any general meeting
- **Proviso**: Shorter notice allowed if 95% of members entitled to vote consent in writing
- Section 101 applies to private companies **unless the articles otherwise provide**
  - Source: **MCA Notification GSR 464(E) dated 5 June 2015** — Sections 101-109 apply to private companies "unless the articles otherwise provide"
  - This means the AoA can completely override the 21-day requirement — no 95% consent needed per-meeting if the AoA itself provides for shorter notice

### Section 462 — Power to Exempt
- Before adopting shorter-notice provisions, verify the company has no filing defaults (delayed financial statements, annual returns) — defaults can render the company ineligible under Section 462 exemptions

### NCDs vs Public Deposits (the "public money" objection)
- **Rule 2(1)(c)(xvii)** of Companies (Acceptance of Deposits) Rules, 2014 explicitly excludes from "deposit":
  > "any amount received by a company by way of **debentures** or bonds or by way of subscription to any debt instrument"
- NCDs issued via private placement through a debenture trustee are **NOT public deposits**
- NCD holders have **no voting rights** under Section 71 — they are creditors, not members
- Therefore, NCDs do NOT restrict the company's ability to adopt shorter-notice EGM provisions

### Section 73 — Acceptance of Deposits
- Restricts companies from accepting deposits from the public
- Private companies are exempt from Section 73(2)(a)-(e) if they accept monies from members not exceeding 100% of paid-up share capital + free reserves
- NOT relevant to debentures or meeting notice periods

## AoA Amendment — Shorter Notice EGM Clause

### Standard 21-Day Notice
- Section 101(1): minimum 21 clear days
- For private companies: AoA can specify a shorter period (GSR 464(E))

### Key Risk: Speed vs Protection
| Aspect | 21-Day Notice | 5-Day Notice |
|--------|--------------|--------------|
| Minority response time | Sufficient to coordinate, seek advice | Very limited |
| Risk for 35% holder | Low — can block special resolutions anyway | Moderate — can't block but can be rushed |
| Protection mechanism | Time to seek injunction if needed | May not have time |

### Recommended Safeguard: Director's Extension Right
Add a clause: *"If any director considers the agenda or supporting material to be substantial enough to require additional review time, they may request an extension. On such request, an appropriate extension shall be granted, up to a maximum of 21 days from the original notice date."*

**Drafting notes:**
- Use "any director" not "a particular director" to avoid personalization
- Cap at 21 days (the statutory default, not exceeding it)
- Use cooperative language ("shall be granted" not "may be granted")
- This preserves fairness while preventing rushed resolutions

## Minority Shareholder Risk Assessment

### For 35% Holder vs 65% Majority
- **Section 14 (AoA amendment)**: Requires Special Resolution (75% majority) — 35% CAN block amendments
- **Ordinary resolutions**: Only need 51% — 35% cannot block operational decisions
- **Shorter-notice clause adoption**: Requires 75% — majority CAN pass it without minority consent
- **Key risk**: NOT the adoption itself (same 75% protects against reversal), but the SPEED of future EGMs (5 days instead of 21)

### The Real Protection: Shareholders' Agreement (SHA)
Unlike the AoA (amendable by 75% majority), an SHA requires **unanimous consent** of all parties to amend. Key protections to lock in SHA:
- Related party transaction approval thresholds
- NCD/borrowing limits and board approval requirements
- Board composition and veto rights
- Minority veto on specific matters (asset sales, major borrowing, change of business)
- Drag-along/tag-along rights

## MCA Notification GSR 464(E) — Key Exemptions for Private Companies

| Section | Subject | Private Company Position |
|---------|---------|------------------------|
| 62(1)(a) | Rights issue period | Can reduce below prescribed period with 90% member consent |
| 67 | Loans for share purchase | Exempted if borrowings < 2x paid-up capital or ₹50Cr |
| 73(2)(a)-(e) | Deposit restrictions | Exempted if member deposits ≤ 100% of paid-up+free reserves |
| **101-109** | **Meetings (notice, quorum, proxies, voting)** | **Apply UNLESS articles otherwise provide** |
| 117(3)(g) | Filing board resolutions | Not required for Sec 179(3) matters |
| 160 | Standing for directorship | Does not apply |
| 162 | Appointment voting | Does not apply |
| 180 | Board powers restrictions | Does not apply |
| 184(2) | Director interest disclosure | Interested director can participate after disclosure |
| 185 | Loans to directors | Modified applicability |
| 188(1) | Related party transactions | No voting restriction on related party members |

## Key Pitfalls
- **Section 136 trap**: Even if AoA allows shorter meeting notice, sending financial statements on shorter notice requires separate 95% consent — applies to AGMs, not EGMs
- **SEBI LODR**: If NCDs are listed, debenture trustee notice obligations exist for matters affecting debenture holder interests — separate from EGM notice
- **Company filing defaults**: Verify no RoC filing defaults before adopting shorter-notice provisions
- **SHA over AoA**: The AoA is the wrong place to lock minority protections — push critical protections to a Shareholders' Agreement
