# Indian Partnership Act — Registration Forms (Form A vs Form C)

## Context

When dealing with partnership deeds for DRAAS entities (Saveganapalli Land Partners, DRA Thindlu Land Partners, etc.), understanding the registration forms under the **Indian Partnership Act, 1932** is essential — especially when advocates request specific documents during litigation.

## Two Key Documents

| Form | What It Is | Who Issues It | Status for Saveganapalli LP |
|:-----|:-----------|:-------------|:-----------------------------|
| **Form A** | The **application for registration** of the firm, filed under **Section 58(1)** of the Indian Partnership Act with the Registrar of Firms. Contains: partners' names, firm name, place of business, duration, capital contribution. | Filed by the partners, submitted to the jurisdictional Registrar of Firms | ❌ **Not in records** — must be obtained from Registrar's office |
| **Form C** | The **Certificate / Acknowledgement of Registration** issued after the Registrar processes Form A, confirming the firm has been entered in the Register of Firms. Issued under **Section 59**. In Karnataka, this is titled "Acknowledgement of Registration of Firm" in Form C format. | Issued by the **Registrar of Firms** under Section 59 of the Act | ✅ **Found in Drive** — `Ack Of firm Registration Sevaganapalli Land partners.pdf` |

## Terminology Variation Across States

Different Indian states use different form naming conventions:

| State | Certificate Form | Notes |
|:------|:----------------|:------|
| **Karnataka** | **Form C** | Issued by District Registrar (Shivajinagar, Rajajinagar, etc.) |
| **Tamil Nadu** | **Form D** | Equivalent document under TN rules |
| **Maharashtra** | **Form V** | Under Maharashtra Partnership Rules |
| **Others** | Varies | Always check the state-specific rules |

**Key lesson:** Do NOT assume "Form D" means the same thing across states. In Karnataka partnership registration, the certificate is Form C. What I (the agent) previously called "Form D" was incorrect for the Karnataka context.

## How to Obtain Form A

Since Form A is the original application (not a certificate issued by the government), it's not automatically in the firm's possession. Options:

### Route 1 — From the Registrar of Firms
- **Jurisdiction:** Office of the Registrar of Firms for the district where the firm is registered
- **Process:** File an application for certified copy of Form A
- **Fee:** Nominal (₹100–₹500)
- **Documents needed:** Firm registration number (e.g., SJN-F490-2023-24), name of firm
- **Timeline:** Usually 3–7 working days

### Route 2 — From the firm's own records
- Check the chartered accountant / company secretary who handled the original registration
- Look for acknowledgment receipts from when Form A was originally filed

### Route 3 — Through legal counsel
- Ask your advocate to requisition certified copies from the Registrar's office
- Most law firms have paralegals who handle this routinely
- This is the recommended approach when litigation is already active

## Significance in Litigation

Form A is relevant in partnership disputes because it typically contains:
- The original list of partners at the time of registration
- Each partner's contribution and profit-sharing ratio
- The date the partnership was constituted

This becomes critical when a dispute involves **whether someone was a partner** at a given date (e.g., D5 Srinivasa Krishnappa's retirement from Saveganapalli Land Partners).

**Practical note from Fair Order analysis (Jul 2026):** The Fair Order in IA 1/2025 in OS 7/2025 already confirmed D5's retirement using the Reconstitution Deed and other evidence. Form A may not be strictly necessary for the current CMA 742/2026 if the Certificate of Registration (Form C) + Partnership Deed + Reconstitution Deed together establish the partner composition. But having the complete set is always better for evidentiary purposes.
