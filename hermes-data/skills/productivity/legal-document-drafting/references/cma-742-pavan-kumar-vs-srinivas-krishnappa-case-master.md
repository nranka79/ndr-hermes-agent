## 10 GROUNDS OF OPPOSITION (D4/D5)

### Ground 1 — Petitioner's Own Plaint Is a Fatal Admission
Paras 3–4 of Pavan's plaint admit transactions were exclusively with D1/D2. He identifies Kakkannur Survey 93/1A as subject property — entirely unrelated to Saveganapalli LP. He makes no mention of D4 or D5 as parties, guarantors, or partners in the underlying transaction. Under Order 8 Rule 5 CPC and the doctrine of approbate and reprobate, he cannot now claim otherwise.

> *Kumar Sen v. Dhanpat Kumar* (2012) 12 SCC 203; *Hiralal v. Daxaben* (2019) 7 SCC 000

### Ground 2 — Srinivasa Had No Economic Interest (1% = ₹1,000)
Srinivasa contributed exactly ₹1,000 to a partnership that acquired lands worth several crores. The Reconstitution Deed (12.12.2024) expressly states his 1% held no value beyond original capital. Under Section 25 Partnership Act 1932, his interest is proportionate to capital contribution — which was nil in economic terms. Even if Pavan's partnership theory is accepted, Srinivasa had no proprietary interest in partnership lands to claim.

> *Lakshmanprasad v. Babu Ram* (2003) 9 SCC 623; *Ramaswamy Iyer v. Brahmayya & Co.* (1966) 2 SCR 147

### Ground 3 — Sale Deed Was Not Pendente Lite
Sale Deed 21201/2023 registered 16 October 2023. OS 7/2025 filed January 2025 — 14 months later. Section 52 TPA (lis pendens) requires the suit to be pending at the time of transfer. There was no litigation at the relevant date. Additionally, **Pavan Kumar himself was the GPA holder** who executed the B-schedule sale — he cannot challenge a transaction he himself authorised.

> *Sharadamma v. Kempegowda* (2012) 3 Kant LJ 3562

### Ground 4 — Saveganapalli LP Has Absolute Title via Registered Sale Deed
Saveganapalli LP holds indefeasible title via Sale Deed 21201/2023. The Petitioner is seeking to attach property he himself sold and received full payment for. An O38 R5 attachment order cannot defeat the title of a bona fide purchaser for value who purchased before any litigation.

> *M. Venkatesh v. Commissioner of Police* (2019) 10 SCC 195; *Lakshmi Vilas Palace v. Tirupur Co-op. Bank* (2019) 5 SCC 672

### Ground 5 — Section 138 Notice Is a Formal Admission of Non-Liability
The Section 138 Legal Notice (22.10.2024), drafted and issued by Petitioner's own advocate (A. Kannadasan, Dharmapuri), named ONLY: (1) Srinivasa Krishnappa, (2) Rohit Krishnappa, (3) Abishek Luthra (Westbury). Nishant Ranka, DRA Realty, and Saveganapalli Land Partners were deliberately excluded. This formal, advocate-signed omission is a binding admission of non-liability. It was issued after the bounced cheques — when Pavan had every motivation to name all parties he believed were liable.

> *Gurudev Singh v. Rajkumar* (2008) 15 SCC 90 (approbate and reprobate)

### Ground 6 — Nishant Ranka's Former Directorship Creates No Present Liability
Nishant is no longer a director, no longer a shareholder, and has no ongoing interest in Westbury. He exited completely. A former director with no current role cannot be made liable for the company's obligations. His own Section 138 notice did not name Nishant — only Abhishek Luthra.

> *Satyagopal Kumar v. Pashupatinath* (2003) 7 SCC 37; *Pioneerudyan v. State Bank of Patiala* (2002) 7 SCC 618

### Ground 7 — CMA Is a Retaliatory Counterblast
Nishant Ranka threatened a recovery suit in December 2024 (for non-delivery of aggregation promises by Pavan). Within weeks, OS 7/2025 was filed (January 2025) — for the first time naming D4 and D5 as defendants. The Petitioner had never named these parties in any prior notice or proceeding. The sequence demonstrates retaliatory inclusion designed to neutralise a threatened recovery action.

> *State of Maharashtra v. Praful B. Desai* (2003) 4 SCC 602; *N.G. Palshikar v. S.B. Palshikar* (2018) 3 SCC 135

### Ground 8 — O38 R5: No Prima Facie Case Against D4 or D5
O38 R5 requires: (a) plaintiff has probable case, (b) defendant has existing right/title in property, (c) defendant likely to deal with property to defeat decree. Against D4: stranger to the loan; properties acquired from Petitioner before any litigation. Against D5: not a party to the loan. ADJ Krishnagiri already rejected this application once.

> *Raman Tech & Process Engg. Co. v. Solanki Traders* (2008) 2 SCC 302; *T.G.P. Pvt. Ltd. v. Additional Commissioner* (2018) 16 SCC 1

### Ground 9 — Wrongful Impleadment as Pressure Tactics
Pavan uses the same facts to pressure multiple unconnected parties. His Section 138 notice against D1/D2 and parallel civil proceedings against D3/D4/D5 shows a calculated strategy to extract payment by dragging strangers into unrelated disputes.

> *Zodiac Estate v. Indian Bank* (2008) 11 SCC 612; *Hindustan Petroleum Corpn. v. S. Narayana* (2015) 8 SCC 230

### Ground 10 — Srinivasa's Post-Retirement Status
At time of OS 7/2025 filing (January 2025), Srinivasa had already retired (Reconstitution Deed 12.12.2024, registered 27.02.2025). The plaint incorrectly describes him as "managing partner" — factually wrong at time of filing. Post-retirement, he has no standing to bind Saveganapalli LP or to create liability for D4/D5.

> Section 31, 32 Partnership Act 1932; *Madhavi v. Raman* (2012) 3 SCC 431
