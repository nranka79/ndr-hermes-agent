## 6 Legal Arguments in Opposition

**1. Plaint discloses cause of action**
- Fraud in FMB (post-sale-deed alteration on 16.09.2024)
- Plan approval on forged/altered documents (01.03.2024)
- Trespass and police interference (02.10.2024 and 28.11.2024)
- Cloud on title from altered sketch uploaded to Dept of Survey website
- Authority: Sun Pharmaceutical Industries vs Reliance Petroleum Ltd. (2022); Kaushalya Rani vs Gopi Krishna (2020)

**2. Revenue officers are NOT courts — no res judicata**
- Tahsildar/Sub-Collector function under TN Survey Act — NOT courts under CPC
- Section 11 CPC requires "court of competent jurisdiction"
- Authority: Ramesh Kumar vs Keshar Bai (SC, 2019); Chand Khan vs IFFCO (SC, 2020); Gurudev vs Vithal (Bom HC, 2018)

**3. WHPL's IA admits boundary dispute exists**
- "the only real grievance is the nature and orientation of the boundaries" — WHPL's own words
- If both parties claim different boundaries, dispute requires full trial, not summary dismissal

**4. Fraud in revenue records requires full investigation**
- Three different sketches admitted by Thalassery to the court
- Naleena/Umadevi have given written statement: signatures on Sub-Collector docs are completely forged
- Extent deviations across all three sub-divisions are telltale signs of hasty/fraudulent preparation

**5. Section 101 TNTCPA cannot protect fraud**
- Plan approval obtained on altered FMB — fraud vitiates all proceedings including statutory approvals
- Authority: Osmanabad Zilla Parishad vs Fatehrao (SC); Bhumika Properties vs Unit Trust of India (SC, 2019)

**6. Tahsildar order is vitiated by procedural errors**
- No hearing to Naleena/Umadevi (whose signatures are alleged to be forged)
- No independent physical survey conducted
- Accepting wrong extents (2A2 ~90c vs 70c, 2A1 57c vs 70c)
- Exceeding jurisdiction by adjudicating on ownership
- Sub-Collector (12.02.2025) removed FMB pending investigation — Tahsildar restored it without explaining why

## 8 Errors in Tahsildar Order (15.05.2025)

1. **No hearing to affected parties** — Naleena @ Naveena and Umadevi were not called for hearing. They are the very persons whose signatures are alleged to be forged. Fundamental violation of natural justice.

2. **No independent physical survey** — Tahsildar did not physically verify boundaries on ground. Findings based entirely on desk review of documents.

3. **Accepting wrong extents without explanation** — 2A2 ~90c (should be 70c), 2A1 57c (should be 70c), 2B 65c (should be 70c). No explanation for these deviations.

4. **No investigation of who altered the sketch** — Sub-Collector (12.02.2025) found "problem" and ordered investigation. Tahsildar did not investigate who altered the FMB, when, and at whose instance.

5. **Exceeding jurisdiction on title** — Held "no dispute regarding ownership and area" — but ownership is precisely what is in dispute in the civil suit. Revenue officers have no jurisdiction to adjudicate title.

6. **Accepting WHPL's version without evidence** — Accepted WHPL's version of "original survey sketch" without independent verification or hearing of persons who prepared it.

7. **No reasoned order** — Does not explain how "original survey sketch" came to be different from revised sketch, why extent deviations are accepted, on what basis Naleena/Umadevi are deemed to have consented.

8. **Procedural lapse in not restoring FMB** — Sub-Collector (12.02.2025) removed FMB pending investigation. Tahsildar (15.05.2025) restored and upheld revised sketch without explaining why Sub-Collector's concern was unfounded.

## 6-Point Oral Argument Structure

**POINT 1 — CAUSE OF ACTION EXISTS**
"The plaintiff purchased S.No.92/2A2 by registered sale deed dated 08.07.2023. The sketch was altered on 16.09.2024 — AFTER the sale deed. The plan approval was obtained on 01.03.2024 based on the altered sketch — a fresh cause of action. The plaintiff has been trespassed upon and police have been used to obstruct entry. The threshold under Rule 11(a) is very high — the plaint must show NO cause of action. Here, the plaint shows MULTIPLE causes of action. The IA must fail."

**POINT 2 — REVENUE OFFICERS ARE NOT COURTS — NO RES JUDICATA**
"The Tahsildar is a revenue officer functioning under the TN Survey Act — he is NOT a court under CPC. Section 11 CPC requires a decision by a court of competent jurisdiction. Revenue officers do not fulfill this requirement. Reference: Ramesh Kumar vs Keshar Bai (SC 2019); Chand Khan vs IFFCO (SC 2020). The defendant's argument that revenue decisions bar this suit is an attempt to expand res judicata beyond its statutory scope."

**POINT 3 — BOUNDARY DISPUTE REQUIRES FULL TRIAL**
"The defendant admits in the IA itself that there is a boundary dispute — 'the only real grievance is the nature and orientation of the boundaries.' If there is a boundary dispute, it must be investigated at trial with evidence and cross-examination — it cannot be decided on a summary dismissal. Multiple sketches exist — this is an admission by the Thalassery that something is wrong."

**POINT 4 — FRAUD IN REVENUE RECORDS REQUIRES FULL INVESTIGATION**
"The plaintiff specifically alleges that Kantharaj and Anandappa (defendant's predecessor vendors) caused the FMB to be altered in 2022. Naleena @ Naveena and Umadevi have given written statements that their signatures on Sub-Collector documents are completely forged. Three different sketches submitted by the Thalassery itself confirm something is wrong. Fraud in revenue records must be investigated at full trial — it cannot be dismissed at the threshold."

**POINT 5 — SECTION 101 TNTCPA CANNOT PROTECT FRAUD**
"The defendant relies on Section 101 TNTCPA to bar the prayer against the plan approval. But Section 101 cannot protect a plan approval obtained by submission of forged/altered documents. The plan approval dated 01.03.2024 was obtained on the basis of the altered FMB — this is fraud. Fraud vitiates all proceedings, including statutory proceedings."

**POINT 6 — TAHILDAR ORDER IS VITIATED BY PROCEDURAL ERRORS**
"The Tahsildar's order (15.05.2025) which the defendant relies on for res judicata is itself vitiated by critical procedural errors. No hearing was given to Naleena @ Naveena and Umadevi. No independent survey was conducted. The order accepts wrong extents. The Tahsildar exceeded his jurisdiction by adjudicating on ownership. A void order cannot create res judicata. This Hon'ble Court must reject the IA and send this matter to full trial."
