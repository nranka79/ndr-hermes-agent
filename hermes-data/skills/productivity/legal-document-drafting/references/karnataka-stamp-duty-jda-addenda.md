# Karnataka Stamp Duty on JDA Addenda — Chargeability Analysis & Case Law

**Class:** Sub-Registrar queries on registering an addendum to a JDA — when the original JDA
duty was paid, whether the addendum (which only varies terms, adds no UDS) is chargeable
AFRESH under Article 5(f), the assignment-deed demand, and the "should we bother registering"
question. Verified Sep-2026 on Ranka NorthStar / Allalsandra (Addendum 2, 30-Nov-2024).

## The statute (Karnataka Stamp Act, 1957 — latest amendments do NOT change rates)
- **Article 5(f)** (as substituted by the 2014 Amendment, w.e.f. 01-03-2014): agreements
  relating to development/construction between owner & developer — duty **2% on the higher of**
  (i) market value of undivided share of land conveyed to developer + consideration + money
  advanced, OR (ii) market value of constructed share delivered to owner + consideration +
  money advanced. **Explanation:** "money advanced" INCLUDES security deposit whether
  refundable or adjustable (this is how the department pulls refundable deposits into the base).
  **Proviso:** if proper duty was paid under Article 41(ea) (GPA for construction) between same
  parties & same property, duty on the corresponding 5(f) agreement shall not exceed ₹200.
- **Article 41(ea)** — mirror GPA clause with the reciprocal ₹200 cap.
- **s.4** — one transaction / multiple instruments: principal instrument bears full duty,
  others nominal (₹100) — legislature's anti-double-duty policy.
- **s.31 / s.32** — adjudication: bring ANY instrument (executed or not) to the Deputy
  Commissioner on ₹100 fee; he determines duty and issues a certificate. s.31(1)(b) relieves
  of penalty on payment of full duty. THE clean procedural route to end a counter dispute.
- **s.34** — no public officer shall register/act upon an instrument unless duly stamped.
  (Argument for the developer: the assignment WAS registered in 2021 → it entered the register
  on the footing it was duly stamped → the State cannot now impugn its own act.)
- **s.46A(1)** — recovery of short/not-levied duty: notice within **5 years** of duty becoming
  payable; **10 years** if fraud/collusion/wilful misstatement/suppression with intent to
  evade. Appeal to Karnataka Appellate Tribunal within 3 months. **A Sub-Registrar has NO power
  to levy under 46A at the counter** — it requires a written show-cause notice from the CCRA
  or authorised officer and a determination after representations.
- **Registration fee** — revised 1% → **2% w.e.f. 31-08-2025** (confirm live; ebiz portal is
  geo-blocked from the server). Nominal for a fixed-duty instrument, but the SRO computes on
  whatever base he adopts.
- General: KBCT 1957 still governs; the Karnataka Stamp (Amendment) Act 2025 (LA Bill 12/2025)
  touches only e-stamping/digital — no rate change.

## When the addendum is NOT chargeable afresh (the argument that wins)
1. **No incremental UDS passes** — Article 5(f)'s taxable event is the undivided share
   conveyed at JDA time; that was paid in 2014. A supplementary instrument that conveys no new
   land interest has no fresh base. The instrument says so itself: "This Addendum is to be
   read as part and parcel of the Agreement dated 07.02.2014."
2. **Amendment agreements are not fresh transactions** — cases:
   - *District Registrar (Administration) v. Tata Communications Data Centres Pvt Ltd* (Madras
     HC DB, Oct-2025): amendment agreements correcting factual errors in a registered lease do
     not gain new property/extra rights → not a fresh chargeable transaction.
   - *Adityaraj Builders v. State of Maharashtra* (Bom HC DB, 17-Feb-2023): once a development
     agreement is stamped, incidental instruments on the same premises cannot be re-assessed
     beyond nominal; "stamp duty cannot be levied twice."
3. **Future/contingent rights are not valued at execution date** — *Wadhwa Construction &
   Infra v. State of Maharashtra* (Bom HC, 07-Sep-2026): duty determined from substance and
   effect of the instrument AS OF its execution date; rights contingent on a future step
   (here: loading TDR/premium FAR at developer's cost, only the 15% uplift on that future FAR)
   "cannot be valued as vested rights"; FSI available at execution governs, not future/maximum.
   → The 15% on additional FAR is NOT chargeable now; when the developer actually buys TDR,
   THAT purchase is itself a fully charged instrument — revenue deferred, not lost.
   (Followed *Suhas Damodar Sathe* 2025 SCC OnLine Bom 576, *Shyamsundar Radheshyam Agrawal*
   (2024) 10 SCC 324, *Kolte Patil Developers*.)
4. **The department's own e-stamp certificate answers the question** — if the addendum was
   e-stamped under residuary Article 5(J) ("Agreement (in any other cases)") at ₹500 with
   consideration Rs. 0 (NorthStar's Addendum 2: e-Stamp IN-KA82505335604880W, 30-Nov-2024), the
   Kaveri/SHCIL system classified it as a residual fixed-duty instrument. Consistency demands
   the same treatment. Put the certificate in front of the SRO.

## The assignment deed — four answers in ascending order of force
1. Payability denied — assignment of developer's rights under a JDA is not a conveyance of
   any land interest (developer holds no UDS at JDA stage) → nominal at most.
2. **s.34** — it was registered (here: Doc 5695, 15-Mar-2021, Malleshwaram) → registered = duly
   stamped on the record.
3. **s.46A limitation** — 5 years from 15-Mar-2021 expired 15-Mar-2026. *Smt. Rajeshwari v.
   State of Karnataka* (KHC, 25-Jun-2026): the 46A limitation is **jurisdictional, not
   procedural**; a notice beyond the outer limit is "functus officio and void"; a bona fide
   subsequent purchaser can't be hit for a vendor's 3-decades-old default.
4. The SRO has no power to levy it at the counter (46A requires CCRA notice + appeal route).
   **Caveat:** *Bharat Petroleum v. District Revenue Officer* (2026 INSC 963) has referred to a
   larger Bench whether fraud must be proved for the 10-year window — cite as pending; do not
   overclaim.

## Should you register the addendum at all? (yes — but not because it's mandatory)
- Not strictly compulsory unless it creates a right/interest in immovable property (s.17(1)(b)
  Registration Act). A pure timeline-extension + deposit-acknowledgement addendum may not be.
- BUT register anyway when it: (a) creates security over landowner inventory (s.17(1)(b)
  triggers; your only protection for the refundable), (b) extends delivery timelines that you
  will rely on, (c) supersedes prior terms ("shall supersede any other previous agreement"),
  (d) will be disclosed to lenders/RERA. Unregistered, s.49 bars it as evidence. Cost is
  trivial on the correct view (₹500 fixed + nominal fee).

## Delivering the opinion — NDR's format preference (Sep-2026)
- For the SRO, he wants a **SHORT opinion** — literally "questions, answers, case law. Very
  simple. Not applicable, not applicable, not applicable." Shape: one-glance summary table
  (question | answer bold No/Yes | principal authority), then per-question blocks of
  Question → Opinion (1 para) → Statute → Authorities. Close politely: if the authority still
  has doubt, request referral to the Deputy Commissioner under s.31 for adjudication — framed
  as protecting the SRO (determination carries penalty waiver + appeal route).
- A LONG opinion exists for the file/counsel (22 A4 pages with letterhead, privileged banner,
  numbered paragraphs, statute block-quotes, table of authorities). Render HTML → PDF via
  chromium headless; upload public-read to Drive. The DNR law-firm letterhead format lives in
  `legal-document-drafting` → `references/law-firm-opinion-letters.md`.
- Have counsel vet before the SRO sees it — this is research-backed drafting, not a signed
  professional opinion.

## Pitfalls
- **The e-stamp certificate's "Description of Document" line is the strongest single
  evidence** — read it BEFORE writing the opinion; it often decides the argument.
- Check GPAs related to the project: if any was stamped under Article 41(ea) between same
  parties/same property, the ₹200 proviso caps the whole 5(f) argument — cheapest possible win.
- Draft "Further Addendum" sharing-single-unit instruments are a DIFFERENT, clearly chargeable
  event (they transfer a specific constructed unit) — do not bundle them with the plain
  addendum registration.
- The death certificate date is often the issue/registration date, not the date of death —
  reconcile before asserting succession facts.
