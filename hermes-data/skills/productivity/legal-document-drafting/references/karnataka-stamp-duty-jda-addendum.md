# Karnataka Stamp Duty — JDA, Addendum to JDA, and Assignment

Condensed research bank for the recurring DRAAS question: **what duty and
registration charges are payable on a JDA, an addendum to a JDA, and an
assignment of the developer's rights?** Built while answering a Sub-Registrar's
queries on the Ranka North Star (Allalasandra) addendum, September 2026.

> Re-verify citations against SCC/Manupatra before anything goes into a filing.
> Two of the authorities below are very recent and the statutory position can
> move. The substance is stable; the pinpoint cites should be re-confirmed.

---

## 1. The statute

**Karnataka Stamp Act, 1957** (Karnataka Act 34 of 1957).

**Latest amendment:** Karnataka Stamp (Amendment) Act, 2025 (LA Bill No. 12 of
2025). It amends ss.2(e), 2(f), 2(h) and s.10/s.10-A only — to bring electronic
stamping within "duly stamped", recognise electronic signatures, and enable
electronic payment. **There is no rate change.** Do not be told otherwise at a
counter.

### The charging provision for a JDA — Article 5(f)

As **substituted by the Karnataka Stamp (Amendment) Act, 2014, w.e.f.
01-03-2014.** Duty is **two rupees for every one hundred rupees or part thereof
(i.e. 2%)** on:

- the **market value of the undivided share / portion of land conveyed to the
  developer**, **plus consideration and money advanced, if any**; **OR**
- the **market value of the share / portion of the constructed or developed
  building delivered to the owner**, **plus consideration and money advanced**;

**whichever is higher.**

The taxable event is the **conveyance of the developer's undivided share**. The
constructed-share limb is an alternative measure, not a separate charge.

**Proviso (the one that ends arguments):** if the proper duty has been paid under
**Article 41(ea)** on a **power of attorney executed between the same parties and
in respect of the same property**, then duty under Article 5(f) **shall not exceed
rupees two hundred**. Article 41(ea) carries the reciprocal proviso.

> **Always check this first.** If a development-side GPA exists on which duty was
> paid under Article 41(ea), the whole 2% argument collapses at once. For Ranka
> North Star the GPAs in favour of DRA Projects were BK-LV 320/2013-2014,
> BK-JV 43/2012-2013 and BK-TV 321/2013-2014 — pull the registered copies before
> arguing anything else.

**Explanation to Article 5(f):** "money advanced" **means and includes the
security deposit whether refundable or adjustable**. This is the hook the
department uses to pull refundable deposits into the base. Counter it by arguing
Article 5(f) does not apply afresh to a supplemental instrument at all — **not**
by arguing the deposits are outside the definition. Argue it on the numbers and
you hand them a base.

### The residual clause — Article 5(J)

**"Agreement (in any other cases)"** — a fixed nominal duty. This is the residual
head for agreements that are not chargeable under a specific clause.

### Anti-double-duty provisions

- **Section 4** — where several instruments are employed in a single transaction
  of sale, mortgage or settlement, only the **principal instrument** bears full
  duty; each other instrument bears a **nominal duty of ₹100** (substituted
  w.e.f. 01-04-1995). Drafted for sale/mortgage/settlement only, so a JDA is not
  literally within it — but it embodies the legislature's settled policy that one
  economic transaction is not taxed twice. Cite it as policy, not as a direct
  charging answer.
- **Sections 5 and 6** — distinct matters are aggregated; an instrument falling
  under several descriptions takes the **highest** duty applicable to any one
  description. Note s.6 does **not** permit aggregating inchoate future rights to
  enlarge the measure.

### Recovery, limitation and adjudication

- **Section 31** — **adjudication.** Any person may produce an instrument,
  executed or not, before the **Deputy Commissioner (Stamps)** on a **₹100 fee**,
  for determination of the duty chargeable. **Section 32** requires the Deputy
  Commissioner to endorse a certificate in the prescribed form. The **proviso to
  s.31(1)(b)** relieves the person from liability to **penalty** where the full
  duty so determined is paid. This is the single highest-value procedural move —
  it converts an oral counter-dispute into a binding, appealable determination
  and takes the Sub-Registrar's discretion out of the picture.
- **Section 33** — examination and impounding of instruments produced before a
  public officer. **Section 34** — no instrument chargeable with duty shall be
  **acted upon, registered or authenticated** by any public officer unless duly
  stamped; if insufficiently stamped it may be admitted on payment of the deficit
  plus penalty.
- **Section 46** — recovery of duties and penalties; s.46(2) makes all duties and
  penalties a **charge on the property** which is the subject matter.
- **Section 46A** — recovery of stamp duty **not levied or short levied**. Notice
  must be served **within five years** from the commencement of the Karnataka
  Stamp (Amendment) Act, 1980 **or the date on which the duty became payable,
  whichever is later**; the period reads **ten years** where non-payment is by
  reason of **fraud, collusion, wilful mis-statement, suppression of facts, or
  contravention with intent to evade** duty. Appeal to the **Karnataka Appellate
  Tribunal within three months**.

**Critical procedural point:** recovery under s.46A requires a **written
show-cause notice from the Chief Controlling Revenue Authority or an authorised
officer**, a determination after considering representations, and carries an
appeal. A **Sub-Registrar has no power to levy duty under s.46A at the counter**,
and no power to condition registration of instrument A on payment of duty
allegedly due on instrument B. His ss.33–34 powers operate only on the instrument
actually produced before him. Say this plainly and politely.

### Registration fee

Revised to **2% of value with effect from 31-08-2025** (previously 1%), per the
State's August 2025 notification; JDAs and GPA-linked-to-JDA instruments were
brought within the hike. **Confirm the current figure and get the Kaveri written
estimate in advance** — do not discover it on the day. (The official Karnataka
levies table at ebiz.karnataka.gov.in is geo-blocked from the Hermes server; get
the number locally.)

### Registration Act, 1908

- **s.17(1)(b)** — compulsory registration of non-testamentary instruments
  creating, declaring, assigning, limiting or extinguishing any right, title or
  interest in immovable property of value ₹100 and upwards.
- **s.49** — an unregistered instrument required to be registered is
  inadmissible as evidence of that transaction.

## 2. Case law

### On contingent / future rights — the best authority

**_Wadhwa Construction and Infrastructure Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Maharashtra_,
Bombay High Court, 7 September 2026.** The governing principle: stamp duty **must
be determined from the substance and effect of the instrument as on its date of
execution**. Development rights contingent on a future "Swap Notice" were **"not
presently transferred development rights and could not be valued as vested
rights"** on the execution date. The applicable FSI is the FSI **actually
available on the date of execution**, not future or maximum FSI. And the strict
construction rule for fiscal statutes: **"consideration or development potential
not operative under the instrument on the relevant date cannot be added by
implication."**

Followed: **_Suhas Damodar Sathe v. State of Maharashtra_**, 2025 SCC OnLine Bom
576; **_Shyamsundar Radheshyam Agrawal v. Pushpabai Nilkanth Patil_**, (2024) 10
SCC 324; **_Kolte Patil Developers Ltd._**

> This is the authority for excluding a landowner entitlement to a share of
> **future TDR / premium FAR** from the measure of duty, where the entitlement is
> contingent, optional and funded at the developer's cost.

### On supplemental / amendment instruments

**_Adityaraj Builders v. State of Maharashtra_**, Bombay High Court, **Division
Bench** (G.S. Patel and Neela Gokhale JJ.), 17 February 2023. Once the
development agreement is stamped, an incidental instrument relating to the same
premises **cannot be separately assessed beyond the nominal requirement**;
**"stamp duty cannot be levied twice"**; members were not "purchasers" of areas
to which they were already entitled; duty payable only to the extent of any
**additional area purchased for actual stated consideration**. Contradictory
departmental circulars were **struck down.**

**_District Registrar (Administration) v. Tata Communications Data Centres Pvt.
Ltd._**, Madras High Court, **Division Bench**. An **amendment agreement** to a
registered lease deed does not constitute a **fresh transaction** for levying
additional stamp duty — "by executing the amendment agreement the lessee did not
gain any new property or extra rights." Provisions considered: Indian Stamp Act
s.2(14); Transfer of Property Act s.105.

> These two together are the backbone of a "no fresh charge on an addendum" brief.

### On limitation of recovery — the strongest defence against reopening an old document

**_Smt. Rajeshwari w/o Muttanna Biraj v. The State of Karnataka_**, Karnataka High
Court, judgment dated **25 June 2026**. Under **s.46A(1)** the power to recover is
**not "unbridled"**; the limitation is **"not merely procedural but
jurisdictional"**; a notice issued beyond the maximum period is **"functus
officio and void"**; a subsequent **bona fide purchaser** cannot be made liable for
the alleged default of a vendor of **three decades earlier** (on the facts, a 1993
sale deed with a demand raised in 2025 — quashed).

> Note the a fortiori use: if it protects a later purchaser from a vendor's
> decades-old default, it protects a developer whose transaction was **registered
> by the department itself**.

### On impounding and penalty

**_Seetharama Shetty v. Monappa Shetty_**, 2024 INSC 650 — Supreme Court
summarising the sequence for impounding and imposing penalty under **ss.33, 34,
35** of the Karnataka Stamp Act. Relevant if an officer threatens impounding.

### Live / unsettled

**_Bharat Petroleum Corporation Ltd. v. District Revenue Officer (Stamps)_**, 2026
INSC 963 — the Supreme Court has **referred to a larger Bench** the question
whether stamp duty undervaluation proceedings can be initiated **without proof of
fraudulent intent** under the parallel s.47-A regime. This cuts both ways: it is
the authority the department would use to get itself into the ten-year window on a
bare allegation. **Reserve it expressly** rather than being ambushed by it. The
outer limit under s.46A nonetheless remains a hard bar.

## 3. Reading the executed instrument — the decisive technique

**Page 1 of an executed Indian instrument's PDF carries an e-Stamp Certificate**
in a data block. **Read it before arguing anything.** It is the State's own
classification of the document and frequently decides chargeability outright.

Extract: certificate number · issue date and time · **"Description of Document"**
(the Article under the stamp schedule) · property description · consideration ·
**stamp duty paid**.

Worked example — Ranka North Star First Addendum, executed 30 November 2024:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| e-Stamp Certificate No. | IN-KA82505335604880W |
| Issued | 30-Nov-2024, 03:44 PM |
| Description of Document | **Article 5(J) — Agreement (in any other cases)** |
| Property | "ADDENDUM TO JOINT DEVELOPMENT AGREEMENT DATED 07.02.2014" |
| Consideration | Rs. 0 (Zero) |
| First Party / Second Party | V SWAMINATHAN AND OTHERS / DRA RANKA HOLDINGS |
| Stamp Duty Paid | **Rs. 500** |

The State's own Kaveri/SHCIL system classified the addendum as a **residual
fixed-duty agreement**, **not** a 2% Article 5(f) development agreement, and
accepted ₹500. **Put the certificate in front of the officer** — consistency
demands the same treatment on re-presentation. This is usually the strongest
single exhibit available and it costs nothing to produce.

### Also read the recitals and operative clauses properly

- **Recitals** give the chain: original agreement date + document number + book +
  Sub-Registrar; assignment date and number; deaths, Wills and their registration
  numbers. Check whether each party signed **in their own right** or **through a
  GPA holder** — the recital usually says so expressly ("duly represented by
  their GPA Holder Sri. …"). This determines who must physically attend
  registration.
- **The schedule** maps each owner to their site, khata number, area, and
  acquisition sale deed. Site-number-to-owner mapping is the fastest way to
  answer "who is party to what".
- **The supersession clause** matters — an unregistered instrument purporting to
  supersede a registered one creates a direct conflict on the record.
- **The security clause** is what makes an otherwise non-chargeable addendum
  compulsorily registrable, because it creates an interest in immovable property.

## 4. Reusable argument skeleton

**Q1 — Is the addendum chargeable afresh under Article 5(f)?**
No. No fresh undivided share of land passes; the UDS was conveyed and stamped by
the original JDA; the addendum is expressly "to be read as part and parcel of" it
and creates no new right for consideration. *Adityaraj Builders* + *Tata
Communications* + s.4 policy + the e-stamp certificate's own Article 5(J)
classification.

**Q2 — Is a landowner share of future TDR / premium FAR in the measure?**
No. Contingent, optional, developer-funded, not operative on the execution date.
*Wadhwa*. **Add the revenue-neutrality point:** when the TDR is actually
purchased, that purchase is itself an instrument bearing full duty and
registration, and the incremental FAR becomes consideration in that transaction.
**Duty deferred, not avoided — the State loses nothing**, so no strained
construction is justified.

**Q3 — Can duty on an old assignment be claimed now?**
No, on three independent grounds: (i) it was registered, and **s.34 forbids**
registration unless duly stamped, so the State cannot now impugn its own act;
(ii) **s.46A(1)** — five years expired, no fraud alleged, *Rajeshwari* makes the
limitation jurisdictional; (iii) the Sub-Registrar **has no jurisdiction** to levy
under s.46A at the counter or to condition registration on it.

**Q4 — Is registration mandatory?**
Not compelled merely because the instrument exists — but **advise registration**
where any clause declares rights over future units or creates collateral security
over inventory, since that is compulsorily registrable under s.17(1)(b), and
without registration the security is unenforceable *in rem* and inadmissible
under s.49. Also weigh: extended timelines, the supersession clause, lender and
RERA disclosure requirements, and succession risk in old landowner families.

## 5. Pitfalls

- **Do not accept "the assignment was registered unstamped" as a fact.** Pull the
  registration particulars. A registered instrument is presumed duly stamped by
  the act of registration (s.34).
- **Do not argue the refundable deposits are outside "money advanced".** The
  Explanation to Article 5(f) expressly includes refundable and adjustable
  security deposits. Argue Article 5(f) does not apply afresh at all.
- **Do not let a Sub-Registrar's oral query go unanswered in writing.** Put the
  position in a short letter citing the e-stamp certificate and the authorities,
  and copy it to the file.
- **Do not bundle a separately chargeable document** (e.g. a further addendum that
  transfers a specific constructed unit to the developer as compensation) with a
  nominal registration. That is a different and clearly chargeable event and needs
  its own analysis and its own duty.
- **Watch the date on a death certificate.** A certificate dated later than the
  death is the **issue/registration** date, not the date of death. For Ranka
  North Star the recital gives death on 02.08.2017 while the index certificate is
  dated 01.04.2019. Get this right before anything is asserted about succession.
- **Geo-blocked sources are not a dead end** — if the official levy table is
  unreachable, say so and use the nearest hard evidence (an e-stamp certificate on
  a comparable instrument) rather than inventing a rate.
