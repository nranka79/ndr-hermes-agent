# Counter-facing legal opinion — the SHORT format the authority will actually read

Verified 2026-09-18, Karnataka Stamp Act opinion for the Ranka North Star JDA addendum.
Trigger: NDR needs a legal opinion to **hand to a Sub-Registrar / government authority** —
not an internal working note.

## 1. Two versions exist — do not conflate them

| | Internal / research version | Counter version |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | NDR, his counsel | The Sub-Registrar |
| Length | 20+ pages is fine | **target ~6 A4 pages** |
| Depth | full reasoning, every authority, caveats, both sides | one paragraph of opinion per question |
| Tone | analytical, honest about the case against | cooperative, respectful, "may we request" |

**The length correction, verbatim (NDR):** *"that legal opinion can be shortened, it's too
big. We want to bring it down to the minimum. Basically, what's important so the sub-registrar
can read — questions, answers, case law. Very simple. Not applicable, not applicable, not
applicable. We don't want to get into too much detail right now."* The first build ran 22 A4
pages and was rejected. Rebuilt at 6.

Write the long version for the file; write the counter version to be **read at a counter in
two minutes**. Put the reasoning in the long one and only the conclusion + authority in the
short one.

## 2. Presentation

- Style it as a **professional law-firm opinion** — NDR named the firm himself ("DNR Law
  Firm"). Real letterhead, privileged-and-confidential banner, numbered paragraphs, firm name
  and date.
- Build in **HTML+CSS → PDF, A4 portrait with page breaks**, and deliver **two Drive links**:
  the PDF (to hand over) and the HTML source (to edit and re-render). Public-read,
  anyone-with-link.
- A **summary table up front** is the single most valuable element: `Question | Answer
  (bolded, one word) | Principal authority`. The reader gets the whole answer from the table.
- Then per question: **Question as asked → Our opinion (one short paragraph) → Statutory
  provisions → Authorities cited.** Then a table of authorities at the end.

## 3. The closing section — ask for adjudication, do not argue

When the instrument is genuinely arguable, close by **requesting referral rather than
pressing**. The wording that landed: if the authority still has any doubt, request that the
question be referred to the **Deputy Commissioner under Section 31** for adjudication rather
than being settled at the counter.

Frame it as **protecting the officer**, not as a threat:
- an adjudication determination carries a **penalty waiver under the proviso to s.31(1)(b)**
  when the duty is paid;
- it carries an **appeal route**, which a counter-level decision does not;
- it takes the individual officer's discretion out of the picture.

## 4. Ground it in the department's own record

The strongest paragraph in this matter was not case law — it was the **e-stamp certificate on
the instrument itself**, showing the State's own stamping system had classified the very same
document under the residual agreement article at a fixed duty, with consideration recorded as
nil. Always read the executed instrument's stamp certificate and cite the department's own
prior classification before citing anything else.

## 5. Non-negotiable caveats

State plainly, in the deliverable itself, that this is research-backed drafting formatted as a
firm opinion and **not signed by a licensed advocate** — counsel should review before it goes
to the authority. Also list the inputs you could not verify locally and asked the team to
confirm, rather than implying they were checked.
