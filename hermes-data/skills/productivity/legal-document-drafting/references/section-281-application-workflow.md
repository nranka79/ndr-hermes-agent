# Section 281 — Income Tax Approval for Land Contribution

## What It Is

Section 281 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 requires prior approval from the assessing officer before **any person whose income-tax proceedings are pending** can transfer immovable property. When a landowner/partner contributes land to a partnership firm (capital contribution), this counts as a "transfer" — so Section 281 clearance is needed.

## When This Comes Up

- A partner contributes land (Palya, Byadarahalli, etc.) as capital to the firm
- The firm's reconstitution/partnership deed is being registered
- The sub-registrar may require 281 clearance before registering the deed
- **Covers both:** individual landowners (Ashok Kumar) AND partnership firms (Satvik Developers)

## Application Structure

You need **separate applications** for each combination of:

| Variable | Options |
|----------|---------|
| **Applicant** | Each individual partner (Ashok Kumar), each firm (Satvik Developers), each co-owner (Nagendra) |
| **Land parcel** | Different survey numbers / Schedule references (Palya Sy 8/1 vs Byadarahalli Schedule B) |

So an Ashok Kumar with 2 land parcels → 2 applications (1 per parcel).
A Satvik Developers with 1 land parcel → 1 application.

## Contents of Each Application

### Applicant Details (MANDATORY)
- Full legal name
- PAN
- Aadhaar Number
- Residential address
- Jurisdictional Assessing Officer (AO) / Ward / Circle
- Phone number
- Email
- Last 3 years' ITR acknowledgment numbers (for reference)

### Land Details
- Survey numbers (exact from Schedule)
- Total extent (acres/guntas or sq meters)
- Village / Hobli / Taluk
- How the land is described in the firm's deed (Schedule reference)
- Current market value / consideration for contribution
- Source of acquisition (original purchase deed details if available)

### Purpose & Background
- That the land is being contributed as capital to [Firm Name]
- The firm's PAN and registration details
- That no tax evasion is intended (standard recital)
- That the transfer is for genuine business purposes

### Standard Recitals
- "The applicant has no intention to evade tax"
- "The transfer is in the ordinary course of business"
- "Proceeds will be used for business purposes of the firm"
- Request for expedited approval

## Drive Search Pattern — Finding Partner Details

When the user asks you to find partner details (PAN, Aadhaar) for Section 281 applications, the information is typically scattered across **multiple old deed documents** on Drive. Do NOT search by a single deed name.

### Search Strategy

1. **Identify all known entity/deed names** from the conversation:
   - "DRA Muthanallur Land Partners" (original deed)
   - "DRA Satwik Land Partners" / "Satvik Developers"
   - "DRA Federally Land Partners"
   - "DRA KAAJ Development Partners"
   - Any reconstitution deeds, contribution deeds, separation deeds

2. **Search Drive comprehensively** for each concept:
   - Search by each entity name + "deed" + "partnership"
   - Look for PDFs, Google Docs, scanned copies
   - Check multiple Drive folders (My Drive, Shared drives, TMP)

3. **What to extract from each deed:**
   - Partner names (full legal names)
   - PAN of each partner (look for "PAN:", "Permanent Account Number")
   - Aadhaar / Address
   - Firm registration number (if any)
   - Whether the firm is dissolved or active

4. **Cross-reference across deeds:**
   - A person's PAN appears the same across all deeds — use it to confirm identity
   - The same person may be listed under different name variants (Ashok Kumar = Ashok Kumar S)
   - Partnership deeds often include PAN as part of the party description

5. **If PAN is missing from all deeds:**
   - Check Gmail for any communication mentioning the PAN
   - Ask the user via WhatsApp to request the dissolution/separation deed from the lawyer (this often has the missing party's full details)
   - The dissolution deed is critical because it names ALL partners who were part of the firm

### Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Deed only lists partner names, not PAN | The PAN is typically in the party description block (long paragraph at the start of the deed). Read the full party description, not just the name line. |
| Partner listed as "[●]" placeholder | The deed was templated with unknown details. Find the actual signed/registered version or the dissolution deed. |
| Multiple deed versions exist (draft vs registered) | Check both — the registered version may have details the draft doesn't. |
| Old deeds use different firm names | Search by ALL known names — the user may have told you 3-4 variants. |

## Post-Draft Workflow

1. **Draft** each application as a Google Doc in the project folder
2. **Share** with the legal advisor/lawyer (editor, 7-day expiry)
3. **Get printouts** for the applicant to sign
4. **WhatsApp the lawyer** with:
   - Bold caption: "SECTION 281 APPLICATIONS — [Project Name]"
   - What's drafted, what's ready
   - What's missing (and what you need from them: dissolution deed, PAN, etc.)
   - Action items (print, sign, courier)
5. **Coordinate with lawyer** for hard copy delivery / e-filing
6. **Delete internal drafts** only after the user explicitly approves

## Understanding the Dissolution → Partition Chain

When preparing Section 281 applications, you'll frequently encounter deeds of dissolution and partition deeds for the same partnership. These are distinct legal instruments that serve different purposes:

### Dissolution Deed
- **Purpose:** Terminates the partnership firm as a legal entity
- **Effect:** The firm is wound up, accounts settled, the partnership ceases to exist
- **Covers:** The relationship between partners (discharge of mutual obligations)
- **Does NOT:** By itself, transfer title to specific immovable properties to individual partners
- **Trigger:** Section 63 of the Indian Partnership Act (notice to Registrar of Firms)

### Partition Deed (Partition Cum Settlement Deed)
- **Purpose:** Divides specific immovable properties among the erstwhile partners with legal finality
- **Effect:** Vests title to each land parcel in the individual partner
- **Covers:** Survey numbers, extents, boundaries — who gets which specific parcel
- **Does NOT:** Terminate the partnership (this is done by the dissolution deed)
- **Registration:** Typically registered under the Registration Act, 1908

### Key Timing Nuance
The Partition Deed often **predates** the Dissolution Deed:
1. First, partners agree on how to divide assets (Partition Deed)
2. Then, they formally dissolve the entity (Dissolution Deed)

This is because you need to know what each person gets before you can finally settle accounts and dissolve.

### Impact on Section 281 Applications
- The **Partition Deed** is what legally established each partner's individual ownership of specific land parcels
- Therefore, the Partition Deed is the authoritative source for:
  - Which properties belong to Ashok Kumar (Palya + Byadarahalli Part A)
  - Which properties belong to C.R. Nagendra (Byadarahalli, Schedule C)
  - Survey numbers, extents, boundaries for each parcel
- The **Dissolution Deed** is the authoritative source for:
  - Partner names and addresses
  - Profit-sharing ratios
  - Dates and registration details
- Both deeds together establish the complete chain of title for the Section 281 application

### Common Confusion Point
"You'd think once partition is done, the partnership just dissolves anyway" — but they're separate legal acts:
- **Partition** = dividing the pie (contractual/consent-based)
- **Dissolution** = shutting down the bakery (statutory, under Partnership Act)
- A dissolution can happen without a partition (if no immovable property to divide)
- A partition should always be followed by a dissolution (but may not be in practice)

## Reference: Example Structure

```
APPLICATION UNDER SECTION 281 OF THE INCOME TAX ACT, 1961

To,
The Assessing Officer,
[Ward/Circle],
Bangalore.

From:
[Full Name]
PAN: [PAN]
Aadhaar: [Aadhaar]
Address: [Address]

Subject: Application for prior approval under Section 281 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 for transfer of immovable property — [Land Description]

Sir/Madam,

I, [Name], son of [Father's Name], PAN [PAN], resident of [Address], do hereby submit this application as under:

1. I am a partner in [Firm Name], having PAN [Firm PAN], engaged in the business of real estate development.

2. I propose to contribute the following immovable properties belonging to me to the capital of the said partnership firm:

SCHEDULE OF PROPERTIES
[Table with survey numbers, extent, location]

3. The said properties were acquired by me vide [deed details / source of acquisition].

4. The contribution of the said properties to the partnership firm is for legitimate business purposes and there is no intention to evade tax.

5. The market value of the said properties is approximately Rs. [Amount].

6. I undertake to comply with all provisions of the Income Tax Act, 1961 in respect of the said transfer.

7. I request your honour to grant prior approval under Section 281 of the Income Tax Act, 1961 for the above transfer at the earliest.

Thanking you,

Yours faithfully,

[Signature]
[Full Name]
[PAN]
[Date]

Enclosures:
1. Copy of PAN Card
2. Copy of Aadhaar Card
3. Copy of Partnership Deed
4. Copy of title deeds / sale deed of properties
5. Latest ITR acknowledgment
6. Any other relevant documents
```
