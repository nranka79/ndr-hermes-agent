# Document Tone Transformation — Legal → Casual

## When to Use

User asks to rewrite a formal legal agreement into a casual, friendly letter. Trigger phrases: "make it less legal", "more casual", "friendly language", "doesn't look like a contract", "among friendly parties", "just a note to put on record".

## Transformation Rules

| Formal Legal Element | Casual Replacement |
|---|---|
| "Agreement" / "Deed" | "A Quick Note", "Record of Understanding", "Friendly Letter" |
| "the Shareholders / the Company / the Parties" | "all of us", "we", first names |
| Numbered clauses (1, 2, 3...) | Flowing paragraphs (no bullets/numbers) |
| "Whereas" / "Witnesseth" | "This note is simply to put down in writing what we've all discussed..." |
| "Applicable Law", "fiduciary duties" | "as required by law" (minimal) |
| "indemnify, defend, and hold harmless" | Plain language describing the commitment |
| Signature blocks | Keep as-is for execution |

## Intent to Preserve

- Who the parties are
- What they're agreeing to (cooperation on routine procedural matters)
- Duration / trigger for expiry
- Any witness or mediator role
- Company as confirming party

## Format

- **Title:** Centered, bold, ~14pt
- **Body:** 11pt Arial (cleaner than Bookman for letters)
- **Opening paragraph:** Italicized for warmth
- **Line spacing:** 1.15
- **Disclaimers:** None (no "Applicable Law" sections)
- **Signature blocks:** Retained

## Pitfalls

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|----------|-------|
| Remove substantive legal protections | Re-word them casually but preserve the commitment |
| Make it sound ambiguous | Keep intent as clear as the original |
| Add fluff / run long | Casual ≠ wordy — keep it concise |
| Call it an "Agreement" | Use "Letter" or "Note" |

## Real Example (DRA Aadithya, Jun 2026)

**Original (formal agreement):**
> The Shareholders wish to formalise certain cooperation commitments so that the Company may continue its ordinary course operations...

**Rewritten (casual letter):**
> This note is simply to put down in writing what we've all discussed and agreed orally, so there's no confusion on the basics while we work through the bigger picture.
