# RERA Recovery Notice and Tahsildar Powers Reference

This reference doc provides the legal framework, defense tactics, and statutory context for handling Tahsildar recovery notices issued under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964, specifically relating to RERA Court orders and joint developmental agreements.

---

## 1. Statutory Context: The Karnataka Land Revenue Act (KLRA), 1964
When RERA issues a recovery certification for arrears (e.g., delay compensation), it forwards it to the Deputy Commissioner / Tahsildar for execution under the **KLRA, 1964**.

* **Section 190 (Recovery of Arrears):** Land revenue arrears (or amounts deemed such, like RERA orders) are recovered via:
  1. Serving a written notice of demand on the defaulter.
  2. Attachment and sale of the **defaulter's** movable property.
  3. Attachment and sale of the **defaulter's** immovable property.
* **Tahsildar Jurisdiction:** The Tahsildar possesses the administrative authority to execute recovery. However, their powers are strictly limited to recovering from the **actual defaulter / principal debtor** (the promoter responsible for the default) or the specific estate attached to the liability.

---

## 2. Inherited Liabilities and Asset Devolvement

A critical point of protection arises when a notices targets the legal heirs of a deceased landowner (e.g., legal heirs of Late Dinesh Ranka).

### A. Section 50 of the Civil Procedure Code (CPC), 1908
Under Section 50, if a decree-holder seeks to execute a decree against the legal heirs of a deceased person:
* **The Rule of Limited Liability:** The legal representatives / heirs are **only liable to the extent of the property of the deceased** which has come into their hands and has not been duly disposed of.
* **Self-Acquired / settled Assets are Protected:** The personal or self-acquired assets of the heirs **cannot** be attached or auctioned. 
* **Prior Settlement Devolvement:** If assets were ancestral/jointly-held or devolved and legally partitioned/transferred prior to the attachment order, they are no longer part of the "deceased's unencumbered estate" under the direct recovery process.

## 3. Landowner vs. Developer Liability in JDAs

In Joint Development Agreements (JDAs), while both the landowner and developer are "Promoters" under RERA:
* **Construction & Delayed Delivery Defaults (RERA Sec 18):** Courts have clearly demarcated liability. The Supreme Court of India (*HCBS Association vs. Union of India*) and various State RERA appellate tribunals have determined that **delay compensation is the primary and sole liability of the executive builder/developer** who undertook the construction obligation.
* **Exhaustion of Developer Assets:** The Tahsildar must exhaust all recovery proceedings against the primary executive default party (e.g., KPDL, its active bank accounts, its project-share assets, and inventory) before arbitrarily targeting the landowner's separate estate.
* **Indemnity Enforcement:** Standard JDAs contain strict indemnity clauses where the developer indemnifies the landowner against all penalties, delay claims, fees, and litigation. This JDA indemnity serves as the foundation for seeking civil/writ court injunctions against the Tahsildar's recovery actions.

## 4. Immediate Defensive Action Plan for Landowners

When served a recovery notice from a Tahsildar's office, the following tactical steps must be executed within the standard 10-day window:

1. **Submit a Formal Written Objection to the Tahsildar:**
   * Detail that the primary default party under RERA is the developer (KPDL), and Case Case No. 6093 concerns delayed construction compensation.
   * Cite the JDA showing developer build obligations and standard developer indemnities.
   * Provide evidence of active developer assets in the project (unsold units, developer share) that must be liquidated first.
   * Inform the office of the landowner's deceased status and show that no assets remain under his unencumbered name.

2. **Invoke Developer JDA Indemnity / Demand Settlement Notice:**
   * Issue a legal demand notice to the developer calling upon them to satisfy the RERA decree directly into the Tahsildar's designated recovery bank account.
   * Hold the developer liable for any consequential losses or clouding of title caused by their default.

3. **File a Writ Petition in the Karnataka High Court:**
   * If the Tahsildar threatens coercive action, file a Writ Petition seeking an **interim stay** because the authority is acting *ultra vires* (beyond powers) by attempting to attach properties of legal heirs without due process or exhausting recovery against the principal debtor first.
