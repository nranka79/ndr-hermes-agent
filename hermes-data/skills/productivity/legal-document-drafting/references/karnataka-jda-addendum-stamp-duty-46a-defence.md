# Stamp Duty on JDA Addendum / Assignment — the Section 34 + 46A defence (Karnataka)

Source: NDR's email of 19 Sep 2026 (Ranka North Star thread). Verified against the general
Karnataka Stamp Act position. Use when **three Sub-Registrars tell the team "full stamp duty
may be payable"** on an addendum, because an upstream assignment was under-stamped.

## The problem as it presents

Local team reports: SROs say full duty is payable on the Addendum to the JDA, because no
stamp duty was paid on the **Assignment Agreement** which moved the development rights from
the original developer entity to the current one — and the SRO treats that inter-entity
transfer of rights as a fresh taxable transaction. Three SROs said this; the team prepared
to pay.

**Do not accept a counter-level demand on a point of law.** The department's own records
answer it.

## The four-step answer

1. **The assignments WERE e-stamped, at Rs. 200 under "Article 12 — Bond"**, with
   consideration recorded as **Rs. 0 (Zero)**. (Sites 1–8: Doc No. `5695`; Site 10: Doc No.
   `MLSD-04-03033-2013-14`.) The SRO's premise — that no duty was paid — is factually wrong.
2. **The department registered both, in March 2021.** Under **Section 34, Karnataka Stamp
   Act**, a public officer *may not register* an instrument unless it is duly stamped. The
   State cannot impugn its own act of registration five years later.
3. **Recovery is time-barred under Section 46A(1).** The five-year window from registration
   expired **15 March 2026**. The extended ten-year window opens **only** on fraud,
   collusion, wilful mis-statement or suppression of facts — none of which is alleged.
4. **The Addendum itself was properly stamped** at **Rs. 500 under "Article 5(J) — Agreement
   (in any other cases)"**, consideration recorded as zero. That matters: the department's
   *own stamping system* classified this exact document under the residual clause, **not** as
   an Article 5(f) development agreement. Its own classification defeats a re-characterisation
   argument.

**Authority:** *Smt. Rajeshwari v. State of Karnataka*, Karnataka High Court, 25 June 2026 —
the Section 46A limitation is **jurisdictional and not merely procedural**; a notice issued
beyond the outer limit is **void**.

## The remedy if an officer still presses

Do **not** resolve it at the counter. File a **Section 31 adjudication application** before
the Deputy Commissioner. This:
- removes the Sub-Registrar's discretion entirely, and
- carries a penalty waiver.

Lead with that as the fallback — it converts an argument with a counter clerk into a
determination by a competent authority.

## Where this sits in the GPA chain

The addendum **cannot be registered** until the fresh GPA is executed, attested, couriered
and adjudicated. So the stamp-duty fight and the GPA are **sequential, not parallel** —
resolving the duty point does not unlock registration on its own. Say this plainly when the
team proposes a date; in Sep 2026 the team proposed Monday and had to retract it.
