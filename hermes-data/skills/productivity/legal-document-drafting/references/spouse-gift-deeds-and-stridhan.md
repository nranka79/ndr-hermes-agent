# Gift deed to a spouse, "stridhan", and the land → firm → partner chain

Use when the ask is: husband gifts land to his wife, "stridhan", "gift deed to his
wife", "protect it from any tax claim", or when a landowner is being swapped out of
a partnership firm and his spouse put in his place. Also read
`references/tamil-nadu-stamp-duty-land-contribution.md` for the contribution leg.

## 1. HARD CORRECTION — there is no "Hindu Stridhan Act"

NDR's voice dictation sometimes invents this ("under some Hindu Sridhan act or
something"). **No such statute exists.** Stridhan is a concept under Hindu law
(property a woman receives by gift, at or after marriage, etc.), not an Act.

Never cite it in a deed, an email, or an instruction to a colleague. The correct
foundation, in this order:

- **Transfer of Property Act, 1882, ss. 122–126** — gift: voluntary, without
  consideration, by a living person; acceptance by the donee is essential;
  s. 123 requires a **registered instrument** for immovable property; s. 126
  governs revocation (a gift is irrevocable except on the narrow s. 126 grounds).
- **Hindu Succession Act, 1956, s. 14** — property acquired by a female Hindu is
  held by her as **full owner, not a limited owner**. This is what converts the
  gift into her absolute property and is the statutory hook that actually does
  the work people think "stridhan" does.
- HSA ss. 15–16 for succession if she dies intestate — worth a line in the note.
- Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, s. 27 — property presented at or about marriage
  (relevant only if a matrimonial dispute is in the background).

Correct the term once, in the instruction email, and move on. Do not embarrass
the colleague about it.

## 2. The recital narration NDR wants

He dictates a specific, human framing and it should be preserved almost verbatim
in substance. Structure:

1. At the time of the marriage, and repeatedly over the years that followed, the
   husband promised his wife that this land would be hers.
2. She has stood with him through the marriage, carried the household and
   contributed to everything the family has built; in his understanding **she has
   earned it**.
3. The gift is the **fulfilment of a promise made long ago**, now being executed.
4. Made **voluntarily, out of natural love and affection, for no consideration,
   and irrevocable**.
5. On execution the property becomes her **absolute stridhan**, free of any claim
   by him.
6. **She accepts the gift in the deed, in her own words of acceptance.** A gift
   without acceptance is defective — do not omit the donee's acceptance recital.

Purpose, and say this to the drafter explicitly: the deed itself must answer any
future question from an authority about *why* this transfer happened — a gift, to
a spouse, for no consideration, out of love and affection, in fulfilment of a
long-standing promise, accepted and registered.

## 3. Tax position — and the caveat that actually matters

- **s. 47(iii), Income-tax Act 1961** — a gift is not a "transfer" for capital
  gains. No capital gains to the husband.
- **s. 56(2)(x)** — a **spouse is a "relative"**, so the gift is not taxable as
  income in the wife's hands. (Relative is defined via s. 2(41).)
- **s. 64(1)(iv) — clubbing. THIS IS THE REAL EXPOSURE.** Income from an asset
  transferred to a spouse without adequate consideration is clubbed back in the
  **transferor's** hands. Carve-outs: the relationship did not subsist at the date
  of transfer or at the time the income accrues (i.e. an agreement to live apart).
- The hard question a drafter must research, not assume: **does the s. 64(1)(iv)
  exposure survive the wife contributing the land to a partnership firm, with the
  income then arriving as her share of firm profits?** There is a body of case law
  on clubbing following substituted assets. **Never give an unqualified "no tax"
  opinion on this point** — instruct the drafter to research it and flag it.
- If a house is ever the subject instead of land, note **s. 27** deems the
  transferor the owner of a house property transferred to a spouse without
  adequate consideration — that is the house-property analogue of clubbing.

## 4. Stamp duty and registration

- **Karnataka Stamp Act, 1957, Article 28 (Gift).** Verify the current rate, and
  specifically whether a **concession for a gift to a spouse / family member**
  applies. Do not quote a rate from memory — SRO practice and the current
  amendment schedule decide it.
- Registration fee, plus whether the **agricultural classification** changes the
  rate, and whether **DC conversion** is needed before or after.
- **Karnataka Land Reforms Act ss. 79A and 79B were omitted by the 2020
  amendment.** Confirm there is no surviving bar on a **non-agriculturist** holding
  the land — the wife may not be an agriculturist, and older checklists will still
  flag this as a blocker when it no longer is.
- Execution: e-stamp, both parties present at the SRO with jurisdiction over the
  property, two witnesses, photos, Aadhaar/PAN of both. Registered under
  Registration Act s. 17.

## 5. THE SEQUENCING TRAP — check before drafting a single line

Where the landowner is already inside a joint-development or partnership
structure, **look for an existing contribution deed before drafting the gift.**

If a contribution deed already transfers the same land to the firm and it has been
**executed and registered**, then the donor **no longer owns the land and cannot
gift it**. The gift route is dead as drafted and the earlier contribution must be
undone or superseded first.

Establish, in writing, before drafting:

- signed? stamped? **registered at which SRO, and what document number?**
- does the reconstitution / admission of partners ever get completed?

**Do not assume the contribution lapsed because the reconstitution was never
completed.** A registered contribution deed stands on its own. This single fact
decides whether the whole plan works — put it at the top of the instruction email
and ask for the answer the same day.

## 6. The full chain, in order

1. **Gift deed** — owner → spouse, registered at the SRO with jurisdiction.
2. **Mutation** — pahani/RTC and khata into her name; or, if the rules allow,
   contribute first and mutate straight to the firm (fewer steps, less cost —
   research which is permissible rather than defaulting to two mutations).
3. **Contribution deed** — spouse contributes the land to the firm as her capital
   contribution. **s. 45(3), Income-tax Act** — consideration is the amount
   recorded in the firm's books, so contributing at book value avoids an immediate
   gain. Stamp duty on a capital contribution of immovable property must be
   checked, and **matched to what was paid when the original owner contributed** —
   there is usually a cheaper route and no reason to pay more the second time.
4. **Reconstitution deed** — original owner retires, spouse admitted in his place.

Value the contribution the **same as the earlier one** (the Palya land was taken
in at ₹8,00,00,000 per acre in the June 2026 contribution deed). Changing the
valuation between the two contributions invites questions.

### Reconstitution vs a fresh firm — say which, and why
NDR sometimes asks for both in one breath ("create a new partnership firm with his
wife… replace her as the partner in DRA KAAJ"). They are alternatives, not steps.

**Prefer reconstitution.** A fresh firm orphans the existing asset pool and any
advances the outgoing partner already deployed into the firm — those become
unsecured claims against a dissolved entity instead of capital in a live one. When
the partner has money sitting in the firm, reconstitution is almost always right.
Flag the ambiguity back to NDR rather than silently picking one.

## 7. Data required before the deed can be completed

Ask for all of it in one go, not in dribbles:

- Donee's **full name as on Aadhaar**, age, father's name, Aadhaar, PAN, address.
- **Date and place of marriage, and the form of marriage** — the deed must recite
  a subsisting marriage (it underpins both the s. 56(2)(x) relative exemption and
  the s. 64(1)(iv) carve-out).
- Donor's **current** address for the deed. Cross-check every source: title
  reports, older contribution deeds and KYC records routinely carry different
  addresses. Ask which is current rather than picking the newest file.
- Whether any part of the land has been **sold, mortgaged, leased or otherwise
  dealt with** since the acquisition, and whether possession is with him.

## 8. Brief the drafting LLM with originals, not the summary

Standing rule for this class: the **title report is a summary — the sale deed and
the confirmation/rectification deed are the source**. Give the LLM the original
registered documents, and the schedule of property exactly as it appears in the
sale deed, with boundaries as rectified by the confirmation deed. Where a
confirmation deed exists it rectifies specific recitals — never quote the sale
deed schedule without checking what the confirmation deed changed.

Use the owner's **earlier contribution deed as the drafting template** for style,
recitals and schedule format, so the new documents read consistently with what is
already on record at the SRO and the Registrar of Firms.
