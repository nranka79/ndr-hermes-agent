# Financing-Clause Interpretation for Real-Estate MOUs (DRAAS)

From the Anvi Consultancy financing MOU review (Aug 2026). Use when the user
asks "what does this clause mean" on a financing/consultant MOU, or when
reviewing fee-protection and financing-structure clauses.

## Core concept: "term loan exit" for construction finance

In Indian real-estate lending there are two debt stages:

- **Construction finance (CF)**: short-term (typically 2–3 years), higher
  interest (~11–13%+), disbursed in tranches against construction progress.
  Must be repaid or *replaced* at maturity.
- **Term loan**: long-term (10–15 years), lower interest, amortising, taken
  against a completed / income-producing asset.

A **"term loan exit"** = when CF matures, a fresh long-term term loan pays off
("takes out") the construction finance instead of the developer repaying from
cash. Industry name: **takeout funding / refinancing**. Why a developer wants
it written into the consultant's scope:
1. CF lenders underwrite more easily when a defined exit (refinancing path)
   exists — better sanction odds and terms.
2. Avoids a balloon-payment trap at CF maturity when sales/rental cash flow
   may be thin.

Drafting critique of "wherever feasible, the Consultant shall also arrange a
term loan exit for the construction finance" (as it appeared):
- "wherever feasible" is undefined — who decides, and no consequence if not
  feasible.
- It sat inside the *project list* clause (2.2(e)) but is a *service
  obligation* — structurally misplaced; move to a standalone clause.
- No fee linkage — say explicitly that arranging the exit is covered by the
  main fee (no separate fee unless agreed in writing).
- Duplicates "Takeout Funding" listed for one project in the same MOU —
  harmonise: keep the project-specific mention and add one general clause.

## Other unclear clauses worth flagging in financing MOUs (checklist)

1. **"Minimum aggregate sanction"** vs "Indicative Terms" — a number like
   "minimum Rs 90 Cr" sitting under a heading that says "for reference only,
   not binding" is contradictory. Clarify: target vs commitment; also specify
   which projects make up the aggregate, and whether "available for immediate
   drawdown by Mr [Principal]" means the company or the individual (LAP
   portions are inherently personal).
2. **Back-out fee basis** — "fee calculated on the sanctioned amount" if the
   client signs a term sheet then withdraws without cause. 1% of the full
   sanction (e.g. 90 Cr → ₹90L) with zero disbursed is a huge exposure.
   Define "without cause" (exclude rejection of unacceptable terms) and
   consider capping to the lender-ready disbursement amount.
3. **Fee-protection clock** — a 30-day fee-protection period running "from the
   date of this MOU" vs an in-principle-sanction target "within N working days
   of complete data submission": if data is submitted late the clock may have
   already expired. Add an extension tied to late data submission.
4. **"MOTD"** — Memorandum of Title Deeds (the document creating an equitable
   mortgage by deposit of title deeds). Spell out once in legal docs.
5. **Arbitration deadlock** — "sole arbitrator mutually appointed" with no
   fallback stalls disputes if parties can't agree. Add: failing agreement in
   N days, either party may apply under Section 11, Arbitration and
   Conciliation Act 1996.
6. **Fee payable "on each disbursement"** vs exception on full sanction —
   reconcile the two bases explicitly.

## Fee-protection structure that worked (Anvi MOU, non-binding)

- MOU explicitly non-binding except Clause 5 (fee protection) which binds only
  on satisfaction of stated conditions.
- Fee due ONLY on: client accepts lender terms + signs term sheet/sanction
  letter/loan agreement, all CPs satisfied, lender actually disburses, and
  client actually receives funds.
- Exception (fee still payable without drawdown): client signed term sheet,
  DD cleared, lender ready to disburse, client backs out without cause.
- All other outcomes (lender declines, client rejects unacceptable terms,
  no acceptable financing within fee-protection period) = no fee.
- Consultant fee + bank processing/arrangement fee capped in aggregate (e.g.
  1% + 0.5% ≤ 1.5% of sanctioned amount + GST).
