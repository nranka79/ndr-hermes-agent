# Partnership Document — Collaborative Tone & Framing

**User:** Nishant (DRAAS)
**Context:** Documents shared with co-partners (Salman, Amir in TerraGreens) — risk analysis notes, transaction structure memos, governance proposals.

## Core Principle

Documents shared with partners must **identify hard risks without feeling like a power grab**. The reader should feel consulted, not dictated to. Every clause should invite discussion, not demand acceptance.

## Framing Rules

### 1. Ownership/Control Provisions → Frame as Structural Necessity

Never write "I/we need 51% control." Instead:

| ❌ Direct/Assertive | ✅ Structural Necessity |
|---|---|
| "DRA Realty must hold 51% and control the board" | "Given DRA Realty's IPO path and the ability to merge subsidiaries, a 51% holding with corresponding board majority is critical for the parent company structure. The profit-sharing ratio and investment split can be a separate discussion." |
| "I will have the right to take over" | "To protect the brand and project continuity, a takeover right at fair value should exist for scenarios where any act or omission materially affects the brand or project. Open to suggestions on triggers and valuation mechanism." |
| "I need 3:2 board seats" | "A 3:2 board composition naturally follows from the 51:49 ownership structure, ensuring decision-making alignment with capital at risk." |

### 2. Risk Statements → Frame as Shared Problem, Not Accusation

| ❌ Accusatory | ✅ Collaborative |
|---|---|
| "If a partner fails to bring in capital" | "Capital shortfalls can affect project timelines — how should we handle a scenario where one partner cannot meet a capital call?" |
| "Free-rider risk — a partner doesn't do their share" | "If effort contribution becomes uneven over time — which is natural in long partnerships — what mechanism should we have to address it?" |
| "Brand damage if partner misconduct occurs" | "The brand we're building together is an asset for all of us. What guardrails should we put in place so that no action by any partner harms that shared asset?" |

### 3. Use Suggestion + Invitation Pattern

Every proposal should follow this structure:

```
[Identify the risk/concern] → [Propose a mechanism] → [Invite alternatives]
```

**Template:**
> **Risk:** [One-sentence description of what could go wrong]
>
> **Suggestion:** One approach could be [proposed mechanism]. This would ensure [benefit] for all partners.
>
> **Discussion:** If anyone has a different view or an alternative mechanism that better addresses this, please suggest — the goal is to find what works best for all of us.

**Real example from TerraGreens D2:**
> **Capital Call Default.** If a partner cannot meet a capital call within the agreed timeframe — which can happen for legitimate reasons — the remaining partners may need to cover the shortfall. One approach could be that non-participating partners' stakes are diluted on a pro-rata basis at the aggregate NAV of the current project pool. Open to suggestions on the dilution formula or any alternative mechanism the team prefers.

### 4. Avoid "I" Claims — Use "We" / "The Partnership"

- ❌ "I am putting this document together to protect my interests"
- ✅ "This document is intended to help all of us think through scenarios before they arise — so we're never scrambling for an answer when things get difficult"

- ❌ "I need control over X because without it I can't merge with DRA"
- ✅ "For DRA Realty's ability to integrate this entity under its parent structure for a potential IPO, 51% holding is critical. This doesn't affect profit-sharing ratios — that's a separate discussion."

### 5. Preamble / Briefing Note Required

Every document shared with partners must open with a preamble that sets the collaborative expectation:

```
This note is a collaborative planning exercise. The goal is to anticipate what could 
go wrong between reasonable people who want to remain reasonable — so we have agreed 
mechanisms ready, rather than scrambling when things get difficult.

Some scenarios discussed here may feel unlikely or uncomfortable. That's by design — 
we're addressing them precisely because we value this partnership and want it to last.

Every risk identified is paired with a suggested approach, not a demand. If any item 
reads as targeting a specific partner, that's a drafting failure — please flag it and 
we will rephrase.
```

### 6. Specific Control Provisions — Framing Language

| Provision | Suggested Framing |
|---|---|
| **51% ownership** | "For DRA Realty's parent-company structure and potential IPO path, the ability to consolidate this entity at 51% is structurally necessary. The profit share and investment ratio can be mutually agreed separately." |
| **3:2 Board** | "A 3:2 board representation aligns with the 51:49 ownership structure. The smaller partner's 2 seats ensure their voice is heard on every decision, while 3 seats provide decision-making clarity." |
| **Takeover right at fair value** | "If any act or omission by a partner materially affects the brand, project timeline, or regulatory standing, the other partners should have the option to acquire their stake at a fair value determined by an independent valuer. This protects the partnership from being held hostage by a single partner's actions." |
| **Right to merge with parent** | "For a parent company planning an IPO, the ability to merge subsidiaries into the listed entity is standard and expected. The merged entity would continue with the same governance and profit-sharing framework unless otherwise agreed." |
| **Tag-along / Drag-along** | "Standard protections so that if a majority partner sells, minority can join on the same terms (tag-along), and if the majority receives a full-company offer, they can compel all partners to participate (drag-along)." |

### 7. Check Before Sending

Before delivering any document to partners, verify:

- [ ] Does every control provision read as "this is necessary for the structure" rather than "I want control"?
- [ ] Are there suggestions invited (explicit "open to suggestions" / "happy to discuss alternatives") after every substantive point?
- [ ] Is the preamble present and does it set a collaborative tone?
- [ ] Are all risks phrased as "what if" scenarios, not "you will" accusations?
- [ ] Is "I" minimized in favor of "we" / "the partnership" / "the entity"?
- [ ] Would a reasonable partner read this and feel consulted rather than dictated to?
