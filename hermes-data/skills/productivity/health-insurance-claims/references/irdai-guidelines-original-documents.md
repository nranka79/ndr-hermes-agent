# IRDAI Guidelines: Original Documents for Health Insurance Claims

**Researched:** 30 July 2026 | **Context:** Kanta Ranka Royal Sundaram Lifeline Elite claim via Medi Assist TPA  
**Sources:** IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (Ref: IRDAI/HLT/CIR/PRO/84/5/2024, 29 May 2024), IRDAI Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests (Ref: IRDAI/PP&GR/CIR/MISC/117/9/2024, 5 Sep 2024)

---

## Key Finding: Insurer/TPA Must Collect from Hospitals, Not the Insured

Per the **IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (May 2024)** and **Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests (Sep 2024)**:

> **"For claim settlement, the insurer and the third-party administrator must collect documents from hospitals and not from the insured."**
> — New Indian Express reporting on the 29 May 2024 circular; confirmed by Insurance Ombudsman rulings and IRDAI consumer-facing communications.

The circulars consolidate 55 prior circulars into one binding rulebook. The direction is clear: **the responsibility of document collection rests with the insurer/TPA**, not the policyholder.

## What This Means for Reimbursement Claims

| Common TPA Practice | IRDAI Position |
|---|---|
| Ask policyholder to submit original discharge summary, bills, reports | TPA should collect these **from the hospital** directly |
| Require originals via courier before processing claim | Scanned/copied documents should suffice for initial processing; originals only if flagged for verification |
| Insist on portal-based submission | Policyholder already submitted via email — portal duplication is unnecessary red tape |
| Keep originals after claim settlement | Originals must be **returned** to the policyholder after processing |

## Standard Industry Practice

Most TPAs (including Medi Assist) still ask for original documents as part of their process. The key distinction:

- **Scanned copies** are accepted for initial claim processing (via app, portal, or email)
- **Hard copies** (originals) are requested within 15-30 days of discharge — claims are "settled only upon receipt and validation of physical documents" (Medi Assist blog)
- However, the **IRDAI Master Circular shifts this burden** to the TPA to collect from hospitals

## Policyholder Rights Regarding Originals

1. **No IRDAI mandate** requires a policyholder to submit original medical records for reimbursement
2. Original medical records are **personal health data** — the patient is entitled to retain custody
3. **Notarized copies** carry legal evidentiary value and should be accepted as an alternative
4. If originals are submitted, the insurer/TPA must **return them** after claim settlement
5. The policy wording itself may specify "original documents" — this is the insurer's contractual term, not an IRDAI regulation

## Response Strategy for Escalation Emails

When drafting a response to a TPA demanding originals and portal submission:

1. **Cite IRDAI Master Circular on Health Insurance Business (May 2024):** TPAs must collect documents from hospitals, not insured
2. **Cite IRDAI PP&GR Master Circular (Sep 2024):** Document collection responsibility lies with insurer/TPA
3. **Offer notarized copies** as reasonable compromise — they carry legal evidentiary value
4. **Point out** that documents were already submitted via email — asking for portal re-submission is procedural duplication
5. **Reference the delay** — the TPA took 13+ days to respond despite a 24-48 hour auto-acknowledgment promise
