# Tamil Nadu Property Title Due Diligence — Key Differences from Karnataka

**Added:** 27 June 2026  
**Context:** Balaji Land (Relby Holdings) — Sevaganapalli Village, Hosur Taluk, Krishnagiri District  
**Why:** DRAAS is acquiring land in Hosur region for Ranka Oasis project. TN land laws, revenue records, and conveyancing practice differ significantly from Karnataka.

---

## 1. Land Measurement Units

Tamil Nadu uses a **Hectare-Are-Centiare** system in revenue records, with acre-conversion provided.

| TN Revenue Format | Meaning | Approx. Acres |
|------------------|---------|---------------|
| Hec.0.13.0 | 0 Ha, 13 Ares, 0 Centiares | 0.32 ac |
| Hec.0.50.0 | 0 Ha, 50 Ares, 0 Centiares | 1.235 ac |
| Hec.0.04.5 | 0 Ha, 4 Ares, 5 Centiares | 0.11 ac |

**Conversion:** 1 Are = 100 sq.m. 1 Centiare = 1 sq.m. 1 Acre = 4046.86 sq.m.

The advocate's opinion gives BOTH Hec and Ac values — always cross-verify. Our session found Sy.76/1-76/2 = Hec.0.08.0 total = 0.20 acres (NOT 2.0 acres — verify carefully when the user expects larger extents).

**Pitfall:** "Ac.0.20 cents" means 0.20 acres (one-fifth), NOT 2 acres. Tamil Nadu expresses fractions in cents (1 acre = 100 cents). So "Ac.0.20" = 20 cents = 0.20 acre.

## 2. Key Revenue Documents — Tamil Nadu

| Document | TN Name | Karnataka Equivalent | What It Shows |
|----------|---------|---------------------|---------------|
| Record of Rights | **Chitta** (computer generated) | RTC | Current pattadar, survey no, extent, classification |
| Crop / Cultivation Register | **Adangal** | — | Actual cultivation, personal occupation |
| Old Revenue Register | **Old "A" Register** | Old RTC / Record of Rights | Pre-UDR ownership history |
| UDR Register | **UDR "A" Register** | — | Post-UDR survey ownership (1980s-90s) |
| Survey Map | **FMB (Field Measurement Book) Sketch** | Tippan / Survey Sketch | Topography, boundaries, sub-divisions |
| Tax Receipt | **Adangal + Tax Paid Receipt** | Property Tax Receipt | Tax payment history |
| Tenure Certificate | **VAO Certificate** (no AD-condition) | — | Confirms land is free of restricted tenure |

**AD-Condition Certificates:** VAO certifies land is NOT Agraharam, Devadaya, Inam, Temple Manyam, or other restricted tenure land. This is **essential** — without it, title may be unmarketable.

## 3. Title Chain Analysis — Common Patterns

### Oral Partitions
TN legal opinions frequently rely on **oral family partitions** from the 1970s-80s as the root of title. Unlike Karnataka (where registered partition deeds are standard), oral partitions are accepted in TN conveyancing practice when:
- Followed by registered sale deeds that confirm the division
- Supported by revenue records (UDR patta in the name of the oral allottee)
- Continuous possession and tax payment by the allottee

**Risk:** Oral partitions can be challenged. Mitigate by ensuring the chain of registered deeds (from the 1970s/80s onward) and revenue entries continuously support the claimed division.

### The Ratification / Rectification Deed Pattern — Two Variants

**Variant 1: Ratification Deed (Curing Non-Joinder of Heirs)**
When a sale deed inadvertently omits some legal heirs (daughters, distant LRs), a **Ratification Deed** is executed later — the omitted heirs sign a registered document ratifying/confirming the original sale.
- Omitted heirs received some consideration
- Ratification confirms they have no further claim
- **If no Ratification Deed exists:** The omitted heirs can potentially challenge the sale

**Variant 2: Rectification Deed (Curing Survey Number Typos)**
When a sale deed has a typographical error in the survey number (e.g., Sy.151 instead of Sy.161 — one-digit typo), a registered **Rectification Deed** corrects the error. This is a known pattern in Hosur region where a single digit mistake in a multi-page deed document is later fixed.
- The rectification deed references the original sale deed number
- It only changes the specific erroneous entry (page/paragraph reference)
- Without it, the original sale deed's description would not match the actual property
- **Always flag if no rectification deed exists for an obvious typo** — the buyer's registration may be rejected if the prior deed has mismatched survey numbers

### Unregistered Wills
TN legal opinions sometimes cite unregistered Wills as part of the title chain. Under Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, an unregistered Will is valid but evidentiary value can be challenged.

**Mitigation:** If beneficiaries of the unregistered Will subsequently participated in a registered sale deed as sellers (receiving consideration), the defect is substantially cured — they have effectively ratified by accepting payment.

## 4. Encumbrance Certificates (EC)

TN ECs are obtained from the **Sub-Registrar's Office (SRO)** of the applicable registration district.

| Coverage | What to Look For |
|----------|-----------------|
| **30-year minimum** | Standard for TN property transactions |
| **System generated + Old register** | System EC covers computerised period; old EC covers pre-computerisation |
| **Period gaps** | Ensure no gap between old EC and system EC. Our session: Doc 2 had 4 ECs covering 1960-2026 (66 years) with overlapping dates — ideal |
| **What appears** | Registered sale deeds, mortgages, gift deeds, liens, court orders |

**The Same-Day EC (Critical Closing Step):**
On the morning of registration, obtain a **same-day EC** for ALL survey numbers via the SRO online portal or physical visit. TN is a high-risk jurisdiction for last-minute fraudulent registrations (a seller could register a sale/mortgage hours before your purchase). If the EC is clean, proceed. If a new entry appears, STOP registration and investigate.

**Pitfall:** EC only shows REGISTERED encumbrances. Equitable mortgages (deposit of title deeds) may not appear. Verify physical possession of original title documents.

## 5. Legal Opinion Structure (TN Advocate Style)

TN legal opinions from advocates follow a consistent format:

1. **Description of Properties** — Survey no, extent, boundaries (N/E/S/W)
2. **List of Documents** — Table with date, document description, registration number
3. **Name & Address of Present Owner**
4. **Brief Summary of Title** — Narrative chain from root to present
5. **Revenue Records** — Patta, Chitta, Adangal, FMB, VAO cert analysis
6. **Encumbrance Certificate** — Periods reviewed and findings
7. **Opinion** — Three-paragraph standard:
   - (a) Good, clear, marketable, absolute title
   - (b) EC shows no subsisting encumbrance
   - (c) Owner is legally entitled to sell

**Key difference from Karnataka opinions:** TN opinions are typically shorter and more formulaic. They rely heavily on revenue records for title verification (vs Karnataka's emphasis on tracing from a registered root deed). This is because TN settlement records often predate registered deeds.

## 6. Missing Document Risk — Partnership Deed

When the seller is a **Partnership Firm** (not an individual), the legal opinion MUST review the **Partnership Deed** to confirm:
- The firm exists and is validly constituted
- The partners signing the sale deed have authority to sell firm property
- The firm is not dissolved or under liquidation

**If NOT reviewed** (as in our session with Relby Holdings): This is a HIGH priority gap. Without it, the sale deed could be challenged for want of authority. Obtain and review before closing.

## 7. Zoning & Land Use — Hosur/Krishnagiri

Sevaganapalli falls under **Hosur Local Planning Area** within the Chennai-Bangalore Industrial Corridor.

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|-----------|
| **Planning Authority** | DTCP Krishnagiri (Directorate of Town & Country Planning) |
| **Land Classification** | "Dry" (Punjai) — non-irrigated agricultural |
| **Conversion Required** | Section 49, TN Land Reforms Act for non-agricultural use |
| **Layout Approval** | DTCP Krishnagiri + Village Panchayat |
| **Restrictions** | VAO cert confirms no temple/manyam/AD-condition land |

**VAO certificate is critical** — without it, land could be subject to temple trust claims or inam tenure restrictions.

## 8. Pitfalls Specific to Hosur Region

1. **Border ambiguity:** Hosur is on TN-Karnataka border. Some documents may reference Karnataka laws (e.g., Section 109 KLRA). Verify jurisdiction — TN land follows TN laws, not Karnataka's KLRA.

2. **Multiple survey systems:** Settlement survey → UDR survey → sub-division can create numbering confusion. Our session had a rectification deed correcting Sy.151→161 (one digit error). Always cross-check survey numbers across all documents.

3. **Minor's property:** When minors appear as co-owners (through inheritance), their share must be properly represented. Natural guardian (father) can sell minor's property for legal necessity. Natural guardian representation is standard in TN practice but a court-appointed guardian is stronger. Flag as residual risk.

4. **Daughters' omission:** Pre-2005 Hindu Succession Act amendments, daughters were sometimes omitted from family settlements. If a Ratification Deed includes daughters who were originally omitted, this strengthens the title. If daughters are still missing, flag as a risk.

5. **Extent mismatch in multi-parcel acquisitions:** When buying multiple parcels from the same vendor, verify that the TOTAL extent in the legal opinions matches the TOTAL extent in the agreement/termsheet. If the termsheet says 7 acres but opinions cover only 2.575ac (as in our session), the remaining parcels need separate legal opinions.

6. **DTCP "Deshu Book" (Survey-Number-Wise Land Use Register):** The TN Town & Country Planning Act mandates a survey-number-wise land use classification maintained by the DTCP (referred to locally as the "Deshu Book" or Detailed Development Plan register). This is DIFFERENT from the VAO's revenue classification ("Dry" vs "Wet") — the DTCP classification determines what DEVELOPMENT is allowed (Residential, Commercial, Agricultural, Industrial, or reserved for public purpose). Always verify this BEFORE purchase. If the DTCP map shows Agricultural but you intend Residential, you need conversion approval under Section 9 KLRA. If it shows a reserved purpose (road widening, park, school), development is blocked regardless of clean title. **This is the #1 developability check in TN.**

## 9. Batch Legal Opinion Analysis — Multi-Survey Number Framework

When the seller provides **multiple legal opinions for different survey numbers** from the same advocate (common when Relby Holdings or similar entity consolidates parcels), use this framework:

### 9.1 Document Inventory

Create a summary table:

| # | Opinion Date | Survey Nos | Extent (Ac) | Seller→Buyer | Lineage | EC Coverage | Patta Nos |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 26.06.2026 | 161/5A-5D | 2.375 | Doc 15/2013 + 11759/2014 | Munisamy Reddy | 55 yrs | 1137, 1136 etc. |
| 2 | 26.06.2026 | 76/1-2 | 0.200 | Doc 52/2013 + 9893/2014 | Narayanappa | 66 yrs | 1044 |

### 9.2 Lineage Identification

Group opinions by family lineage — this reveals which title chains are independent vs. interconnected. In our Balaji Land session, 4 opinions split into exactly **2 lineages** (Munisamy Reddy covered Sy.161/5A-5D + 164/2A-2E; Narayanappa covered Sy.76/1-2 + 161/6). Each lineage has its own oral partition, inheritance chain, and risk profile.

**⚠ CRITICAL PITFALL — Treat Each Opinion's ROOT Independently:**
When grouping by "lineage," the **intermediate vendor family** (who sold to the current owner) is NOT necessarily the **original source family**. Two opinions can share the same vendor names but trace back to completely different original owners. In our session:
- Sy.161/5A-5D: **Original source = Biddappa & Badigappa (Kamma Muniyappa lineage)** — the Munisamy Reddys were PURCHASERS
- Sy.164/2A-2E: **Original source = Munisamy Reddy (ancestral)** — directly inherited

Both opinions listed "Munisamy Reddy" as the lineage (because the Munisamy Reddys were the vendors to Relby Holdings), but the ROOT source families are different. **Always trace: "Who originally owned this land before the current vendor's family?" — not just "Which family sold to Relby?"**

**How to catch this:** Look at the earliest deed in each opinion's chain. If Doc 1's earliest deed is a PURCHASE by the family from a different family (1979 Biddappa→Gopala Reddy), while Doc 3's earliest deed is ancestral (Munisamy Reddy's father), they have different roots even though both involve Munisamy Reddys as vendors.

### 9.3 Genealogical Tree Construction

For each lineage, trace every individual named in ALL opinions for that lineage:

- **Root ancestor** → list all children
- **Marital status**: wife named? Joint sale with wife?
- **Deceased persons**: confirm death + legal heirs named
- **Minors**: who acted as natural guardian? Court-appointed or parent?
- **Released/reversionary rights**: was there a release deed or consent deed?
- **Omitted family members**: daughters, distant LRs — are they included as vendors in later deeds?

**Format (monospace, tree structure):**
```
MUNISAMY REDDY (died, ancestral)
 ├── Nanjunda Reddy (died)
 │    └── Shanthammal @ Santhamma (wife)
 │         └── Ramachandra Reddy
 │              ├── Pavan Kumar (minor)
 │              └── Madhupriya (minor)
 ├── Krishna Reddy
 │    └── Sidhartha Reddy
 └── ...
```

**When there are two independent source families (like Biddappa/Badigappa + Munisamy Reddy), create SEPARATE trees.** Do NOT merge them even if the same vendor names appear in both chains. In the interconnected note, clarify that the same persons appear as purchasers in one chain AND as ancestral heirs in the other.

### 9.4 Consolidated Title Flow Timeline

Merge all opinions into one chronological timeline (earliest→latest) showing how each parcel moved from root ownership to the current seller. This reveals:

- Whether parcels from different lineages converge at the same seller (Relby Holdings)
- Whether the seller acquired different parcels at different times
- Whether any registered deeds cover multiple survey numbers simultaneously (consolidating purchases)

**When a sub-chain has its own independent original source, show it as a SEPARATE timeline (Sub-Chain A, Sub-Chain B) under the same section header with a clear structural note explaining the interconnection.**

### 9.5 Boundary Cross-Reference

Extract N/E/S/W boundaries from each opinion and compare them:

- Opinion 1 says "East: Land in Sy.161"
- Opinion 3 says "West: Land in Sy.164"
- Do the boundaries match? If survey A's East boundary is Sy.B and survey B's West boundary is Sy.A, they are **adjacent**.

Create a contiguity map from the boundary data. This is NOT a substitute for an actual FMB superimposition — it is a desk check before the survey.

### 9.6 Total Extent Reconciliation

**Always calculate the total extent and compare against the expected total.** In our session, the IRR spreadsheet referenced ~7 acres but legal opinions only covered 4.59 acres — a 2.41 acre gap that needed clarification with the seller.

### 9.7 Opinion Consistency Check

Compare all opinions for:
- Same advocate name and enrollment number?
- Same date? (Batch legal opinions should ideally be contemporaneous)
- Same document list structure?
- Any opinion that uses weaker language or has more caveats?
- Any opinion where EC coverage is shorter than others? (Flag the shorter one)

## 11. FMB Superimposition & Contiguity Verification

This is the most commonly **overlooked** aspect in TN legal opinions — advocates opine on each survey number independently but do NOT verify contiguity.

### Why It Matters
For a residential layout, the land must be a **single contiguous block**. Fragmented parcels with third-party land in between cannot be developed as one layout.

### Desk Check (Before Survey)
Collect N/E/S/W boundaries from each legal opinion and cross-reference:

| Opinion | Survey | East | West | North | South |
|---------|--------|------|------|------|-------|
| Doc 1 | 161/5D | ... | 161/6 & 164 | ... | ... |
| Doc 4 | 161/6 | 77 & 76/1 | 161/5D | ... | ... |

If boundary descriptions consistently reference each other (e.g., Sy.161/5D West = Sy.161/6, and Sy.161/6 West = Sy.161/5D), contiguity is **plausible**. If not, flag it.

### Required Survey Actions
1. **Engage government-licensed surveyor** — obtain FMB sketches for ALL survey numbers
2. **Composite overlay** — superimpose all FMBs onto a single map
3. **Ground boundary fixation under landowner supervision** — The landowner (Balaji Babu or Relby Holdings rep) MUST be present on site to point out all boundary markers. The surveyor physically fixes boundaries using: (i) existing stone markers / pillars, (ii) GPS coordinates of each corner, (iii) measurement from nearest permanent landmark (road junction, canal bridge). Record in a **Boundary Fixation Report** signed by: surveyor, landowner, and neighbour representatives. Any boundary dispute discovered here must be resolved BEFORE purchase — otherwise the buyer inherits the dispute. This is essential because TN legal opinions describe boundaries from revenue records, not from ground-truth.
4. **Neighbour boundary acknowledgment** — The surveyor should call neighbouring landowners (as listed in the opinions' boundary descriptions) to acknowledge boundaries in writing. This prevents future boundary litigation over encroachment claims.
5. **Physical ground measurement (total station)** — Compare measured extent vs FMB extent vs title extent from opinions. A ±5% difference is normal in TN; anything beyond needs explanation. You buy what exists on GROUND, not what the 1950s A-Register says.
6. **Certificate** — surveyor certifies total extent, contiguity, road access, and boundary resolution

### Physical Possession Documentation
Before closing, document the site with:
- Panoramic photographs from all 4 corners covering the full extent
- Photographs of boundary markers with neighbouring Sy.Nos visible
- Photographs of any existing structures/wells/trees/crops
- Advocate's Physical Possession Certificate (proves vacant possession on a specific date — essential for adverse possession defence)

### Road Frontage Specific
- **Identify which survey number(s) abut the public road** — if none, the land may be landlocked
- **Road width** — determines development regulations (setback, access width)
- **Road authority** — Panchayat road (≤20ft) vs PWD road vs NH — determines who can issue access permissions
- **If no direct frontage** — need access easement from neighbour or purchase of a road-fronting strip

## 12. PWD/WRD Bridge & Canal Crossing Permission

When a property is separated from the main project by a **canal** (common in TN with irrigation canals), development viability depends on obtaining PWD permission for a bridge/crossing.

### TN Regulatory Context
- **Authority:** PWD — Water Resources Department (WRD), District-level office
- **Typical canal buffer:** 3 metres (approx. 10 feet) from canal bank — no permanent structures inside
- **Bridge required:** Connects two parts of the development across the canal

### PWD Application Process
1. **Informal meeting** with Executive Engineer, PWD/WRD (district office)
2. **Formal application** addressed to EE, PWD/WRD containing:
   - Survey sketch showing canal location + proposed bridge position
   - Bridge design / structural drawing (minimal — slab culvert for small bridges)
   - NOC from Assistant Engineer of concerned canal section
   - Land ownership documents (legal opinions)
   - Undertaking to restore canal bank to original condition post-construction
3. **Technical review** by PWD engineers
4. **Written permission / license** issued (may include conditions)

### Feasibility Within 3m Buffer
| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Bridge type | Precast RCC slab culvert — minimal foundation, piers at buffer edges |
| Span | ~10ft (3m) — within buffer, no permanent structure in buffer beyond piers |
| Risk | If PWD refuses any structure within buffer→need cantilevered/suspended bridge (₹15-25 L) |

### Indicative Cost Estimate
| Item | Cost (₹) |
|------|----------|
| Design & Structural Engineering | 50,000 - 75,000 |
| PWD Application & Licensing Fees | 25,000 - 50,000 |
| RCC Slab Bridge (10ft span, single lane) | 3,00,000 - 5,00,000 |
| Approach Roads (both sides) | 1,50,000 - 2,50,000 |
| Contingency | 50,000 - 1,00,000 |
| **Total** | **5,75,000 - 9,75,000** |

### Timeline
- PWD processing: 4-6 weeks minimum
- Start early — do NOT close the land purchase before PWD feasibility confirmation

## 13. The 3-Tier Risk Classification System

When presenting due diligence findings, classify risks as:

| Tier | Label | Description | Examples |
|------|-------|-------------|----------|
| **🔴 Tier 1** | **BLOCKER** | Must close before purchase — if unresolved, do not proceed | Partnership Deed missing, contiguity unverified, road frontage absent, PWD bridge refused |
| **🟡 Tier 2** | **HIGH RISK** | Resolve before closing — material but mitigable | Unregistered Will, oral partition gaps, only photostats (no certified copies), court search not done |
| **🔵 Tier 3** | **DEVELOPMENT STAGE** | Post-acquisition, pre-construction | DTCP zoning, conversion approval, environmental clearance, TDS compliance, tax arrears clearance |

## 14. Action Execution Checklist Format (5-Column)

| # | Action Item | Detailed Description & How to Perform | Delivery / Outcome | What We Achieve |
|---|-------------|---------------------------------------|-------------------|-----------------|

The last column tags each item with the pillars it serves: **T** (Title), **P** (Possession), **I** (Physical Integrity), **D** (Developability).

Group items by:
- **🔴 SECTION A — BLOCKERS** (Complete before any purchase commitment)
- **🟡 SECTION B — TITLE & OWNERSHIP VERIFICATION**

- **🟢 SECTION F — ENTITY & SELLER VERIFICATION**
- **🟢 SECTION G — FINAL CLOSING DOCUMENTS**

Each row's "Detailed Description" should read like a SOP — step-by-step instructions actionable by a DRAAS team member or external advocate, not just a label. Include: which government office to visit, what documents to submit, what fees to expect, and what to check for.

The "Delivery/Outcome" column lists the exact document or result (e.g., "Certified A-Register extracts for all 10 sub-divisions").

The "What We Achieve" column states how this serves the four pillars (T/P/I/D). This is what the user's team needs to understand WHY each action matters.
