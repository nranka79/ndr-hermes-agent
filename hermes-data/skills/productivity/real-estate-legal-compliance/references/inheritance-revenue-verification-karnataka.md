# Inheritance & Revenue Record Verification — Karnataka Agricultural Land

## When to Apply

When the title of the vendor/seller in a due diligence is derived through **inheritance** (IHR/Hakku Varasat Mutation) rather than a registered sale deed, the legal opinion cannot stop at the inheritance order alone. The entire revenue chain before and after the inheritance entry must be verified.

This is critical for agricultural land in Karnataka, particularly in high-value areas like Devanahalli, Whitefield, and Bengaluru Rural district, where land values command ₹7+ Cr for even small extents (2-3 acres).

## Why Inheritance Is Not Root of Title

- Inheritance (IHR/Hakku Varasat) is a **mode of transmission** upon death
- It is NOT a root deed of acquisition
- A 30-year search alone is insufficient when title traces through inheritance
- The root deed of the deceased owner (or earliest revenue record) must be traced

## Minimum Revenue Documents to Verify

| # | Document | Purpose | Period |
|---|----------|---------|--------|
| 1 | **Inheritance Order (IHR No.)** | Certified copy of Tahsildar/Revenue Authority order. Verify legal heirs and extent inherited. | Year of order |
| 2 | **Mutation Register Extract (MR)** | Entry based on IHR. Check if mutation was challenged or appealed. Critical for tracing ownership changes. | From IHR entry to present |
| 3 | **RTC/Pahani Before Mutation** | Confirm deceased owner's name and possession prior to inheritance | At least 5 years before IHR (e.g., 1987-88 to 1992-93) |
| 4 | **RTC/Pahani After Mutation** | Verify continuous possession and revenue entries. RTC is the primary revenue record. | IHR year to current year |
| 5 | **Mutation History** | Complete chain from earliest available record. Ensure no cancelled or disputed mutations. | Earliest to present |
| 6 | **Khata Extract / Record of Rights** | Confirm revenue recognition of ownership | Current |
| 7 | **Death Certificate** | Of the person from whom inheritance was claimed | Date of death |
| 8 | **Family Tree / Genealogy** | Certified or notarized family tree tracing ALL legal heirs | — |
| 9 | **Revenue Tax Paid Receipts** | Confirm tax payment continuity | Preferably from IHR year onwards |
| 10 | **Village Map / Tippani / Survey Records** | Verify survey number continuity and extent | — |

## Period of Verification for Final Legal Opinion

| Document | Safe Period to Check |
|----------|---------------------|
| Registered title documents | Minimum 30 years |
| RTC/Pahani | From inheritance year till current year |
| Mutation records | Complete chain from earliest available record |
| Encumbrance Certificate | 30 years till current date |
| Court cases | Current status and past litigation search |

## Legal Opinion Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Parent Title Deed Before IHR IS Available
Check title from the document by which the deceased owner acquired the property and continue up to present.

**Recommended wording:**
> "I have verified the title deeds and revenue records from the parent title deed of the deceased owner up to the present transaction and found a continuous chain of title."

### Scenario 2 — Parent Title Deed Is NOT Available
A 30-year search is insufficient because inheritance is only a mode of transmission, not the root of title. Verify:
- IHR order (certified copy)
- Death certificate
- Certified/notarized family tree
- Mutation Register extract relating to IHR
- RTC/Pahani from at least 5 years BEFORE the IHR year
- Earliest available RTC showing the deceased owner's name
- MR records prior to IHR

**Recommended wording (qualified):**
> "Title is traced through revenue records and inheritance proceedings from IHR No. [X] onwards. The parent title deed of the deceased owner is not available for review."

### Preferred Approach for High-Value Agricultural Land
For agricultural lands in Devanahalli and similar high-value areas, prefer to trace title from the **original grant or earliest registered root deed** rather than merely stating a "30-year" or "40-year" search period.

**Strongest wording:**
> "Title traced from original grant / root deed / earliest available revenue record."

## IHR Number Pattern in the Report

When the Confirmation Deed or Sale Deed recitals mention "IHR No. XX/YYYY-YY", this must appear in:

1. **Executive Summary** — stated as the source of the vendors' title
2. **Chain of Title** — as a row in the chronological table (the transmission event)
3. **Inheritance Verification section** — as the primary document needing certified copy
4. **Red Flags** — flagged as not provided if certified copy is absent
5. **Conclusion — Pending Requirements** — listed as item requiring certified copy

## Common Pitfalls

- **Confirmation Deed revealing incomplete title search:** The Confirmation Deed recital often states that LRs were not joined because "the purchaser was not aware of their whereabouts." This is an implicit admission that the title search at the original sale was incomplete. Always flag this as residual risk.
- **Multiple vendors (15+):** Large co-owner groups from inheritance increase risk of undiscovered LRs. Always recommend a certified family tree.
- **Elderly vendors (70+):** Capacity concern with substantial consideration amounts. Note in red flags.
- **Confirmation Deed registration fee:** If the fee is nominal (~₹500-₹1,000), it confirms the deed is a ratification, not a fresh transfer. Cross-check against the main Sale Deed fees.
