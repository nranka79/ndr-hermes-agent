# BMTF Notice Defense Strategy — Developer Response Framework

## Overview

Recurring problem for DRAAS projects: Bangalore Metropolitan Task Force (BMTF) issues notices to developers alleging encroachment on government kharab land, buffer zone violations, or bylaw violations — then demands appearance for "inquiry" at BMTF office. These are widely documented as extortion attempts (see Bangalore Mirror 2022: "activists allege BMTF has become another police station where cops are eager to mediate illegally in civil disputes").

## BMTF Formation & Legal Basis

- **Constituted:** Government Order No. UDD 247 MNU 95 dated 19.03.1996, Urban Development Department, Karnataka
- **Original mandate:** Protect government property (BBMP, BDA, BWSSB lands)
- **Police powers:** Conferred separately by Home Department in 1996
- **Critical limitation:** The Karnataka High Court noted the State government **did NOT extend BMTF powers after 18.03.2013** — after that date, BMTF officials had "no authority to arrest and detain" (The Hindu, Sep 2018, NICEL compensation case)

## Three Independent Defects in a Typical BMTF Notice

Before citing precedents, check every BMTF notice for these 3 fatal defects — each is independently sufficient to disregard the notice:

**Defect 1 — No FIR/Crime Number**
Per the **Karnataka HC judgment (July 30, 2024)** in WP on BNSS Section 35:
> *"It is mandatory for a notice under Section 35 BNSS to mention the crime number, the offence alleged, and append a copy of the FIR. Without these, the noticee is not obliged to appear before the officer and no coercive action can be taken for non-appearance."*

**Defect 2 — No sections of law cited**
If the notice says "complaint is under inquiry" but cites zero penal sections, it is vague and unenforceable. BMTF can only act under specific Acts (not IPC).

**Defect 3 — No copy of the complaint/FIR attached**
The 2024 Karnataka HC judgment expressly requires a copy of the FIR to be appended. Without it, the notice is invalid.

## Key Karnataka High Court Precedents

### 0. May 2026 — Revenue Authorities Have NO Jurisdiction Within BBMP/GBA Limits
- **Division Bench of Karnataka HC (Justices DK Singh and TM Nadaf, May 2026)**
- **Held:** *"Revenue authorities have lost their jurisdiction to take any action... under the provisions of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act on land within BBMP/GBA limits — even if the land retains agricultural classification in revenue records."*
- **Application:** A property with a BBMP E-Khatha/PID is squarely within BBMP limits. The entire premise of "encroachment on government kharab land" under the KLR Act cannot even be investigated by revenue authorities, let alone the BMTF.

### 1. C Geetha v. State by BMTF Police (CRL.P 4384/2013)
- **Held:** FIR registered by BMTF is vitiated — "the FIR having been registered by BMTF, the entire proceedings are vitiated and are liable to be quashed"
- **Application:** Quash FIR at registration stage itself on ground of BMTF's lack of jurisdiction

### 2. Brigade Enterprises Ltd v. BMTF (2015, Justice A.N. Venugopala Gowda)
- **Held:** "BMTF has no jurisdiction to investigate the case under the IPC"
- BMTF can investigate only offences under the Karnataka Municipalities Act (KMC Act)
- BMTF **cannot** investigate IPC offences (sections 119, 409, 420, etc.)
- **"Prosecution for an offence under Section 192-A of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act cannot be maintained in the absence of show-cause notice to the accused"**
- "BMTF must first issue a notice before initiating action under the Karnataka Land Revenue Act"
- **Source:** Bangalore Mirror, High Court tells BMTF it can't file a criminal case; Indian Kanoon doc 161693282

### 3. SS Topgi v. State of Karnataka (2019, Karnataka HC)
- **Held:** BMTF had no jurisdiction either to register the case or to investigate
- **Source:** Indian Kanoon doc 101637273

### 4. Compensation for Wrongful Detention (The Hindu, 29 Sep 2018)
- HC held BMTF **"has no power of general police station"** to take cognizance of IPC offences
- Directed State to pay **Rs 1 lakh compensation** to each of two persons for wrongful detention by BMTF
- State did NOT extend BMTF powers after 18.03.2013
- Quashed cases against NICEL executives including Ashok Kheny

### 5. Sri Sri Ravi Shankar v. BMTF (Mar 2026, Justice M. Nagaprasanna)
- **Most recent and directly applicable precedent**
- HC **quashed** BMTF FIR under Section 192-A KLR Act for land encroachment
- Finding: no prima facie evidence against the accused
- **Source:** OpIndia, Deccan Herald, 25 Mar 2026

### 6. Pavankumar B v. State of Karnataka (WP 26162/2013, Karnataka HC)
- BMTF confined to offences under KMC Act or allied Acts
- If IPC offences are the MAIN allegations with KMC Act used as "innovative method to lug complaint into jurisdiction" — entire FIR is liable to be quashed

## Section 192-A KLR Act — Procedural Requirement

Section 192-A creates the offence of "unlawfully entering or occupying government land."

**BUT the HC has specifically ruled [Brigade case, Srinivas Raju v. State of Karnataka (2023)]:**
- A **mandatory show-cause notice** must be issued first
- The notice must call upon the alleged encroacher to file objections within **15 days**
- Only after considering objections can any action be taken
- Without this notice, prosecution under Section 192-A is void

**Key point:** A preliminary "inquiry call" notice (like the one from PSI Manoj Kurane) is NOT a show-cause notice under Section 192-A. The mandatory procedure has not been triggered.

## What Happens If You Don't Appear

**BMTF's next steps after skipping the inquiry:**
1. Send a second/final notice with warning
2. Refer the complaint to the jurisdictional CMM (Chief Metropolitan Magistrate) seeking direction for FIR registration
3. If FIR is registered — it is **liable to be quashed** per existing HC judgments

**There is NO arrest power, NO automatic FIR registration for skipping a preliminary inquiry call.** They are fishing.

## Phased Defense Strategy

### Phase 1: Challenge Jurisdiction Immediately (Before Hearing Date)

Send written reply by RPAD (speed post with acknowledgement due):

Subject: Reply to Notice No. [BMTF Ref No.]

Body should state:
- The company is the lawful owner of the property with valid BBMP e-Khatha/PID
- RTC records confirm NO government kharab land is involved
- BBMP building plan approval has been obtained
- BMTF lacks jurisdiction to investigate this matter per Karnataka HC judgments in C Geetha, Brigade Enterprises, and SS Topgi
- BMTF powers were NOT extended after 18.03.2013 as noted by the HC
- No show-cause notice under Section 192-A KLR Act has been issued
- The complaint is a civil dispute over property title, not a criminal matter
- The company is not appearing for inquiry until BMTF demonstrates valid legal authority

### Phase 2: If FIR is Registered

File a **Criminal Petition under Section 482 CrPC / Article 226 before Karnataka High Court** immediately to quash the FIR on ground of **lack of jurisdiction**.

Cite these precedents in order:
1. C Geetha (2013) — proceedings vitiated if registered by BMTF
2. Brigade Enterprises — BMTF has no IPC jurisdiction
3. SS Topgi — no jurisdiction to register or investigate
4. Sri Sri Ravi Shankar (Mar 2026) — FIR quashed for lack of evidence
5. The 18.03.2013 expiry of BMTF powers

This gets a stay automatically — the HC routinely stays proceedings when jurisdiction is challenged.

### Phase 3: Exploit Section 192-A Procedural Requirements

If BMTF tries Section 192-A KLR Act:
- Demand the **mandatory show-cause notice** with 15-day objection period
- File detailed objections
- Challenge show-cause notice before Deputy Commissioner / Karnataka High Court on grounds:
  - The land is NOT government kharab land (RTC proves this)
  - It is an Ikata property within BBMP limits with valid PID
  - No buffer zone violation exists — no nala, no stormwater drain in vicinity
  - The complaint is actuated by malafides/extortion motive

### Phase 4: Counter-Attack Options

File complaints with:
- **ADGP, BMTF** against the concerned PSI for exceeding jurisdiction
- **Karnataka Lokayukta** if there's evidence of extortion demand
- **Karnataka High Court PIL division** — expose BMTF's pattern of extortion in civil disputes

### Phase 5: Starve Them — Repeated Documentation Requests

When they send repeated notices, respond each time asking for:
- Copy of Government Order extending BMTF powers beyond 18.03.2013
- Copy of the complaint / petition reference
- Show-cause notice under Section 192-A KLR Act (as mandated by HC)
- Proof of jurisdiction under KMC Act (the only Act where HC has said BMTF can act)

Every reply must be sent by **RPAD with acknowledgement due** — creates evidence trail.

## Why BMTF Notices Are Transparent Extortion

1. **No kharab land** — RTC confirms this. HC has quashed BMTF FIRs exactly on this ground
2. **BBMP Ikata property** — valid e-Khatha/PID means BBMP has regularised the property. BMTF cannot sit in appeal over BBMP's own regularisation
3. **No buffer zone trigger** — no nala, stormwater drain, or lake nearby. The buffer zone allegation is a standard template
4. **BMTF's documented pattern** — Bangalore Mirror (2022): "Activists allege BMTF has become another police station where cops are eager to mediate illegally in civil disputes... working as mediators between land developers and land owners"
5. **HC finding** (2018): Forced Rs 1 lakh compensation from State for wrongful BMTF detention
