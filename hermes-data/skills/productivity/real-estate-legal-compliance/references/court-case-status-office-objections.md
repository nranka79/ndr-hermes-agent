# Court Case Status — Terminology & Interpretation

## Overview

When tracking a pending writ petition (WP) or other case in a High Court, the eCourts portal shows cryptic status entries. This reference decodes the most common ones for Karnataka High Court and explains the procedural flow.

## Common Status Entries Decoded

| Status Entry | Meaning | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **NON-COMPLIANCE OF OFFICE-OBJNS** | The court registry has flagged procedural defects in the filed paperwork (court fees, copy count, pagination, annexures, service proof). The registry office — not any party — raised these. | The **petitioner's counsel** must fix them before the case can be heard on merits. |
| **FOR 1ST/2ND/3RD/4TH TIME** | How many times the case has been listed for this same issue without resolution. "4TH TIME" means it's been adjourned 3+ times already while objections stay uncleared. | Court patience wears thin after 3–4 listings. |
| **NON-COMPLIANCE OF OFFICE-OBJNS FOR 4TH TIME** | The most common stuck state — listed 4+ times, objections still pending. | Risk of peremptory warning → dismissal. |
| **ADJOURNED** | Case called, no effective hearing — either party sought time or court couldn't take it up. | No progress. Repeated adjournments signal procedural stalling. |
| **PEREMPTORY** | A final warning from the court: "Last chance — comply or case dismissed." | Dismissal risk at next non-compliance. |
| **NOTICE** | Court issues notice to respondents to appear and file responses. | Normal progression — case is alive and moving. |
| **DISPOSED** | Case is finally decided or dismissed. | Check disposal nature: "Allowed" vs "Dismissed" vs "Withdrawn". |

## How Cases Get Stuck: The Office Objections Loop

```
Petition filed → Registry examines paperwork
                     ↓
          Objections found ❌
                     ↓
          Case listed as "NON-COMPLIANCE OF OFFICE-OBJNS"
                     ↓
          Adjourned (parties seek time to fix)
                     ↓
          Listed again → still not fixed
                     ↓
          Court grants 2-3 more chances
                     ↓
          Peremptory warning issued
                     ↓
          Next non-compliance → DISMISSED
```

## Karnataka High Court Precedents on Dismissal for Non-Compliance

### 1. K. Hemamalini vs State of Karnataka (14 Oct 2025)

> *"Despite having been granted sufficient time, learned counsel for petitioner failed to comply with the office objections. This non-compliance by the petitioner would indicate that the petitioners have no genuine intent to diligently pursue the matter. Accordingly, writ petition stands dismissed for non-compliance of office objections."*

**Lesson:** Once the court determines non-compliance is chronic, dismissal is swift — no further warnings.

### 2. BARC vs Y.N. Hanumanthegowda (MFA/7830/2019, decided 30 Aug 2021)

> *"The court held that when office objections are not remedied despite repeated extensions and warnings by the court, dismissal of the appeal is justified."*

**Lesson:** Even a government entity (BARC) got dismissed. Status as a government/PSU party does NOT immunise against dismissal for non-compliance.

**Binding on:** All subordinate courts in Karnataka.

### 3. Goverdhan B N vs Authorized Officer (30 Jan 2024)

The court passed a peremptory order:

> *"Finally, two weeks time is granted to comply with the office objections, failing which, the petition would be dismissed for its non-prosecution."*

**Lesson:** The peremptory warning is explicit — "last chance, failing which dismissed." After this, one more adjournment without compliance = dismissal.

## Who Is Responsible for Office Objections?

**Always the Petitioner(s)** — the party who filed the case. Their counsel must cure every registry-raised defect and file a compliance memo.

Respondents (Union of India, State Govt, Bagmane Texworth, etc.) have no role in curing office objections. If the case stays stuck on "NON-COMPLIANCE OF OFFICE-OBJNS", it's the petitioner's counsel dragging their feet — not the respondents delaying.

## When Does the Court Usually Dismiss?

There's **no fixed count** of adjournments. The pattern is:

1. **First 2-3 listings** — routine extensions, no pushback
2. **Next 1-2 listings** — court starts noting it's been repeatedly listed
3. **Peremptory warning issued** — written order saying "final opportunity"
4. **One more non-compliance after warning** → dismissal

**In PILs:** Courts are more lenient — they prefer not to dismiss public interest matters on technicalities. But leniency has limits.

**Key factor:** Whether the court has issued a **peremptory warning** yet. If not, dismissal is unlikely next hearing. If yes, next non-compliance = high risk.

## eCourts Portal — Manual Query Steps

When the portal requires CAPTCHA (always), provide the user with the exact URL and step-by-step.

### For Karnataka High Court (Principal Bench, Bengaluru)

**URL:** https://hcservices.ecourts.gov.in/ecourtindiaHC/index_highcourt.php?state_cd=3&dist_cd=1&stateNm=Karnataka

**Steps:**
1. Click "Case Status" → "Case Number"
2. Select Case Type: **WP - Writ Petition(144)**
3. Enter Case Number: `38186`
4. Enter Year: `2025`
5. Type the CAPTCHA shown
6. Click GO

### For District Courts (Karnataka) via eCourts

**URL:** https://services.ecourts.gov.in/ecourtindia_v6/?p=casestatus/index&app_token=

**Steps:**
1. Select State: **Karnataka**
2. Select District → Select Court Complex
3. Switch tab to "Case Number" or "Party Name"
4. Fill details + CAPTCHA
5. Click GO

## Hearing History — What to Look For

When reading a case history table (as shown on the eCourts portal), interpret these patterns:

| Pattern | Signal | Example |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Same bench, consistent adjournments | Routine progress | CJ + Poonacha adjourning 3 times |
| Bench changes mid-stream | May reset the clock — new judge unaware of prior warnings | CJ + Poonacha → CJ + Hemalekha |
| Multiple "NON-COMPLIANCE" listings | Petitioner not fixing paperwork | Same issue across 4 dates |
| "Counter filed" / "Objections filed" by respondents | Respondents cooperating, case moving on merits | R3 counter on 05/03 |
| Long gaps with no entry | Portal not updated OR case dormant | Check cause list |
