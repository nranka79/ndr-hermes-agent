# Buying a DRC / TDR certificate (Karnataka — BDA / Greater Bengaluru Authority)

A DRC purchase is **not** a land purchase. You are buying a FAR-credit
certificate; the land that generated it has already been acquired by the
authority. Title to the *certificate* is not the same question as title to the
*land*, and the certificate can be recalled if the award behind it was
double-drawn. Review both, and review the certificate's own validity separately
from the landowner's chain of title.

Trigger: broker offers "GBA TDR / DRC worth ₹X", sends a title report on a
"Development Right Certificate", asks for a view or a price.

## 1. Establish the four identities before reading anything else

Every DRC document carries these; they must all agree across the title report,
the certificate, the acquisition file and the broker's email:

1. DRC number + date + ledger folio (e.g. `GBA/TDR Cell/DRC/<no>/<fy>`)
2. Survey number / sub-division + village + hobli + taluk of the **originating plot**
3. Surrendered extent, and the **FAR credit issued** (usually 2× the surrendered area at 2.0 FAR — check the arithmetic)
4. The landowner names on the award

If the broker's document does not carry all four, you do not yet know what is
being sold.

## 2. Unit arithmetic is a real defect detector

```
1 acre  = 40 guntas = 4,046.86 sqm
1 gunta = 101.17 sqm
```

Worked catch from a live file: a lawyer's title report said the DRC originated
from **20.05 guntas** while also stating **2,074.01 sqm**. 2,074.01 / 101.17 =
20.50 guntas — so "20.05" was a transposition in the report, not a land defect.
The acquisition file and the Form IV application both said **20½ guntas**, and
the DRC credit was exactly 2 × 2,074.01 = 4,148.02 sqm. Do this arithmetic on
every extent you are given, in every direction, before you write an opinion.
Report it as a typo in the report when it is one — and as a defect when the
documents genuinely disagree.

## 3. The double-draw check — the single highest-value question

Under the BDA/GBA PRR scheme the landowner can take **either** a monetary
consent award **or** TDR. If cash was drawn *and* the DRC was issued for the
same land, the authority can recall the DRC — that risk attaches to the asset,
not just to title.

Evidence to assemble:

- The **Form IV "Option for Development Rights Certificate by the Applicant"** (sub-rule (4) of rule 3) signed by the owners — this is the election.
- The **consent award bill** and the **payment voucher** in the acquisition file.
- A written statement from the SLAO / GBA TDR Cell on whether the award was drawn.

Reading a payment voucher correctly: an unsigned "Signature of the Payee" box
and a blank "Cheque no." means the voucher was **raised but not executed**.
That is consistent with the TDR route having been taken — but it is *not*
proof of non-payment. Ask for the payee-signed voucher, the cheque/RTGS
reference, or a non-drawal certificate. Never present an unsigned voucher as
"the cash was not taken".

## 4. Where a DRC file actually lives

The certificate alone is never enough. Ask for / expect a bundle that includes:

| Document | What it proves |
|---|---|
| DRC certificate face + GBA ledger folio extract | The instrument exists, its credit, validity, utilisation endorsements |
| Final acquisition notification (e.g. `UDD/399/MNX/2006 dt 29.06.2007`) | The acquisition was lawful and covers the plot |
| Preliminary + final notification, mahazar (spot inspection) | Possession was taken; malkies/assets recorded |
| Award order (DC-LA / Special LA Officer) | Compensation fixed; conditions imposed before possession |
| Consent award bill + payment voucher | Whether cash was taken (see §3) |
| Form IV TDR option + application | The owners elected TDR, not cash |
| TDR Cell note for the financial year | Internal sanction chain, area accepted (sqm) |
| Chain deeds, RTC/pahani, EC, village map / survey sketch | The landowner actually owned what was acquired |
| BDA acquisition/TDR register tables + abstract signed by Commissioner | Cross-check the entry exists in the register |

A bundle named like a certificate is frequently the **acquisition case file**,
not the certificate. Verify by content. Also expect such a bundle to contain
*other* landowners' objections, wills and GPAs for the same village — read
around them, don't attribute them to your vendor.

## 5. Assessment of a vendor-side title report

Broker-supplied title reports are usually commissioned by the seller. Check, in
this order:

- **Addressee and reliance clause.** If it is addressed to a third party and
  carries "use of this report ... by any other person is not authorized ... no
  liability is assumed", the buyer has **no right of reliance**. You need a
  fresh report in the buyer's name, or a reliance/addressee letter on the
  advocate's letterhead with enrolment number.
- **Signed and sealed?** Unsigned with no enrolment number is not an opinion.
- **Root of title.** Old grants (e.g. a 1941 darkhast grant, `D4.GP.79/1940-41`)
  carry conditions — non-alienation, class restriction, occupancy. If the grant
  order / Saguvali Chit / Grant Issue Register are "not available", the root is
  unproven, however clean the later chain looks.
- **Inheritance transmissions.** A transmission justified only by sale-deed
  recitals plus "nobody has claimed in 55 years" is a presumption, not proof.
  Demand death certificates, family trees, and affidavits from all Class I
  heirs for every intestate death in the chain.
- **EC continuity.** Check the search period year by year against the deeds
  listed in the chain; a 76-year EC that omits three of the deeds in the chain
  is not a clean EC. Duplicate overlapping ECs in the annexure that the body
  never discusses are a drafting defect worth naming.
- **RTC continuity.** List the missing years explicitly; an RTC series starting
  decades after the root grant leaves the gap un-evidenced.
- **Litigation.** A fraudulent registered sale deed cured by a compromise
  decree is a decent cure (admission, not ex-parte) — but a consent decree
  binds only the parties to it. It does not expunge the deed from the
  sub-registrar's register, does not bind third parties who dealt with the
  fraudsters, and needs the FIR/criminal case status alongside it.
- **The certificate itself is usually not opined on at all** — validity period,
  utilisation, transferability/endorsement mechanics, e-Khata, conditions of
  use. Get a written position from the issuing cell.
- **No public notice / no court or tribunal search** on a property that has
  already carried one forged deed is the wrong way round.

## 6. Commercial sanity check

DRCs trade **below** guideline value because they can only be set off against
premium FAR and carry utilisation charges. A headline discount against the
stated plot value is not a discount until benchmarked against:

- the going Bengaluru DRC rate per sqm of FAR, and
- what the buyer would pay the authority directly for the same premium FAR.

Also confirm the maximum percentage of FAR that DRC can cover **at the specific
project site** — it is site/zone dependent, not a general rule.

## 7. Standard requisition list

1. Certified 1941/root grant order, Saguvali Chit, Grant Issue Register extract.
2. Death certificates + family trees + Class I heir affidavits for every intestate transmission.
3. Continuous EC from both sub-registrars for the full period, showing the fraudulent deed and the decree.
4. RTCs for every missing year in the series.
5. FIR / criminal case status on the forged deed; sub-registrar's note of the decree.
6. Original title deeds for inspection.
7. Award copy + proof of how compensation was settled (cash vs TDR).
8. DRC certificate face, GBA ledger extract, utilisation status, validity.
9. Written confirmation from the GBA TDR Cell on transfer to the buyer entity and usability limits.
10. Public notice.
11. Fresh title report in the buyer's name, or reliance letter from the vendor's advocate.

## 8. Reporting to the user

Nishant wants: a one-line verdict first ("structurally competent, substantively
not bankable"), then the numbered things that matter, then the single point to
resolve first, then the requisition list. Do not bury the reliance-clause or
double-draw findings in prose — they are the two that change the price.

## 9. Filing

File the certificate and every bundle received with it to the buying entity's
proposal folder (BuxRanka → BRDPL → Proposal by <broker>), named
`YYYYMMDD_<Entity>_DRC <no>_Sy <no>-<sub> <Village>_<Doc type>_<pp>pp.pdf`.
Verify the upload by MD5 before telling the user it is filed.
