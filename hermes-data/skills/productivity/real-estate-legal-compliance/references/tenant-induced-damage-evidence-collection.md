# Tenant-Induced Appliance/Equipment Damage — Evidence Collection & Cost Recovery

## Trigger

A tenanted property (especially a corporate/diplomatic lease like British High Commission, consulates, MNCs) has experienced premature equipment or appliance failure — and the tenant's repeated requests for modified building settings (higher water pressure, altered electrical load, etc.) are the suspected root cause. You need to:

1. Collect documented evidence proving the causation chain
2. Use that evidence to negotiate cost sharing with the tenant
3. Put the claim on record formally

## The Causation Chain to Prove

```
Default Building Setting
  → Tenant Requests Modification (date-stamped, written)
  → Building Team Approves & Adjusts Setting
  → New Setting Exceeds Manufacturer-Recommended Range for Equipment
  → Equipment Fails Prematurely (repeat failures = stronger proof)
```

Each link in this chain needs **documented evidence**.

## Evidence Collection Checklist

### 1. Default / Standard Building Setting

- **What is the building's standard setting?** (e.g., water pressure in PSI/bar, voltage range, load capacity)
- **Source:** Building maintenance team, property manager, original building specifications
- **Documentation needed:** Written confirmation from building maintenance, photos of regulator/gauge settings, building spec sheets

### 2. Current Actual Reading

- **What is the actual current reading at the specific floor/unit?**
- **Source:** On-site measurement by building engineer
- **Documentation needed:** Photo of pressure gauge/gauge reading at the unit's supply point, signed note from engineer

### 3. Every Tenant Request for Modification

- **For EACH request:** Date, who made it (name/email), who they spoke to, what was specifically requested, what was done in response
- **Source:** Email trails, maintenance logs, work orders, WhatsApp messages
- **Documentation needed:** Forwarded emails, maintenance log extracts, work order copies, WhatsApp screenshots

### 4. Work Orders / Maintenance Logs

- Any work orders raised for pressure adjustments, pump settings, or regulator changes on that floor
- **Source:** Building maintenance team, Prestige/association management
- **Documentation needed:** Signed/dated work orders, email approvals

### 5. Manufacturer Specs for Equipment

- **For each affected appliance:** What is the manufacturer's recommended operating range for the setting that was modified?
  - Water pressure: Most dishwashers/washing machines specify 0.3–6 bar (check specific model)
  - Voltage: Most appliances specify 220-240V ±10%
- **Source:** Product manuals, specification sheets from manufacturer website, or contact manufacturer support
- **Documentation needed:** Screenshots from spec sheets, email from manufacturer support confirming recommended range

### 6. Photos & Visual Evidence

- Photos of the failed equipment showing the condition
- Photos of pressure gauges or regulator settings
- Photos of manufacturer label/spec sticker on the failed equipment
- Photos of the repair in progress (technician working on it)

## Building the Argument

### The Paper Trail Narrative

```
[Default Setting] → [Date: Tenant Request #1] → [Date: Adjustment Made] 
→ [Date: Tenant Request #2] → [Date: Further Adjustment] 
→ [Setting now = X, which exceeds manufacturer spec of Y] 
→ [Date: First equipment failure] → [Date: Repair attempt] 
→ [Date: Repeat failure] → [Siemens/etc technician declares "beyond economical repair"]
```

### The Cost-Sharing Request

**Logic:** The tenant's repeated, documented requests for a non-standard building setting directly contributed to — and in the case where the setting exceeded manufacturer specs, caused — the premature failure of equipment. It is therefore reasonable for the tenant to bear a proportional share of the replacement cost.

**Suggested ask:** 50% of replacement/repair cost, with DRA absorbing the other 50% as good-faith gesture.

**Supporting argument:** DRA would not have increased the setting beyond standard/default without the tenant's explicit requests. The equipment would have operated normally at standard settings (like other units in the building).

### Putting It on Record

1. **Evidence compilation** — Collect all evidence into a structured folder on Drive
2. **Internal message to engineer** — Brief the site/property engineer on what evidence to collect and why
3. **Formal letter/email to tenant** — State the claim with supporting evidence, propose cost-sharing, request confirmation
4. **File for records** — Even if the tenant disputes, the documented record protects DRA's position for future claims or exit inspection

## Pitfalls

- **Don't assume the tenant will agree** — Your goal is to put the documented claim on record. If they dispute, you still have the evidence for exit inspection / security deposit negotiation.
- **Written requests are gold** — Email trails where the tenant requested pressure increases are the strongest evidence. Verbal requests are harder to prove.
- **Manufacturer specs matter** — If the adjusted setting is *within* manufacturer specs, the causation argument weakens. Always check the spec sheet first.
- **Multiple appliance failures strengthen the case** — If it's just one dishwasher, the tenant can argue normal wear. Showing the washing machine and geyser are ALSO affected proves it's a systemic issue (pressure), not random equipment failure.
- **Siemens/Bosch/etc service reports are independent third-party evidence** — The technician's written declaration ("beyond economical repair") is harder for the tenant to dispute than DRA's own assessment.
