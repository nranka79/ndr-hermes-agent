# Consent-Decree Title Verification — 6-Tier Decision Tree

For properties where the current owner's title rests on consent decrees / compromise decrees (rather than a registered sale deed from the previous title holder), standard EC-and-sale-deed verification is insufficient. The consent decree may be vulnerable to challenge if:

- The party that gave up its claim lacked authority (e.g., trustees without court approval)
- The decree was based on false pleadings or collusion
- No valid consideration was paid
- All necessary parties were not joined

## Trigger

Use this workflow when the title analysis reveals that the current owner's possession or ownership derives from a **consent decree / compromise petition** — especially when the previous title holder was a **Trust**, a **minor**, a **government body**, or a **company in liquidation**.

## Reference: Session-Specific Example

This pattern was developed during the **Cunningham Crescent Road (No. 9) title analysis** (June 2026). In that case, Smt. Narasamma's title rested on consent decrees from the H.C. Dinshaw Trust (1986) — not a registered sale deed. The Trust's authority to compromise, the validity of trustee succession (3 original trustees died 1958-1966), and the consideration (if any) paid were all unverified. The 6-tier checklist below was designed for that property but is generalised here.

## The 6-Tier Decision Tree

```
TIER 1: TRUST / ENTITY COMPROMISE VALIDITY
  ├─ If FAILS → ⛔ TITLE FAILS — no need to proceed
  └─ If PASSES → TIER 2
TIER 2: TITLE DOCUMENTS
  ├─ If FAILS → ⛔ Chain broken
  └─ If PASSES → TIER 3
TIER 3: COURT RECORDS
  ├─ Gaps → Legal opinion needed
  └─ All verified → TIER 4
TIER 4: SURVEY & REVENUE RECORDS
  ├─ Government claim found → ⚠️ Strategy needed
  └─ Clean → TIER 5
TIER 5: OCCUPATION CLAIMS
  ├─ False claims found → Credibility risk
  └─ Verified → TIER 6
TIER 6: CROSS-VERIFICATION
  └─ Clean → ✅ MARKETABLE TITLE
```

## Detailed Checklist

### TIER 1 — Gatekeepers (highest importance, easiest to verify)

**1.1 Verify the Compromise/Consent Decree**
- [ ] Obtain certified copy of the compromise petition filed in court
- [ ] Identify ALL persons who signed on behalf of the entity giving up its claim
- [ ] Verify each signatory was a VALID trustee/director/office-bearer at the date of signing
  - Source: Trust Deed / Board Resolutions / Minutes of Meeting
  - If trustees died before the compromise: was the trust properly reconstituted?
- [ ] Verify the signatories had authority to alienate or compromise property
  - Check Trust Deed / MoA / Partnership Deed for permitting provisions
  - If not explicit: Was court approval obtained (e.g., Section 34 of Indian Trusts Act, 1882)?
- [ ] Verify CONSIDERATION: Was any money paid for the compromise?
  - If zero consideration: the compromise is vulnerable as a gift of trust property
  - Cross-check entity's accounting/tax records for receipt of consideration
- [ ] Check: Was the compromise recorded as a formal court decree or merely a memo?
  - Source: Certified copy of court order/decree from the trial court

**1.2 Verify the Entity's Title to the Property**
- [ ] Obtain certified copies of all registered documents in the title chain
- [ ] Verify the entity's founding document (Trust Deed, MoA, Partnership Deed) validly covers this property
- [ ] Cross-check property schedule descriptions across all documents
- [ ] Verify succession of trustees/directors/partners from entity creation to compromise date
  - Every gap in succession is a potential challenge point

**1.3 Verify Subsequent Consent Decrees (if multiple)**
- [ ] Obtain certified copies of all compromise decrees
- [ ] Verify all necessary parties joined
- [ ] Verify court had jurisdiction over subject matter
- [ ] Check for minor parties requiring guardian ad litem

### TIER 2 — Title Documents
- [ ] Search for ANY document showing how the occupant originally came into possession
  - Tenancy? Employment? License? Adverse possession?
- [ ] Verify khatha transfer orders (were they interim or final?)
- [ ] Verify continuous property tax payments (demonstrates government recognition)
- [ ] Verify any subsequent sale deeds (was stamp duty properly paid? Any undervaluation dispute?)

### TIER 3 — Court Records
- [ ] Obtain certified plaint and written statement from the original suit
- [ ] Verify each decree: on merits? on consent? on withdrawal?
- [ ] Check for any pending appeals or cross-appeals
- [ ] For BBMP/government claims: verify if orders were on merits or jurisdictional grounds

### TIER 4 — Survey & Revenue Records
- [ ] Match survey numbers across all records (BBMP, City Survey, Revenue)
- [ ] Check for any government classification (lake, tank, forest, government land)
- [ ] Verify distance from any claimed water body (government's own maps may disprove their claim)

### TIER 5 — Occupation Claims Verification
- [ ] If the occupant made factual claims in pleadings (ancestral property, agricultural land), verify them
- [ ] False claims can be used to attack credibility in any proceeding
- [ ] Interview surviving family members about original possession basis

### TIER 6 — Cross-Verification
- [ ] Is the entity that gave up its claim still active? Could it revive its claim?
- [ ] Search court records for any pending suits or caveats against the property
- [ ] Physical site inspection to confirm actual possession
- [ ] Interview neighbours about possession history

## Recommended Legal Opinion Structure

When presenting findings to counsel, structure the brief as:

1. **The gap:** "The title rests on a compromise decree, not a registered sale deed"
2. **The risk:** "If the compromise is invalidated, the current owner's entire chain collapses"
3. **The verification needed:** Tier 1 items ranked by how quickly each can be checked
4. **The fallback:** "If Tier 1 items cannot be verified within [time/cost], the property should not be transacted"
