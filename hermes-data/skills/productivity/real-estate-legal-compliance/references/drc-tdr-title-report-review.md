# DRC / TDR Title-Report Review (Karnataka / GBA)

Class-level checklist for reviewing a lawyer's title report on a **Development
Right Certificate (DRC)** or Transferable Development Rights (TDR) certificate in
Bengaluru, and for grading that report's solidity. This is the "critique the
opinion" workflow — grading a report someone else wrote — not the "read the
deeds and build the chain" workflow.

When NDR forwards a report and says *"give me your view, read out on this title
opinion and the solidity of this opinion"*, answer in this order:

1. **One-line verdict + comfort percentage**, plus what it is and isn't good for
   (e.g. "structurally competent, substantively not bankable").
2. **What the instrument actually is** — number, date, quantum, parties, stated
   value — restated off the face of the document, and correct his dictation
   (village / entity spellings) explicitly and in the same breath.
3. **Numbered material issues**, quoting the report's own words wherever it
   waives or presumes something.
4. **The single point that must be resolved before anything else.**
5. **Requisitions** to send the advocate.
6. **Commercial sanity check on the money** if he quoted value — do the
   arithmetic yourself and say what it implies.

Prose and numbered points, not a table dump. He wants the read-out, not a
document.

## What the instrument is

- A DRC is not land. It is a **FAR credit** issued by the planning authority
  (Greater Bengaluru Authority — GBA — since 2025; previously BDA) as
  compensation when the authority acquires land and the owner elects TDR
  instead of cash.
- **Quantum: DRC sqm is normally 2 × the acquired area in sqm.** Verify the
  multiple on the certificate face; don't assume.
- Value = DRC sqm × the stated ₹/sqm. The report usually calls this "market
  value of the surrendered plot".
- The certificate names **joint holders** — all must sign a transfer.
- Acquisition is run by the land-acquisition authority (BDA/SLAO); the
  certificate is issued by the planning authority (GBA). Reports flag this
  split without opining on it; that is normal and not itself a defect.

## Arithmetic to always run

For a stated DRC of `A` sqm at `R` ₹/sqm and an ask of `P`:

- Notional value = `A × R`
- Effective cost per sqm of FAR = `P ÷ A`
- Discount to notional = `1 − P ÷ (A × R)`

Then sanity-check the discount, because the notional number misleads:

- DRCs trade **well below** the stated ₹/sqm — they can only be set off against
  premium FAR and carry utilisation charges. A 30-35% "discount" to notional can
  be at or above the real market.
- Benchmark against (a) the going Bengaluru DRC/TDR rate and (b) what the SPV
  would pay the authority **directly** for the same premium FAR. If DRA has
  already costed premium FAR/TDR/BBMP charges for that project, use that
  working — never invent a rate.
- Confirm the **maximum percentage of FAR** the authority permits to be met via
  DRC at the destination plot before assuming the full quantum is usable.

Worked example (Byrathi, Oct 2026): 4,148.02 sqm × ₹36,300 = ₹15.06 cr
notional; ask ₹10 cr = ₹24,108/sqm of FAR, a 33.6% discount to notional. That is
a *notional* discount, not a market discount — say so.

## The highest-value check: award-versus-DRC double-dip

Reports frequently say both of these in adjacent paragraphs without reconciling
them:

1. "the Award payable to the owners was fixed at ₹X and the interest of ₹Y …
   totally amounting to ₹Z to [owner]"
2. "further to the option given to the landowners to claim the award in the form
   of transferable development rights **instead of monetary compensation**, the
   owners … have sought for grant of transferable development rights"

If the cash award was actually drawn **and** the DRC issued for the same extent,
the certificate is exposed to **recall / recovery by the issuing authority**.
That is a defect in the asset, not merely in title. Resolve it before valuing
anything: certified copy of the award, proof of how compensation was settled
(cash / TDR / part), and the authority's ledger entry showing the DRC valid,
unutilised and not under recovery.

## Grading the opinion itself

- **Who is it addressed to?** A report addressed to the vendor or broker that
  carries "use of this title search report for any other purpose or by any other
  person is not authorized … no liability is assumed" gives the buyer **no right
  of reliance**. The opinion is then legally useless to us however good it is.
  Obtain a fresh report in the SPV's name, or a reliance letter on the
  advocate's letterhead with enrolment number.
- **Is it actually signed?** A Word-generated PDF ending in a typed advocate
  name, no seal, no letterhead, no enrolment number, is not a signed opinion.
- **Who engaged the advocate?** If the broker or vendor introduced him, it is a
  vendor-side report — say so plainly.
- **What did the lawyer expressly WAIVE?** Search for "we have not insisted",
  "we cannot comment", "we are not furnished", "we have not instructed". Each is
  a hole, and it is often exactly the document NDR asked about.
- **Presumption vs proof.** "55 years have passed and nobody else has claimed"
  is a presumption of convenience, not proof. For every inheritance transmission
  ask: death certificate? family tree? Class I heir affidavit? If absent the
  transmission is unproven however old it is. A widow succeeding on a sale-deed
  recital alone is worse — the children take equally with her under the Hindu
  Succession Act.
- **Original vs photocopy.** "We have not inspected the original documents of
  title" downgrades the whole opinion.
- **Public notice.** If none was published, no third-party claims have been
  flushed out. On a property with any litigation history that is the wrong way
  round, and it should be said so.
- **Court / revenue search scope.** One disclosed suit, however well cured, is
  not a litigation search.
- **"Opinion subject to production of the following documents"** is not
  boilerplate. It is the lawyer's own list of what he could not verify — read
  every condition and mark which are substantive.
- **"We have not insisted on the endorsements … since the subject matter is
  development rights"** is a scope waiver, not a clean bill. Note it, then
  decide separately whether it matters for a DRC (usually less than for land —
  but say so rather than silently accepting).

## Verify the EC and RTC ladders — never accept them as stated

- Build the EC **period ladder** and check for gaps AND overlaps. A duplicate
  period carrying a different S.A. number means one is a correction — find out
  which, and note that the report body may never mention the duplicate that
  appears in its own annexure.
- Cross-check **every registered deed named in the chain narrative against the
  EC period that should contain it.** Reports routinely furnish an EC whose
  entries omit deeds the report itself recites (a 1968, 1980 and 1981 deed all
  missing from a 1950-1986 EC). The tell: those very deeds then reappear as
  "opinion conditions".
- Check the EC is from the **right sub-registrar**. A deed registered at another
  SR will not appear; a deed that should appear and doesn't is either a
  defective EC or a hidden transaction.
- RTC ladder: list the years **furnished**, not the years claimed. 3-5 year gaps
  are normal in these reports. Also check the first RTC year against the
  root-of-title date — an RTC stream starting decades after the grant means the
  earliest revenue record is missing.
- An Akarband furnished as an "online view only" copy is not a certified copy.

## Chain heads specific to DRC-origin land

DRC-origin parcels in Bengaluru's periphery are old agricultural land with long,
thin chains. Expect:

- **Root grant unproven.** A 1940s Deputy Commissioner grant (e.g.
  `D4.GP.79/1940-41`) is often unavailable — no grant order, no Saguvali Chit,
  no Grant Issue Register extract. 1940s grants commonly carried conditions
  (non-alienation, class restriction, resumption on breach). Say the root of
  title is unproven, and separately say whether 80+ years of undisturbed dealing
  makes that practically acceptable. Don't let the report's silence stand.
- **Name drift.** "Appaji Rao" in one paragraph and "Appaji Gowda" in the next,
  for the same grantee, is the kind of inconsistency these reports carry without
  comment. Flag it.
- **Fraud in the chain.** A registered sale deed executed by "fraudulent,
  unconcerned and fictitious persons", later declared void by a **compromise
  decree** where the fraudsters admitted they derived no title, is a reasonably
  strong cure — better than an ex-parte order. But it binds only those parties,
  it does not expunge the deed from the sub-registrar's register, and it says
  nothing about anyone who dealt with them in the interim. Ask for the FIR /
  criminal case status and the sub-registrar's note of the decree.
- **Vesting vs later sale.** Where acquisition was notified in 2005-2007 but the
  property was sold in 2023 and the award/DRC issued in 2026, the later sale
  deeds could convey only the **right to compensation**, not the land. Reports
  routinely omit this. Requisition an express confirmation that the sale deed
  conveyed the compensation claim.

## Requisition template

Certified copies of the root grant order, Saguvali Chit and Grant Issue Register
extract; death certificates and family trees for every transmission with
affidavits from all Class I heirs; a fresh **continuous** EC for the full period
from **both** sub-registrar offices, in which every deed recited in the chain
narrative actually appears; the RTCs for each gap year; FIR/criminal status on
any forged deed plus the sub-registrar's note of the curing decree; original
title deeds produced for inspection; the award and proof of how compensation was
settled; the certificate face showing validity and utilisation endorsements;
written confirmation from the TDR Cell on transferability, endorsement mechanics
and permitted percentage of FAR; express confirmation that the operative sale
deed conveyed the compensation claim; and a public notice.

## Pitfalls

- Do not accept the report's own framing of what it covered. Count the EC
  periods and the RTC years yourself.
- Do not re-do the lawyer's work. The value you add is spotting what he
  *waived*, what he *presumed*, and what he never addressed at all — plus the
  arithmetic.
- NDR's dictation of names arrives mangled: "BugsRanka" = BuxRanka,
  "VRDPL" = BRDPL, "Verity Village" = Byrathi. Correct it explicitly in the
  reply, and never create a Drive folder on the guessed spelling.
