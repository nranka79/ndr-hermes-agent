# Karnataka CLU / DC Conversion Cancellation — General Framework

> **Companion:** `references/karnataka-clu-reversion-application.md` for Serenity Hillview / Sy.93/2 Huralagurki case-specific facts (DC↔Tahsildar chain, BIAAPA OM dates, status ladder). This file covers the **general legal framework** that applies to any converted Karnataka plot where the conversion purpose is infructuous.

## The Two Separate Tracks

Many users conflate "CLU" and "DC Conversion" as one thing. They are **two separate approvals** from **two separate authorities** under **two separate statutes**. Cancelling one does NOT cancel the other.

| | DC Conversion | CLU (Change of Land Use) |
|---|---|---|
| **Law** | Section 95, Karnataka Land Revenue Act 1964 | Section 14-A, Karnataka Town & Country Planning Act 1961 |
| **Authority** | Deputy Commissioner (DC) of the revenue district | BMRDA / BDA / Local Planning Authority (through Government) |
| **What it changes** | Revenue record: agricultural → non-agricultural (Bhoomi/RTC) | Master Plan zone: agri → residential/commercial/industrial |
| **Cancellation path** | Application to DC under s.95(7) or suo motu by DC | Application to planning authority / Government for reversion |

## Grounds for Cancellation of DC Conversion

### 1. Non-fulfillment of condition (most applicable to the "no public road" scenario)

Section 95(2) KLRA allows the DC to grant conversion **subject to conditions**. Typical conditions include:

- The land shall be used only for the approved purpose (e.g., residential)
- Development shall begin within a specified period (commonly 2–5 years)
- The owner shall provide/prove access to a public road
- Drainage, setback, and other planning conditions

If any condition is violated or incapable of fulfillment — **especially if the conversion was for residential use but no public access road exists at all** — the DC has power to cancel the order under s.95(7).

### 2. Non-user / failure to develop within prescribed period

Many DC orders carry a **development timeline condition**:
> "conversion purpose must be put into effect within [2–5] years; failing which, the order may lapse and the land reverts to agricultural classification."

- If the conversion order is old (5+ years) and no residential structure or layout has been built, the DC can cancel on this ground alone.
- If the period hasn't expired but the purpose is **impossible from the outset** (no road → no plan sanction), impossibility can be argued as constructive failure.

### 3. Impossibility / conversion infructuous

When the specific purpose for which conversion was granted **cannot be achieved** due to factors outside the owner's control:
- **No public road abutting the land** → planning authority (BMRDA/BBMP) refuses plan sanction → the residential use cannot be realised → conversion is infructuous
- This is the strongest ground for a **voluntary cancellation petition** by the owner

### 4. Surrender by owner

The DC can accept a voluntary surrender of conversion rights. The owner applies stating the conversion is unworkable and requests reversion to agricultural status. The DC passes a speaking order cancelling the conversion.

## BMRDA / Planning Authority Dimension (The "No Road" Barrier)

- BMRDA's own **CLU Guidelines** (under KTCP s.14-A) require: *"Only properties having approach from existing public road"* as a precondition for CLU consideration
- **BMRDA Building Bye-Laws**: plan sanction is refused if the site has no access to a public road of prescribed width (typically 9–12 m depending on zone)
- If CLU was granted by BMRDA but there's no road, the CLU itself was granted on a non-sustainable basis — this strengthens the case for **both** DC cancellation AND BMRDA reversion

## The "5-Year Rule"

When a conversion is cancelled, Karnataka Land Revenue Rules impose a bar on **re-conversion**: the land cannot be re-converted to non-agricultural purpose for a specified period (commonly 5 years) from the date of cancellation.

- **This bar is irrelevant** if you plan to keep the land as agricultural or sell it as agricultural
- **It only matters** if you intend to re-convert within 5 years
- Get the DC's cancellation order to expressly record the reversion date so the bar's starting point is clear

## Recommended Strategy (Two-Track)

### Track A: Revenue — Cancel DC Conversion

1. **File application** to the Deputy Commissioner of the revenue district (Bengaluru Rural for Devanahalli/Hosakote belt, Bengaluru Urban for city-adjacent land)
2. **Grounds** (cite whichever apply):
   - Impossibility of use (no public road → no plan sanction)
   - Non-fulfillment of condition (if the conversion order required road access)
   - Surrender / voluntary cancellation
   - Non-user (if the development timeline has lapsed)
3. **Supporting documents**: conversion order, CLU order, BMRDA/BBMP plan sanction rejection letter (stating "no public road as the reason"), RTCs, survey sketch, site photos
4. **Hearing**: DC must issue notice and give hearing (per natural justice — Karnataka HC quashes cancellations done without notice)
5. **Outcome**: DC passes speaking order cancelling conversion → land reverts to agricultural in revenue records → get RTCs updated (Bhoomi)

### Track B: Town Planning — Revert the CLU

1. **Apply to the planning authority** (BMRDA through Hoskote Planning Authority or whichever LPA has jurisdiction)
2. **Ground**: CLU was granted for residential use but no plan sanction is possible → CLU cannot be given effect
3. **Request**: reclassify the land-use zone back to Agriculture in the Master Plan / zonal regulations
4. **Coordination**: the planning authority may need the DC's cancellation order as a prerequisite; or may act concurrently

## ⚠️ Key Traps

- **DC request for a report is NOT a cancellation order** — when the DC writes to the Tahsildar asking for a "no-development inspection report," that is a PRELIMINARY step, NOT a cancellation. Don't report progress until the DC has signed the cancellation order.
- **"5-year rule" is about RECONVERSION, not about the land remaining frozen** — once cancelled, the land reverts to agricultural. The 5-year bar only blocks re-converting it BACK to non-agricultural within that window.
- **CLU ≠ DC Conversion** — cancelling the DC conversion does not automatically revert the Master Plan classification. You need a separate BMRDA/LPA application unless the CLU was always conditional.
- **If physical development has occurred** (leveling, roads, structures), reversion to agricultural becomes harder and may require restoration orders.
- **Marketing/residential sales must stop** during the cancellation process — selling agricultural land as residential without valid CLU/conversion is a serious consumer protection / RERA risk.
