# Responding to a BMTF / Karnataka police notice (BNSS s.35)

**When to use.** A DRAAS project receives a notice from the Bengaluru Metropolitan Task Force
(BMTF) or any Karnataka police station calling on the company/directors to "appear for an
inquiry" and produce property documents (sale deed, conversion certificate, approved plan,
RTC/Akarband, mutation, NOCs, RERA NOC). Typical trigger allegation: encroachment on government
kharab land, buffer zone not left, byelaw/sanction-plan violation. Ranka Amber precedent:
notice No. `BMTF/ADGP/Petition/1237/2026`, PSI Manoj Kurane, hearing 07.09.2026.

`references/karnataka-labour-dept-bocw-cess-notices.md`.

scope, rebound hammer / UPV / laser survey / cores), chajja-and-extension removal, façade refit,
and answering NDR's "is the frame good enough?" question →
`references/structural-condition-assessment-and-facade-refit.md`. (Note: this umbrella's SKILL.md

## 1. The provision — BNSS s.35

Section 35 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (in force 01.07.2024) **merges CrPC
ss.41 and 41A into one section**. Karnataka HC states this expressly ("Sections 41 and 41-A of the
Cr.P.C., which is now under Section 35 of the BNSS").

- **35(3)** — "The police officer shall, in all cases where the arrest of a person is not required
  under sub-section (1), issue a notice directing the person against whom a reasonable complaint
  has been made, or credible information has been received, or a reasonable suspicion exists that
  he has committed a **cognizable offence**, to appear before him or at such other place as may be
  specified in the notice."
- **35(4)** — duty of the noticee to comply with the terms.
- **35(5)** — if he complies and continues to comply, he shall not be arrested unless, for reasons
  recorded, the officer is of the opinion he ought to be arrested.
- **35(6)** — on failure to comply / unwillingness to identify himself, the officer may, subject to
  orders of a competent court, arrest him.
- **35(7)** — no arrest without prior permission of an officer not below DSP rank for offences
  punishable under 3 years where the person is infirm or above 60.

Pre-conditions that follow: there must be a **registered cognizable offence** and a complaint
against the named person. **No crime = no power to issue the notice at all.** Watch the reference
format on the notice: a `.../Petition/<n>/<year>` reference indicates a petition/complaint source,
not a crime number — that is the tell that no FIR exists.

## 2. The Tavaragi drill — MANDATORY (this is the core of the defence)

**Sri Tavaragi Rajashekhar Shiva Prasad v. State of Karnataka**, W.P. No. 15125/2024, judgment
dated **19.07.2024**, Justice M. Nagaprasanna, Karnataka High Court. Because the State had not
notified guidelines, the Court laid down a mandatory drill for every s.35 notice:

- **(a)** The notice **shall mention the crime number and the offence alleged in the crime
  number** — communicable conventionally or electronically.
- **(b)** The communication **shall attach a copy of the FIR** so registered, as the FIR would
  contain the gist of the complaint.
- **(c)** In the event the notice does not contain the crime number, the offence alleged, or the
  appending of the FIR — **subject to just exceptions, the noticee is NOT OBLIGED to appear**
  before the officer who directed him to appear and **NO COERCIVE ACTION can be taken for
  non-appearance**.
- **(d)** The Police Department must make FIRs immediately uploadable and search-friendly.

The Registry was directed to transmit the order to the Secretary, Home Department, and the DG & IGP
to frame guidelines within 8 weeks.

Reasoning in the companion line, verbatim: *"the noticee, without knowing the crime number and
without getting a copy of the FIR, cannot be asked to appear before an officer of the police
station on receipt of notice."*

### Case law applying it (use these to show the rule is current, not a one-off)

| Case | Citation | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Sri. Gajendra K M v. State by PSI** | W.P. 31500/2024, **02.12.2024**, NC: 2024:KHC:49481, J. Hemant Chandangoudar | **On all fours.** Notice had no crime number, no offence, no FIR copy → held in contravention of the Tavaragi guidelines; notices **QUASHED**; liberty reserved to issue afresh strictly in accordance with law |
| **Sr. Sharlet Sebastian v. State of Karnataka** | W.P. 17877/2025, **20.06.2025**, NC: 2025:KHC:21641, J. S.R. Krishna Kumar | Drill restated and applied to a s.35(3) notice; notice bad for want of the FIR copy |
| **Shri. Ravi v. State of Karnataka** | Crl.P. 4788/2026, **26.03.2026**, NC: 2026:KHC:17348 | Drill restated and applied again in 2026 — current law |
| **M/s. Jh Quick Solutions Pvt Ltd v. State of Karnataka** | **25.06.2025** | Same drill applied with a **company** as noticee |

### Supreme Court — the latest position

**Satender Kumar Antil v. CBI**, 2026 INSC 115, order dated **15.01.2026**, Justices M.M. Sundresh
and Nongmeikapam Kotiswar Singh (following its own order dated 16.07.2025 and Satender Kumar Antil
(2022) 10 SCC 51; parent authority **Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar, (2014) 8 SCC 273**):

- A s.35(3) notice, for offences punishable up to 7 years, is **the rule**; arrest under s.35(6)
  r/w s.35(1)(b) is **the exception**.
- **Non-compliance with the notice does NOT ipso facto mandate arrest.** It is the last resort,
  after due exercise of discretion on necessity.
- Arrest under 35(6) must be based on **material and factors NOT available** to the officer when
  the notice was issued — so a defective first notice cannot be parlayed into an arrest.
- *"The power of arrest under Section 35(6) ... must be interpreted as a strict objective
  necessity, and not a subjective convenience for the police officer. It does not mean the police
  officer can arrest to simply ask questions."*
- Conclusion (a)–(f) in the order: arrest is statutory discretion, not mandatory; the officer must
  ask whether arrest is a necessity; the 35(1)(b)(i) + one-of-(ii) conditions must exist; notice is
  the rule; arrest not to be undertaken unless absolutely warranted.

## 3. BMTF's own jurisdiction — the second, independent defence

**Formation.** Karnataka Government Order (Urban Development Dept) dated **19.03.1996** created
BMTF. Home Department **Notification No. HD 231 PEG 95 dated 27.05.1996**, issued under **s.2(s)
CrPC 1973** (now s.2(1)(s) BNSS), published in the Gazette, declares the *"Office of the Deputy
Inspector General of Police, Bangalore Metropolitan Task Force, Bangalore"* to be a **police
station**, with territorial jurisdiction over the Bangalore Metropolitan Area and subject-matter
jurisdiction **only over the offences listed in its Schedule**. Confirmed in **Dr. S.M. Kalligudd
v. State of Karnataka, 1998(1) KARLJ 252 (28.05.1997)**.

**The scheduled offences — the complete list:**
- Karnataka Municipal Corporation Act, 1976
- Bangalore Development Authority Act, 1976
- Karnataka Municipalities Act, 1964
- Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Act, 1964
- Karnataka Slum Areas (Improvement and Clearance) Act, 1973
- read with only these IPC provisions, and only as ancillary to a main offence under the above:
  s.34; ss.107–120; s.120B; ss.143–145, 147–151; ss.217–218; ss.332, 333, 353;
  ss.379, 403, 406, 409, 417–420, 423, 424, 426, 427, 431–436, 447, 448, 455–457;
  ss.456, 466–468, 471–477, 477A
- and ss.54–56, 94, 98, 113, 114 of the Karnataka Police Act, 1963.

**The Karnataka Land Revenue Act, 1964 is NOT in the list.** That matters because a "kharab
encroachment" allegation is a KLR Act matter.

**Jurisdiction case law:**
- **H.J. Ravi v. State of Karnataka** (Crl.P., **22.06.2022**): BMTF was created "only to detect
  offences punishable under the provisions of Karnataka Municipal Corporation Act and the Bangalore
  Development Act ... and not to investigate any offences punishable under the provisions of IPC or
  under the provisions of Karnataka Land Revenue Act." Charge sheet + proceedings quashed.
- **Sri R Ramesh v. State of Karnataka** (**15.02.2019**): FIR by BMTF for IPC offences is without
  jurisdiction, liable to be quashed.
- **Brigade Enterprises Ltd v. BMTF** (**29.01.2015**, order on being spoken to), following
  **Crl.P. 5340/2012 dated 10.10.2013** and **W.P. Nos. 7246–7250/2013 dated 26.09.2013**: BMTF
  cannot investigate IPC ss.119/409 r/w 34; it may touch **KLR Act ss.192-A/192-B only if a prior
  show-cause notice had been issued** to the petitioner.
- **Rajanna v. BMTF** (30.07.2014); **Sri H Sandeep Singh v. State of Karnataka** (13.09.2023) —
  charge sheet quashed where the village lay outside the officer's jurisdiction.

**BBMP-land jurisdiction (2026 Division Bench) — decisive on the land itself.**
**W.A. No. 1531/2024 c/w W.A. Nos. 1505/2024 and 1532/2024**, NC: **2026:KHC:19625-DB**,
Division Bench of **Justices D.K. Singh and T.M. Nadaf**: *"though the land even if it is stated to
be remained as agricultural land, in view of the submission to the jurisdiction of BBMP, the
Revenue Authorities have no jurisdiction to entertain any complaint or pass any orders under the
provisions of the KLR Act."* And: *"the Revenue Authorities have lost their jurisdiction to take
any action..."* — the action lies with BBMP/GBA under **GBA Act ss.243(3) and 244(1)** where a
Katha has been issued by BBMP/GBA. So for a BBMP E-Khatha / PID property, the entire revenue-side
premise (and hence a BMTF "kharab" investigation) collapses.

**Preliminary inquiry angle.** If the notice says the complaint is "under inquiry", consider
**BNSS s.173(3)** — a discretionary, limited inquiry merely to ascertain whether a prima facie case
exists for registering a crime (offences 3–7 years). During such an inquiry there is no crime and
therefore **no power under s.35(3) to summon the proposed accused**; a notice to the suspect at
that stage is premature and without jurisdiction.

## 4. Reply skeleton (to the issuing PSI, through counsel)

1. Acknowledge receipt of the notice dated `<date>`.
2. Record that it discloses **no crime number, cites no section of law, and is unaccompanied by any
   copy of the complaint or FIR**.
3. Cite Tavaragi (W.P. 15125/2024, 19.07.2024), Gajendra K M (W.P. 31500/2024, 02.12.2024),
   Sharlet Sebastian (W.P. 17877/2025, 20.06.2025), Ravi (Crl.P. 4788/2026, 26.03.2026) and
   Satender Kumar Antil (2026 INSC 115).
4. State expressly that, per **clause (c) of the Tavaragi drill**, the noticee is not obliged to
   appear and no coercive action can be taken for non-appearance.
5. Plead **jurisdiction**: BMTF's notified charter (HD 231 PEG 95 dated 27.05.1996) covers only the
   five scheduled Acts and the specified ancillary IPC sections; a Kharab encroachment allegation
   under the KLR Act is outside it (H.J. Ravi, R Ramesh, Brigade). No offence under any scheduled
   Act is disclosed.
6. Plead that the property is a BBMP/GBA Katha property within BBMP limits, where the Revenue
   Authorities have no jurisdiction at all (W.A. 1531/2024 DB).
7. Record the factual falsity (e.g. RTC shows no Kharab; no nala/buffer in the vicinity).
8. Demand that, if the office still wishes to proceed, a compliant notice stating the crime number,
   the offence, and enclosing the FIR copy be furnished — whereupon the matter will be examined and
   a reply filed.
9. Reserve all rights, including to seek quashing of the notice and any proceedings founded on it,
   with costs.

## 5. Tactical rules (NDR's standing instruction: frustrate lawful extortion, stay within the law)

- **Do NOT appear** in response to a defective notice. Clause (c) protects you.
- **Do NOT produce or offer a single document.** Nothing to admit, and everything to gain by
  making them perfect their process first. **Article 20(3)** protects against compelled
  self-incrimination; a demand for title deeds in a police "inquiry" is objectionable on that
  ground too.
- **Reply only in writing, through counsel**, each time, demanding the crime number, the offence
  and the FIR copy. Each exchange resets the clock.
- Never treat it as an "inquiry" verbally; always write back asking for the statutory particulars.
- If an FIR is registered, file a **quash petition under Article 226 / s.528 BNSS** (successor to
  s.482 CrPC) immediately — the Karnataka HC has quashed on jurisdiction in virtually every
  contested BMTF matter.
- Keep ready: sanctioned plan (LP no.), building licence, and the RTC evidencing no Kharab.
- Insist all communication be in writing; record any phone call (Karnataka permits one-party
  recording).
- If any officer seeks money, that is material for the **Karnataka Lokayukta / ACB** and a report
  to the ADGP, BMTF — documented, never verbal.

## 5A. The information-asymmetry doctrine — ask FIRST, produce NOTHING (NDR, 2026-09-18)

This is the **general principle** behind the tactical rules above, and NDR states it in his own
words. Apply it to every notice of this class, not just BMTF:

> *"Reply only point to point… furnish the copy and details of the complainant and on what basis
> and evidence the complaint is given… Then if required we will produce the required documents
> from our side."*

**Sequence: denial → demand particulars → offer conditional production. Never enclosures.**

- **Never enclose, list, or volunteer any document in the first reply.** Not the sale deed, not
  the RTC, not the survey sketch, not a "enclosures:" block. The reply is a **demand**, not a
  return. Production is explicitly *conditional* on the complainant's material being furnished
  first ("should any material so furnished disclose a genuine requirement, our clients will
  produce the required documents from their side").
- **Never concede the relevance of anything.** A prior draft of the Ranka Amber reply said our
  clients "recognize the limited relevance of primary revenue records (such as current RTC and
  survey records)" while at the same time demanding survey material. That is self-defeating: it
  simultaneously disclaims the documents that *prove* the denial and invites the officer to
  ignore them. **Do not rank the authority's document list as relevant/irrelevant.** Where the
  list is objectionable (RERA NOC, town-planning NOCs), the correct move is to ask for the
  **specific provision of law and the specific purpose** each document serves — not to opine on
  which ones matter.
- **Do not ask for time.** An extension request ("4 to 6 weeks to retrieve archival records from
  off-site archives") is an implicit admission that the documents are material, and it converts a
  final reply into an interim one. NDR's instruction is to seek **closure**, not adjournment:
  drop the extension request and put the burden on the complainant.
- **The date promptness rule lives in the AHFL sub-reference** (2026-09-19,
  `references/ahfl-bmtf-notice-and-date-promptness.md`) — file the reply and flag the date
  discrepancy, do not sit and wait for the SI's answer. Flag the date discrepancy separately.

### Ranka Amber reply — the shape NDR approved and the traps in it

**Para 1 — deny point-to-point, factually, in his own words.** He dictates the exact
formulation and wants it reproduced, not paraphrased:
*"we have not encroached the plot about the kharab land - there is no kharab or government land in
this plot or any buffer zone."* Carried into the letter as three separate heads: (i) no Kharab /
Government / Government-revenue land within the plot; (ii) no buffer zone in the plot at all, so
the premise of a buffer violation cannot arise; (iii) absolute private converted land on a valid
BBMP e-Khatha with clear marketable title, built strictly within its own boundaries. Then: all
allegations denied in their entirety as baseless and false.

**Para 2 — demand, itemised.** (a) certified copy of the complaint/petition **with all annexures,
enclosures and documents filed therewith**; (b) full name, address, contact particulars and
identity of the complainant, **and whether the complainant is a person interested in the property
or a stranger thereto**; (c) the specific basis and the evidence on which the complaint was given
(survey sketch, revenue/spot inspection report, demarcation report, village map extract,
photographs); (d) the action-taken report recorded before the notice issued; (e) **the specific
provision of law** and the specific allegation of violation relied on. Close with the conditional
production offer.

**Para 3 — prayer.** Furnish particulars → **drop proceedings and close the complaint** as
baseless and false → no coercive action in the interregnum.

**Traps to avoid (all were live in the draft this session rewrote):**

| Stale element | Why it must go |
|---|---|
| `4 to 6 weeks` extension request | Converts a final reply into an interim one; concedes the documents are material |
| "recognize the limited relevance of primary revenue records…" | Disclaims the very records that prove the denial |
| "RE: … **BBMP Sanctioned Plan**, valid BBMP e-Khatha" | Sourcing the denial on an approved plan **numbers** that plan in the BMTF's records and re-opens the alleged byelaw violation the notice complains of. NDR: *"remove all the references like approved plan no."* Keep the title assertion + e-Khatha; drop every plan/licence number |
| A list of enclusures (survey hissa tippani, village map KMZ, village map) | Producing evidence before receiving the complaint. The enclosure set may have been *considered* mid-session; NDR's final instruction was to remove it entirely |
| "**INTERIM** representation" in the subject line | The letter is a final reply seeking closure, not an interim holding response |
| "interim submissions & non-coercive assurance" as its own section | Folded into the prayer; no standalone interim posture |

**Near-match hazard — "enclosure" survives legitimately.** Grep for `Enclosure` and you will hit
`all annexures, enclosures and documents filed therewith` in para 2(a). That is the *complainant's*
filing being demanded, not our enclosure list — it is correct and stays. Tell the two apart before
"fixing" it.

**The produce-only-if-required posture is the same doctrine as the police-complaint skill's
truncation rule, inverted:** there, do not over-disclose a mechanism the evidence does not
support; here, do not pre-emptively disclose documents the authority has not justified demanding.
Both come from the same instinct — never hand over material on the strength of an unspecified
allegation.

## 7. The docs API mechanics — two things that reliably bite

### 7a. Locating the doc when the link the user pasted 404s

NDR pastes Drive URLs whose **file id is wrong, truncated, or from another account**. Verified
2026-09-18: a pasted docs URL returned `HttpError 404 File not found` on all three accounts
(ndr@draas.com, ndr@ahfl.in, nishantranka@gmail.com) and `HttpError 403 The caller does not have
permission` from the default service.

**Do not ask the user to re-paste the link first.** Recover it yourself:

```python
svc = build_service("drive", "v3", service_name="google-draas")
svc.files().list(q="name contains '<Project>'", fields="files(id,name,mimeType,modifiedTime)",
                 pageSize=50, supportsAllDrives=True, includeItemsFromAllDrives=True).execute()
```

Match the draft by name pattern (`<YYYYMMDD>_<Project>_<Topic>_Draft`) and by `modifiedTime`.
The Ranka Amber BMTF reply was found this way at `1cQkTAKSjOujg9X4Is3mJf_3MI4Ur1e79VFxriOUd_A4`
(“20260906_RankaAmber_BMTF_Notice_Proposed_Reply_Draft”, in `DRA Realty / Ranka Amber / Ranka
Amber Legal Notices`) while the pasted id was garbage. Also walk `parents` upward to print the full
folder path rather than guessing it.

**A 403 (not 404) usually means "wrong account for this id", not "no access".** Enumerate which
account a service key maps to before concluding anything:

```python
svc.about().get(fields="user").execute()["user"]["emailAddress"]
```

### 7b. Wholesale replace of a Doc body — the trailing-newline trap

To rewrite a Google Doc in place without churning through per-paragraph
`deleteContentRange`/`insertText` pairs (which fragment styling and shift indices as you go), do
a **single delete of the whole segment + single insert at index 1**. Two API facts matter:

1. `deleteContentRange` **cannot include the newline character at the end of the segment.**
   Using the document's final `endIndex` fails with:

   ```
   Invalid requests[0].deleteContentRange: The range cannot include the newline
   character at the end of the segment.
   ```

   Fix: delete to **`endIndex - 1`**, not `endIndex`.
2. Compute `endIndex` from the live read, never from a hardcoded body length — an earlier
   partial edit leaves the doc shorter and the delete silently truncates real content.

```python
doc = svc.documents().get(documentId=DOC).execute()
end = doc["body"]["content"][-1]["endIndex"]          # read live
svc.documents().batchUpdate(documentId=DOC, body={"requests": [
    {"deleteContentRange": {"range": {"startIndex": 1, "endIndex": end - 1}}},
    {"insertText": {"location": {"index": 1}, "text": NEW_BODY}},
]}).execute()
```

**Always read the doc back and run programmatic content assertions** before telling the user it is
done — inline, in the same script:

```python
txt = "".join("".join(r.get("textRun", {}).get("content", "")
              for r in el["paragraph"].get("elements", []))
              for el in doc["body"]["content"] if "paragraph" in el)
for bad in ["Approved Plan", "Sanctioned Plan", "4 to 6 weeks", "interim"]:
    assert bad.lower() not in txt.lower(), f"stale text still present: {bad}"
```

This is what makes a "removed all references" instruction **verifiable** rather than a claim. The
`assert` list is the user's own list of banned strings — build it from what they told you to strip.
Also count characters before/after and report the delta.

### 7c. "Upload this to my drive" usually means "show me where it is"

When a doc already lives in Drive and the user asks to upload it, **check its current `parents`
first** and walk the path. In this session the doc was already filed in its correct project
sub-folder and the "upload" ask really meant the user had lost track of it because his own pasted
link 404'd. Verify, report the path and a working link, and offer to move a copy only if he names
a different destination. Do not create a duplicate file. (General Drive-filing conventions —
including the root-TMP-first rule — live in `draas-drive-organization`.)
