## Pitfalls

### Formatting — use tables, bold key terms, highlight missing from default template
The default Google-Doc-built offer letter template (Prathvi-style) is text-heavy and key terms are not visually distinguished. When the user says "make it visually appealing and easy to read":

**Use tables for all structured data sections:**
- Compensation breakdown (Base Pay | Attendance Allowance | Performance-Based Pay | Total)
- Office Hours & Attendance rows (sign-in window, sign-out window, break durations, Saturday rule)
- Leave entitlements (Casual Leave, Medical Leave, Public Holidays)
- Required joining-day documents
- KPI / performance areas (if listed inline)

**Table styling:**
- Header row: bold, blue-grey background (`EEF3F8`), white text
- Alternating row shading: white / very-light-blue-grey (`EEF3F8`)
- Saturday exemption row (if applicable): light yellow (`FFF9E6`) background to make it stand out
- Column widths: use fixed `col.width` in inches (e.g. 2.0" for label, 4.0" for value)

**Key term highlighting:**
- Bold the term names in the left column (Base Pay, Attendance Allowance, Performance-Based Pay)
- NOT bold the values — the values sit in the right column unbolded
- Section headers in the body: bold uppercase, small (10pt), dark blue (`003366`)

**Performance Pay — always as bullet points below the compensation table, NOT inline:**
- The compensation table shows the number only
- Directly below the table, a bullet list explains: (1) how it's earned, (2) when it's payable, (3) how it's paid, (4) no carry-forward rule

**If the letter is text-heavy (no tables), flag this to the user before rebuilding:**
When presenting the draft doc link, say: "This is the text-heavy version. If you want tables + visual hierarchy for the compensation and office-hours sections, I can rebuild it as a formatted .docx — which version do you prefer?" The user will usually choose the formatted version and this avoids an extra round-trip.

### KPA / KPI matrix is a probation deliverable, not a day-one artefact
- New roles often don't have a full KPA/KPI matrix on day one. The user prefers a **two-stage** KPI approach:
  1. **Section 7 of the offer letter** lists ~6 **indicative performance areas** (output/cadence, social engagement, content quality, AI/tool adoption, upskill, cross-function support — or role-specific equivalents).
  2. The offer letter explicitly commits to a **detailed KPA / KPI matrix delivered by the end of the probationary period**, jointly reviewed with the reporting manager and management. The matrix should specify:
     - Exact performance indicators
     - Minimum thresholds for each
     - Weightages
     - Formula for computing the performance-based pay component
     - Cadence and format of monitoring, review, and feedback
  3. From end-of-probation forward, that matrix is the basis for perf pay computation.
- Verbatim clause that worked:
  > "Your performance will be tracked against the following high-level performance areas. A detailed KPA / KPI matrix — specifying the exact performance indicators, minimum thresholds for each, the weightages, the formula for computing the performance-based pay component, and the cadence and format of monitoring, review, and feedback — will be formally shared with you, jointly reviewed with [Reporting Manager] and the management, and agreed with you, by the end of the probationary period. That matrix will, from that point forward, be the basis on which your performance is measured, monitored, and used to compute your performance pay."

### Opening paragraph — brief welcome, NOT a resume recap
- The skill's personalisation guidance (anchor on resume hooks) is a DEFAULT, not an absolute. For roles where the user explicitly says "the resume discussion is done, we just need to welcome her," strip the elaborate resume paragraph entirely.
- **Correct pattern:** "We are pleased to offer you the position of Content Creator at DRA Realty Private Limited, located in Bengaluru. Your proposed start date is [date]."
- **Wrong pattern (over-personalised):** listing degrees, prior companies, named tools, and portfolio details in the opening paragraph.
- The resume personalisation still belongs in the document — but in the "Primary Responsibilities" section, as a bridge sentence, NOT in the opener. Example: "Your background in [X] and experience with [Y tools] make you well-suited for the content production responsibilities below."
- Always confirm with the user: "Should the opener be a brief welcome, or do you want a full personalisation paragraph anchored on the resume?" If they say resume is done, use the brief welcome pattern.

### Performance Pay - explicit "Earned, Payable, Paid" language is mandatory
- The user always wants a clear paragraph in the offer letter covering: (1) **how** performance pay is earned, (2) **when** it becomes payable, (3) **how** it is paid.
- Don't just write "Performance-Based Pay: Rs X,XXX per month (paid quarterly, subject to performance appraisal)" - that's the Prathvi-style shorthand and it leaves too much ambiguity.
- Verbatim clause that worked for Pooja Karane (Jun 2026):
  > "Performance Pay - Earned, Payable, Paid: The Performance-Based Pay component is earned against the performance indicators and minimum thresholds that will be jointly agreed in the detailed KPA / KPI matrix to be formalised with you, [Reporting Manager], and the management, by the end of the probationary period. It is subject to a quarterly performance appraisal conducted by the management, and is payable at the end of each quarter, prorated for the portion of the quarter actually worked. Any performance pay not earned in a given quarter, by virtue of performance falling below the agreed minimum thresholds, will not be paid for that quarter and cannot be carried forward to subsequent quarters."
- The "no carry-forward" rule is important - without it, candidates assume unearned perf pay rolls over.
- For retention-heavy roles (e.g. Vinitha R Nov 2025 - 2-year retention with 60% quarterly payout after), the Vinitha pattern applies instead. Default to Pooja pattern unless the role is explicitly retention-tied.

### Performance Pay is optional - confirm before including
- The standard template includes Performance-Based Pay (Prathvi/Neha pattern), but **not all roles have it**. When the user says "no performance pay" or gives a total that's just Base + Attendance, remove the performance row from the compensation table entirely.
- **Language when performance pay is absent:** Instead of removing the topic entirely, add a brief note below the compensation table:
  > "Note: In addition to the above, a Performance-Based Pay component may be introduced upon successful completion of the probationary period, in alignment with company policy and at the discretion of the management."
- This preserves the option post-probation without committing to a specific amount now.
- **Also remove** the "Performance Pay mechanics" bullet section below the compensation table when there's no performance pay - those bullets only make sense with a live performance pay component.

## Part-Time / Hybrid Engagement Pattern

When the user describes a candidate/employee as part-time — "limited hours a day", "X days at office and Y days remote", "work from home" — use THIS pattern instead of the full-time template. Confirmed on Sinchana's contract (Trubld Integrated Services, Feb 2026; see `references/part-time-contract-sinchana.md`).

- **Working hours:** state the EXACT absolute work-time window (e.g. "12:00 PM (noon) to 4:00 PM") and explicitly add "with no lunch break and no breaks in between — this constitutes 4 hours of absolute work time per day". The user distinguishes absolute work time from office presence time — do NOT carry over the standard 1-hour break language from the full-time template.
- **Weekly pattern:** spell out which days are in-office and which are remote (e.g. Mon/Wed/Fri at office, Tue/Thu/Sat work from home, Sunday rest). If the user allows day-switching, add: "With prior permission of the Company, the Employee may switch an in-office day with a work-from-home day, or vice versa."
- **Weekly hours & timesheets:** compute weekly total = daily hours × working days (4 hrs × 6 days = 24 hrs/week). Update EVERY hours reference in the document to match: §2.3 timesheet minimum, §4.2 weekly hours, §4.3 weekly cap, and any Schedule C / CTC notes. Sinchana's contract had a stale "minimum 40 hours per week required" note in Schedule C that contradicted the 24-hr arrangement.
- **Leave for part-time:** the general HR Policy leave (12 CL + 10 Medical) applies to full-time employees ONLY. For part-timers the user grants a single all-inclusive reduced entitlement — e.g. "a total of 6 (six) paid leaves per annum, all-inclusive, covering earned/casual leaves as well as sick leaves", explicitly noting it "deviates from the general DRA HR Policy, which applies to full-time employees". Sick leave beyond the 6 is salary-deducted. Do NOT keep the full-time 12 EL + 10 SL clauses.
- **Edits in blue:** when the user says "make the edits in blue", apply `foregroundColor` RGB (17, 85, 204) = #1155CC to the CHANGED text only — black for untouched content. This is his review mechanism; he scans the blue to see exactly what changed.
- **Contract as Google Doc:** if the source is a `.docx`, the user expects the updated version as a NATIVE Google Doc — upload a converted copy (`drive.files().create` with `mimeType: application/vnd.google-apps.document`, same parent folder, `_Updated` name suffix), edit that, and give the link. Leave the original .docx untouched.
- **Clean template artifacts:** AI-generated contracts often contain literal `__BOLD_PHRASE_0__` markers and stray `**` from the template engine — strip them with `replaceAllText` (offset-safe) before finalising.

## Part-Time / Hybrid Leave Proration

When an employee works a **part-time or hybrid schedule** (fewer hours/day, fewer days/week than the full-time 8 hrs Mon–Fri + 6 hrs Sat), the user's rule is: **give HALF the paid (earned) leaves and HALF the sick leaves** of the full-time HR Policy entitlement (12 CL → **6 paid**, 10 SL → **5 sick**).

Key points learned from the Sinchana Gouda case (Feb 2026, Trubld Integrated Services):

1. **The offer/agreement MUST carry a specific clause** stating the exact prorated amounts AND explicitly noting this deviates from the general HR Policy (which applies to full-time employees). The user said: "put the exact amounts there in the offer letter."
2. **Check the existing doc first** — many contracts silently copy full-time numbers (12 EL + 10 SL). The user's voice instruction "we're giving her half" is the intent; the document may not match it yet. Report the discrepancy explicitly before editing.
3. **Verify the actual hours/day from the document** — the user will ask "how many hours a day is she working?" Pull the working-hours clause (e.g. Sinchana: 12:00 PM–5:00 PM Mon–Fri with 1-hr lunch = 4 hrs/day, 20 hrs/wk; timesheets min 4 hrs/day, 25 hrs/wk). **Flag internal contradictions** — Sinchana's §4.1 said 4 hrs/day but §4.4 said "3 days for 5 hours at office". Ask the user which is authoritative before finalizing.
4. **Confirm the office/remote split** — voice transcription may say "3-day at-office and 3-day remote" while the doc says 3 + 2 (5 working days). Check and confirm.
5. Keep all other leave mechanics (accrual, carry-forward, encashment, public holidays, advance application) per the HR Policy — only the *quantities* change.

Verbatim clause that worked (Sinchana, presented for user approval):

> **Leave Entitlement (Part-Time Schedule):** Your leave entitlement under this Agreement deviates from the DRA HR Policy, which applies to full-time employees working 8 hours per day. Given your part-time arrangement — 3 days at office and 2 days remote, ~4 hours of work per day (~20 hours per week) — you shall be entitled to:
> - Paid (Earned) Leave: **6 days per annum** (half of the full-time entitlement of 12)
> - Sick Leave: **5 days per annum** (half of the full-time entitlement of 10)
>
> All other leave terms (accrual, encashment, public holidays, advance application) remain per the HR Policy and the remainder of this Agreement.

## Appointment Letters vs Offer Letters

The dra-employment-documents umbrella covers both **offer letters** (for new external hires) and **appointment letters** (for internal transitions, consultant-to-employee conversions, or confirmations of existing full-time employment). They differ in key ways:

| Dimension | Offer Letter | Appointment Letter |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Tone | We are pleased to offer you the position of... | We are pleased to confirm your full-time employment with... effective [retrospective date] |
| Probation | Standard 6-month clause | Waived for consultant transitions |
| Background | Welcomes new joiner | References prior consultancy tenure |
| CTC language | Standard Base + Attendance + Perf | May use Incentive Advance pattern |
| JD section | Optional - often deferred to KPI matrix | Detailed, multi-section JD expected |
| Email delivery | Candidate's personal email | Company email + CC HR |
| Document title | YYYYMMDD_Entity_CandidateName_OfferLetter_Title | YYYYMMDD_Entity_EmployeeName_AppointmentLetter_Title |

Always ask the user which document type they want before drafting - the distinction matters for template used and tone.

### GWS token expiry format
The `gws_auth` token file occasionally stores `expiry` as an integer (seconds-from-now, e.g. 3599) instead of an ISO datetime string. This crashes `Credentials.from_authorized_user_file()` with `AttributeError: 'int' object has no attribute 'rstrip'`. Fix before calling `build_service`:
```python
token = json.load(open(token_path))
if isinstance(token.get('expiry'), int):
    from datetime import datetime, timezone, timedelta
    token['expiry'] = (datetime.now(timezone.utc) + timedelta(seconds=token['expiry'])).strftime('%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ')
    json.dump(token, open(token_path, 'w'), indent=2)
```

### Abandon messy patches and create a fresh vN doc
- When patching a Google Doc across several non-contiguous regions with `deleteContentRange` + `insertText` in one batchUpdate, it's easy for offset tracking to go wrong (a section can get inserted in the middle of a paragraph, breaking the surrounding text). See `gws-automation` PITFALL #3 for the technical details.
- **Docs with TABLES break self-computed offsets.** A paragraphs-only text walk (iterating `body.content` for `paragraph` elements and accumulating indices) produces WRONG absolute indices when the doc contains tables, inline objects, or page breaks — table cell text and object placeholders occupy index space the walk doesn't count. A `deleteContentRange`+`insertText` at such an offset can land in the MIDDLE of a table cell and mangle it (observed: a "Payment Method: Direct bank transfer..." row corrupted into "Payme...th"). Two safe patterns: (a) prefer `replaceAllText` — it is offset-safe by design; (b) if delete/insert is unavoidable, take `startIndex` from the element metadata returned by the API (authoritative) rather than accumulating your own counter, and re-fetch between edits.
- **If two recovery passes haven't fixed the mess, abandon the patch entirely and create a fresh vN doc with the full corrected content**, then trash the broken one. Don't try to be heroic — the user's review time on a v2 with structural bugs is more expensive than a fresh v3 build.
- After trashing, send the user the fresh doc's link with a one-line note: "v2 had a section-merge bug, recreated as v3 — please review the fresh link."

## Editing an Existing Employment Contract on Drive (docx → Google Doc, all edits in BLUE)

When the user says "update her employment contract on the drive, make the edits in the contract itself, all edits in blue":

1. **The file on Drive is usually a .docx** (employment contracts land as .docx, e.g. `01022026_Shinchana_EmploymentContract.docx`). Convert to a native Google Doc by re-uploading: `drive.files().create(body={'name': same_name + '_Updated', 'mimeType': 'application/vnd.google-apps.document', 'parents': [same folder]}, media_body=MediaFileUpload(...))`. Original .docx stays untouched.
2. **Clean template artifacts FIRST** via replaceAllText: `__BOLD_PHRASE_0__` markers and stray `**` (AI-generated docx templates leave these literal in the text). The Google Doc will otherwise show them to the employee.
3. **Make content edits with replaceAllText (offset-safe), NOT deleteContentRange+insertText** — see pitfall below.
4. **Color every edited span blue** `#1155CC` (rgb 17/255, 85/255, 204/255) via `updateTextStyle` foregroundColor. User preference: edits visible in blue so the reviewer (often Roshni) can spot changes at a glance.
5. **Verify:** re-fetch the doc, run the checklist (old values absent / new values present / no stray old numbers / blue applied to edited runs), then share the link.

**PITFALL — offset math breaks when the doc contains TABLES (Schedule C etc.):**
- Building a text map by walking only top-level `body['content']` paragraphs MISSES table-cell text, so absolute indices computed from that map are wrong for anything located after a table. `deleteContentRange`+`insertText` then lands in the wrong place (observed: an edit corrupted the "Payment Method" row in Schedule C — text got mashed together).
- Fix: for replacements use `replaceAllText` (no offsets needed). For styling, re-fetch the doc and use each paragraph's **authoritative `startIndex` from the API response**, walking recursively INTO tables (`element['table']['tableRows'][...]['tableCells'][...]['content']`). Reset accidental color leaks by restyling the neighbouring paragraph to black.

**PITFALL — Drive permission removal fails with 403 cannotDeletePermission (inherited):**
- If the Google Doc lives in a folder shared with outsiders (e.g. "Employment Contracts" folder shared with the design firm findingform.design), their access is **inherited from the folder** — `permissions().delete()` on the file returns `403 cannotDeletePermission ... If the permission is inherited`.
- Fix: **move the file** to a folder the outsiders can't see (e.g. the HR folder, owned only by ndr@draas.com), then re-grant the intended editors (rnr@draas.com writer) directly on the file. Inherited grants drop off automatically with the move; verify with `permissions().list()`.
- Note: moving also drops "anyone with link" access if the destination folder has none — usually desired for DRA docs ("This is a DRA document").
- The doc's link (file ID) does NOT change when moved — WhatsApp/email links keep working.

#### 1. Gmail — batch extraction for employee emails
```python
from tools.gws_auth import build_service
svc = build_service('gmail', 'v1')

# Search query — cover all email addresses the employee uses
query = '(from:email1 OR to:email1 OR cc:email1 OR from:email2 OR to:email2 OR cc:email2) after:2023/1/1'

# Step 1: Get ALL message IDs (paginate 500 at a time)
all_ids = []
page_token = None
while True:
    params = {'userId': 'me', 'q': query, 'maxResults': 500}
    if page_token:
        params['pageToken'] = page_token
    result = svc.users().messages().list(**params).execute()
    all_ids.extend([m['id'] for m in result.get('messages', [])])
    page_token = result.get('nextPageToken')
    if not page_token:
        break

# Step 2: Fetch metadata in batches of 100
emails = []
for i in range(0, len(all_ids), 100):
    batch = all_ids[i:i+100]
    for msg_id in batch:
        msg = svc.users().messages().get(
            userId='me', id=msg_id, format='metadata',
            metadataHeaders=['From','To','Cc','Subject','Date']
        ).execute()
        headers = {h['name']: h['value'] for h in msg.get('payload',{}).get('headers',[])}
        emails.append({
            'id': msg_id,
            'from': headers.get('From',''),
            'to': headers.get('To',''),
            'cc': headers.get('Cc',''),
            'subject': headers.get('Subject',''),
            'date': headers.get('Date',''),
            'snippet': msg.get('snippet',''),
            'internalDate': msg.get('internalDate','')
        })
    time.sleep(0.5)
```

**PITFALL — `resultSizeEstimate` often underestimates.** Always paginate fully until `nextPageToken` is absent. The estimate can be 201 while actual count is 444.

**PITFALL — `build_service` may timeout for large attachment downloads.** If `attachments().get()` times out in terminal(), use the direct token loading pattern:

#### 1a. Downloading attachments from forwarded emails (Fwd:)

When the target email is a **Forward** (subject starts with "Fwd:"), attachments are nested inside the `message/rfc822` part of the Gmail API tree, NOT in the outer payload:

```python
def find_and_save_attachments(part, msg_id, svc, depth=0):
    fn = part.get('filename', '')
    body = part.get('body', {})
    if fn and body.get('attachmentId'):
        att = svc.users().messages().attachments().get(
            userId='me', messageId=msg_id, id=body['attachmentId']
        ).execute()
        data = base64.urlsafe_b64decode(att['data'])
        with open(f'/opt/data/{fn}', 'wb') as f:
            f.write(data)
        print(f'Saved: {fn} ({len(data)} bytes)')
    if part.get('parts'):
        for sub in part['parts']:
            find_and_save_attachments(sub, msg_id, svc, depth+1)
    if part.get('mimeType') == 'message/rfc822' and part.get('parts'):
        for sub in part['parts']:
            find_and_save_attachments(sub, msg_id, svc, depth+1)
```

**Gotcha:** Forwarded emails often carry only the spreadsheet attachment. Incentive policy PDFs referenced in the email body may need to be found separately on Drive via file search.

## Employee Onboarding — Data Gathering for Google Workspace Account Creation

### When to use this workflow
- User says "create a draas.com account for [employee name]"
- User asks "give me all details of [employee]" for account setup — phone, address, role, manager, department
- Employee has joined/is joining and needs their `@draas.com` email provisioned

### Workflow

1. **Get the employee name** — often approximate from voice transcription. Cross-check against email headers and offer letter/resume file names.

2. **Search Gmail for the offer letter email thread** — The offer letter (`.docx` attachment) is the primary source for phone, CTC breakdown, role, manager, DOJ, and entity.
   - Search pattern: `from:ndr@draas.com OR from:rnr@draas.com "offer letter" [name]`
   - The offer is usually sent as a `.docx` attachment (NOT PDF). Download via `gmail.users().messages().attachments().get()`.
   - Also look for candidate's reply emails — email address, preferences, DOJ confirmation

3. **Extract text from .docx** — `python-docx` may not be installed. Reliable fallback: unzip + XML parse:
   ```python
   import zipfile, xml.etree.ElementTree as ET
   with zipfile.ZipFile(docx_path) as z:
       with z.open('word/document.xml') as f:
           tree = ET.parse(f)
           text_parts = []
           for t in tree.iter('{http://schemas.openxmlformats.org/wordprocessingml/2006/main}t'):
               if t.text:
                   text_parts.append(t.text)
           full_text = ' '.join(text_parts)
   ```

4. **Search Drive for resume + offer letter (Google Docs version)** — Resume has city/area, education, skills. Offer letter may also exist as a Google Doc.
   - Search: `name contains '[name]'` across Drive
   - Check DRA Recruitment folder (`1wHFU4pv7-h6_OZYx678R_LTcf1jOteoZ`) and naukri resumes folder
   - Resume is usually PDF — extract text with `pdftotext`
   - Google Doc offer letter — use `docs.documents().get()` for cleaner extraction

5. **Compile structured summary** — Present to user as labeled fields:

   | Field | Typical Source |
   |-------|---------------|
   | Full Name | Offer letter header |
   | Phone | Offer letter / Resume (not always in body text — check the address block) |
   | Personal Email | Offer letter addressee block or candidate's email headers |
   | Role/Title | Offer letter — Section 1 |
   | Department | Offer letter — e.g. "Content & Marketing" → user prefers simplified "Marketing" |
   | Manager name & email | Offer letter — Section 1 (Reports to) |
   | Entity/Company | Offer letter header (DRA Realty / DRA Developers / etc.) |
   | Employment type | Offer letter — Full-time, Probation status |
   | CTC breakdown | Offer letter compensation table (Base + Attendance + Performance) |
   | DOJ | Offer letter — Section 1 |
   | Residential Address | Resume (city/area only, often not full). Offer letter may say "to be confirmed at joining" — flag this |
   | Education | Resume |
   | Email convention | Default: first-initial + last-name (e.g. nVaddadi@draas.com). Confirm with user. |

6. **Flag missing fields** — The full residential address is frequently absent (offer letter placeholder: *"[full address to be confirmed at joining]"*). Source options:
   - **Bharat H** (`sales1.blr@draas.com`) — handles joining formalities, collects KYC docs
   - **Roshni Ranka** — may have personal details from hiring process
   - User may need to ask employee directly for emergency contact details

7. **Google Workspace Admin limitation** — As documented in the pitfall above, `gws_auth` tokens do NOT cover the Admin SDK. You CANNOT create the account. You provide the compiled data and the user creates it manually at `admin.google.com`.

8. **Verify email availability** — Once account is created, check that any `@draas.com` draft emails can be addressed to the new ID (delivery will succeed once the account exists).

### Pitfalls
- **No python-docx** — Use zipfile+XML fallback (see step 3) instead of trying to pip install mid-task
- **Address is often missing** — The offer letter says "to be confirmed at joining". The resume at best shows city/pin. Always flag this — don't present "address: available" when only city is known
- **Voice transcription name errors** — Cross-check against offer letter filename (`YYYYMMDD_DRA_CandidateName_...`) and email headers. The attached file is authoritative
- **Gmail may have multiple threads** — Offer letter thread, acceptance thread, and later email-ID request thread. Check the most recent for any data changes
- **Resume vs offer letter phone** — Both should match. If they differ, flag it
- **Email convention confirmation** — User may deviate from first-initial+last-name for specific roles/entities. Present your assumption and ask for confirmation
- **Offer letter may be Google Doc on Drive** — If found, use `docs.documents().get()` for text (cleaner than docx extraction)
- **Resume text via pdftotext** — Use `subprocess.run(['pdftotext', path, '-'], capture_output=True, text=True)` — reliable for text-based PDFs
