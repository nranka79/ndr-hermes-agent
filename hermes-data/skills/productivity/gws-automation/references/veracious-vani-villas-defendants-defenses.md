## LANDOWNER DEFENSES (D4–D8) — All Failed or Weak

### Defense 1: "Co-obligor only, not primary obligor"
**Argument:** Under Clause 3.6 and 10.1-C of DTD, only D1–D3 are liable. Landowners are mere security providers / co-obligors — their liability is derivative and secondary. They received no NCD proceeds.

**Legal basis:** Contract law — surety/co-obligor's liability is secondary; co-obligors can invoke principal debtor's defenses.

**Assessment:** **Medium strength against personal liability; weak against property enforcement.** The registered mortgage creates a property obligation (runs with the land) that is independent of personal contractual liability. The mortgage can be enforced against the 128 apartments regardless of whether the landowners escape personal liability.

**Status:** Rejected at interlocutory stage; Commercial Court held this is a "triable issue" for full trial (May 2025).

---

### Defense 2: Fraud in execution / misrepresentation
**Argument:** Deceased D5 and D4/D6/D8 were induced to sign documents by fraud — they believed they were signing standard JDA/development documents, not financial guarantees. D4 is 73 years old. No independent legal advice. Developer (D1) was their son-in-law (married to D8) — relationship of trust.

**Legal basis:** (1986) 3 SCC 156 — *Central Inland Water Transport v/s Brojo Nath Ganguly* (unconscionable contracts); (2021) 4 SCC 713 — *Avital Post Studioz v/s HSBC PI Holdings* (fraudulent inducement).

**Assessment:** **Weak — delaying tactic.** Registered mortgage deed was executed before sub-registrar who reads key terms. Presumption of proper execution. Commercial Court held this is a "triable issue" — cannot be decided at interlocutory stage. Even if fraud proven against personal liability, mortgage may still bind property.

**Status:** Rejected at interlocutory stage (IA LX/LXI, May 2025).

---

### Defense 3: Not responsible for developer's unauthorized sales
**Argument:** Landowners never gave consent for sale of their 19 unsold units (Schedule D flats); developer (D1) sold these units unilaterally without NOC from Debenture Trustee. Landowners received no proceeds. Cannot be penalized for developer's violations.

**Legal basis:** DTD Clause 8.1A required prior written consent from Debenture Trustee for any sale of unsold units. Landowners claim they never consented.

**Assessment:** **Factually plausible but legally weak.** As registered owners and mortgagors of the property, landowners bear responsibility for its disposition. Their remedy is against the developer (for unauthorized sales) — it does not extinguish the mortgage obligation. The issue of unauthorized sales affects **damages** (proceeds that should have gone to escrow), not the **enforceability of the mortgage itself**.

---

### Defense 4: Dispossession by developer
**Argument:** Developer forcibly took physical possession of landowners' apartment share, collected rent, and sold units without permission. Landowners received no economic benefit from NCD transaction. Therefore they should not bear full mortgage burden.

**Legal basis:** Equitable argument — lenders worked with developer; developer misappropriated; landowners are collateral damage.

**Assessment:** **Sympathetic but legally weak.** Mortgage creates charge on property — it does not require landowner to retain possession as condition of validity. Landowners' remedy is separate claim against developer under JDA/supplementary agreement. Does not extinguish mortgage.

---

### Defense 5: Delete from suit (IA LX/LXI)
**Argument (D5a/b):** Deceased D5 was not a necessary party; surviving heirs should be deleted under Order I Rule 10(2) CPC. Section 2(30) Companies Act definition of debenture; Section 71 Companies Act; Rule 18(1)(b).

**Court finding:** The deceased signed the documents. What role he played and to what extent liability can be fastened on him is a **subject matter of trial**. Without taking the case for trial at this stage, it would not be just to infer he was not a necessary party. Triable issue.

**Status:** **REJECTED May 3, 2025.** Both IA LX (dismissal under Order VI Rule 16) and IA LXI (deletion under Order I Rule 10(2)) were rejected. Case will proceed to full trial.

## PROMOTER DEFENSES (D1–D3)

### Defense: Transaction Audit flawed / IA 302 claim exaggerated
**Argument:** COC appointment of Sarath & Associates was not unanimous; audit period improperly extended; some transactions were legitimate business expenses; write-offs had board approvals.

**Status:** **FAILED.** NCLT Bengaluru Bench, order dated 14.12.2023 in IA 302/2020: held former directors jointly and severally liable for Rs. 13,21,92,691/- under Section 66(1)&(2) IBC. Order has been passed; this is now final unless appealed to higher court.

---

### Defense: IBC moratorium bars civil suit
**Argument:** NCLT order (17.01.2020) under Section 33(2) IBC started moratorium; civil suit enforcement should be stayed.

**Legal position:** Moratorium applies to **Corporate Debtor** (D1) only, not to individual promoters (D2/D3) or landowners (D4–D8). Secured creditors with valid mortgage can generally enforce against security independently of IBC process. Enforcement of secured claims is not automatically stayed by moratorium.

**Assessment:** **Contentious but generally fails** — courts allow secured lenders to proceed against collateral notwithstanding IBC moratorium on corporate debtor.

---

## ALLOTTEE DEFENSES (D9–D40)

### Defense: Independent title / purchase before mortgage
**Argument:** Allottees purchased from developer under valid sale agreements, paid full consideration, have independent title. Mortgage, even if valid, cannot be enforced against apartments already transferred to third parties without notice.

**Legal position:**
- Mortgage registered March 10, 2016
- Most allottees booked after that date (Schedule D of enforcement suit shows bookings from April 2016 onward)
- Registration constitutes **constructive notice** under Section 3 TPA — anyone who purchases after registration is deemed to have notice
- DTD covenant required developer to incorporate mortgage clause in sale deeds and direct payments to escrow

**Assessment:** **Medium strength (fact-specific).** For any allottee who purchased after March 2016, constructive notice of the registered mortgage is very difficult to escape. For the few who purchased before (if any), the argument is stronger. But overall, the registration is the key fact that weakens this defense.

---

### Defense: Bona fide purchaser for value without notice
**Argument:** Even with constructive notice via registration, allottees can claim BFP status — purchased without actual knowledge, paid market price, should be protected.

**Legal position:** For registered mortgages, protection for BFP is limited. Consumer protection / RERA principles may provide some shelter for home buyers. However, the lenders' security interest was created before these sales — the allottees took subject to the mortgage.

**Assessment:** **Fact-intensive; generally weak for post-March 2016 purchases.** The IA58 attachment application is precisely designed to freeze these units pending resolution of this issue.

## PROCEDURAL DEFENSES

### Delete from suit (IA LX/LXI) — FAILED
See above — Commercial Court rejected on May 3, 2025.

### Jurisdiction challenge
**Argument:** Commercial Court lacks jurisdiction over mortgage enforcement under TPA.

**Status:** Appears to have been abandoned — Commercial Court has been actively hearing Comm.OS.No.13/2019 since 2019.

### Arbitration clause
**Argument:** DTD contains arbitration clause; plaintiffs filed court action without prior arbitration.

**Status:** Likely rejected — defendant participated in proceedings without fresh objection. Common delaying tactic; generally fails when raised late.

## STRATEGIC TAKEAWAYS FOR LENDER

1. **Focus on property enforcement (IA58)** — the registered mortgage is the cornerstone. It binds the 128 apartments regardless of personal liability arguments. IA58 targets 45 unsold landowner flats — the most important pending proceeding.

2. **Landowners' "co-obligor only" defense is their strongest but is undermined by the registered mortgage** — property obligations run with the land. The personal liability argument is secondary.

3. **Fraud in execution is a delaying tactic** — requires full trial (3–5 years). Commercial Court recognized this. Prepare for long proceedings on this specific issue.

4. **Allottees' title claims are a significant risk** — if bona fide purchasers without notice are proven for even some of the 74 sold units, lenders' security package is eroded. The IA58 freezes these units.

5. **Escrow violations (Rs. 15.82 Cr outside escrow)** are well-documented by Sarath & Associates audit (March 2024). Strengthens damages claim and IA58 attachment application.

6. **Section 66 order (Rs. 13.21 Cr) is final** — recovery depends on whether directors have attachable assets. Separate enforcement exercise.

7. **Mortgage Deed P1 covenants** — Clause 10.3.1 (non-disposal), Clause 5.2(a) escrow obligations, Clause 8.1A (prior consent for sales) are all violated by developer's actions. These give lenders acceleration rights (Clause 18(1)(a)) and enforcement rights (Clause 18(1)(b)).
