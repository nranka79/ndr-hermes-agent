# Habit Cough (Somatic Cough Syndrome) — Research Findings

Research compiled from PubMed live search (Jun 2026) during Ruhaan Ranka's persistent cough second-opinion dossier. The key clinical feature: **cough completely disappears during sleep**.

---

## Key Papers

### 1. Wright & Balfour-Lynn — *Pediatr Pulmonol* 2018 (PMID 29363880)

**Title:** Habit-tic cough: Presentation and outcome with simple reassurance.

**Key stats from 55 children:**
- **93%** had cough absent during sleep
- **93%** received prior medications (median 3 trials) — **none** resolved the cough
- **82%** resolved completely after a single consultation with simple reassurance
- **59%** resolved within 4 weeks (12% on the day itself)
- **96% resolution** in parents who BELIEVED the diagnosis vs **54%** in skeptical parents
- Median age 9.9 years, median cough duration 3 months (range up to 3 years)
- 18% later diagnosed with tic disorder or behavioral issue

**Conclusion:** "The crucial question is whether the cough disappears during sleep."

### 2. Haydour et al. — *Chest* 2014 (PMID 24833061)

**Title:** Management and diagnosis of psychogenic cough, habit cough, and tic cough: a systematic review.

**Findings from 223 patients (96% children):**
- **95%** had no cough during sleep
- **Suggestion therapy**: 96% resolution
- **Hypnosis**: 78% resolution, 5% improved
- Nonpharmacologic approaches consistently outperformed medications
- Low quality evidence overall (no RCTs, all retrospective)

### 3. Vertigan et al. — *Chest* 2015 (PMID 25856777)

**Title:** Somatic Cough Syndrome and Tic Cough in Adults and Children: CHEST Guideline.

**Key Changes:**
- Renamed "habit cough" → **somatic cough syndrome**
- Renamed "psychogenic cough" → **tic cough**
- First-line: **nonpharmacologic** — hypnosis, suggestion therapy, counseling, reassurance
- Cough absent during sleep = critical diagnostic feature

### 4. Weinberger & Buettner — *Pediatr Pulmonol* 2024 (PMID 37921546)

**Title:** The Habit Cough Syndrome.

- Recent update confirming the diagnostic approach
- Re-emphasizes: single session reassurance as first-line treatment

## Clinical Implications for Ruhaan's Case

| Feature | Supports Habit Cough | Suggests Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Cough disappears during sleep | **Pathognomonic** (93% of habit cough) | Atypical for asthma (worsens at night) |
| Normal FEV1 (93%), FeNO 21 | Expected in habit cough | Possible in SAD with normal FEV1 |
| Low FEF25-75 (50-76% across 4 PFTs) | Atypical — not typical for pure habit cough | Core feature of SAD |
| No BD response (+2.2%) | Expected | Possible in SAD |
| Failed steroid + nebs | Expected (93% had prior failed trials) | Also possible with steroid-resistant cough |
| Two clinicians diagnosed habit cough | Consistent with literature | — |

**Key takeaway:** The two diagnoses can coexist. Habit cough explains the sleep-disappearing pattern. Low FEF25-75 suggests underlying SAD. Best next step: **IOS** to quantify small airway involvement. If IOS normal, habit cough is the primary diagnosis and reassurance/suggestion therapy is indicated.
