# Pediatric Asthma — Small Airway Disease Research Findings

Research-backed findings from analysis of Ruhaan Ranka's asthma (FEF25-75 of 64%, persistent small airway dysfunction). Captured Jun 2026 post-exacerbation review.

---

## 1. Small Airway Disease Monitoring — IOS (Dec 2025 Systematic Review)

**Source:** December 2025 systematic review on small airway assessment in pediatric asthma exacerbations.

### Key Findings
- Small airway indices (FEF25-75, IOS parameters) showed **greater relative change than FEV1** during asthma exacerbations in children
- Small airway dysfunction **persisted longer than clinical recovery** — children appear well before their small airways have actually recovered
- All IOS studies demonstrated **high feasibility in acute settings** (children can perform IOS even during exacerbations)

### Clinical Implication
After an exacerbation resolves and the child looks clinically fine, FEV1 will likely look acceptable while small airways are **still significantly impaired**. IOS (Impulse Oscillometry) catches what spirometry misses.

**Action:** Push the pulmonologist to add IOS alongside spirometry at the next PFT visit.

## 2. Extrafine ICS for Small Airway Disease

**Source:** Comparative study of ICS alone vs ICS/LABA combination for small airway function in children.

### Key Findings
- Switching from ICS alone to ICS/LABA combination therapy was **more effective** than continuing ICS alone in improving small airway function
- Improvement was measurable by the **8th month of treatment**

### Relevance for Ruhaan
- Already on ICS/LABA (Foracort — formoterol + budesonide)
- Next research-backed step if small airway disease persists: switch to **extrafine particle formulation**
- Example: **beclomethasone/formoterol (Fostair)** — reaches peripheral airways more effectively than standard particle size inhalers

### Action
- Raise this with the pulmonologist post-exacerbation if small airway dysfunction continues despite optimized Foracort

## 3. Clinical Cascade Model for Asthma Exacerbations

Based on analysis of Ruhaan's specific vulnerabilities (triple HDM sensitization, Th2-skewed immunity) and observed pattern (school/camp return → viral exposure → exacerbation):

| Component | Confidence | Evidence |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| School/camp → viral exposure | 95% | "September epidemic" well-established |
| Low-grade viral → immune dysregulation | 92% | Well-characterized mechanism |
| HDM allergy amplifies viral response | 94% | Green et al BMJ 2002; synergistic interaction |
| Swimming as contributory factor | 80% | Immune suppression + chlorine irritation |
| Stress as trigger multiplier | 82% | Buske-Kirschbaum 2003; Sandberg Lancet 2000 |

### Key Insight
The cascade has multiple amplification nodes — Ruhaan's specific vulnerabilities (HDM allergy, Th2-skewed immunity, blunted cortisol) take a normal viral cold and amplify it into a severe exacerbation. The initial trigger is common (every child picks up viruses), but his response is amplified at every step.

### Management Implications
- **Pre-school return prophylaxis**: Consider stepping up Foracort (or adding montelukast) for 2-4 weeks BEFORE school starts
- **HDM environmental control**: Encasings, HEPA, humidity <50%, 60°C bedding wash weekly
- **SLIT initiation**: Single most effective intervention to break HDM-virus synergy cycle
- **Written asthma action plan for school**: Peak flow monitoring before/after swimming, pre-exercise bronchodilator
- **Viral season preparedness**: "Yellow zone" plan — at first sign of runny nose, step up Foracort to TID and start Levolin SOS proactively
