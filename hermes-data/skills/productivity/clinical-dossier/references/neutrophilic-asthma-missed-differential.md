# Neutrophilic Asthma — Missed Differential (Lesson from Ruhaan Case, Jun 2026)

## The Miss

The Ruhaan Ranka dossier (Jun 2026) was structured as a binary: **Habit Cough (View A) vs Small Airways Disease (View B)**. Neutrophilic asthma was never presented as a distinct View C — but it turned out to be the correct diagnosis per Dr. Srikanta JT.

## Why It Was Missed — 6 Specific Failures

### 1. Binary framing trapped the analysis
The dossier explicitly framed "Two Competing Views." All subsequent analysis went into proving/disproving just those two. Neutrophilic asthma sat outside both and was invisible.

### 2. Normal FeNO → stopped too soon
FeNO 21 ppb is normal. Correct conclusion: "low eosinophilic inflammation." Wrong pivot: treating "low eosinophilic" as "no active asthma inflammation." The question "if it's not eosinophilic, could it be neutrophilic?" was never asked.

### 3. Key clue was buried in literature review
The research deep dive (14 Jun 2026) cited Kheterpal 2022: *"viral triggers cause neutrophilic airway inflammation that is relatively steroid-resistant"* and *"macrolide therapy as alternatives for steroid-resistant cough."* This was embedded under heading #5 ("Treatment-resistant post-viral cough") — not elevated as a standalone diagnostic hypothesis. The data was there but the framework didn't surface it.

### 4. Sleep-resolution pattern was over-weighted for habit cough
The daytime-only cough was treated as nearly pathognomonic for habit cough (classic teaching per Weinberger 2017). But neutrophilic airway inflammation triggered by daytime activity/viral exposure could also settle at rest/sleep — the pattern is compatible with an activity-dependent inflammatory process.

### 5. Normal IgE was misinterpreted
IgE 81.8 IU/mL (ref <295) was correctly called "normal." But the inference — "allergic inflammation not the dominant driver" — stopped there. Normal IgE + confirmed allergic sensitization on SPT actually points toward a **non-eosinophilic** mechanism. Neutrophilic asthma is classically low-IgE, low-FeNO, steroid-resistant.

### 6. Steroid resistance attributed to wrong cause
Predmet 16mg × 5 days didn't settle the cough. This was interpreted as "steroid-refractory → therefore not inflammatory → therefore habit cough." But steroid resistance is the hallmark of neutrophilic asthma (Th17-driven, IL-8-mediated).

## The Path Forward: Recognition Checklist

Consider neutrophilic asthma when ALL of these are present:

| Feature | How It Presents |
|---------|-----------------|
| Normal FeNO | ≤25 ppb |
| Normal/slightly elevated IgE | <295 IU/mL |
| Normal eosinophils | CBC differential |
| Confirmed allergic sensitization | Positive SPT but no clinical eosinophilic response |
| Steroid-resistant cough | No response to oral corticosteroids |
| Viral trigger | Onset or exacerbation with febrile illness |
| Azithromycin response | 2-3 days of AZEE settles cough (anti-inflammatory effect, not antibiotic) |
| Cough pattern | Daytime only (activity-dependent) — CAN disappear during sleep |

## Clinical Pearl for Dossier Construction

When drafting the "Two Competing Views" section, always add a third option:

> **View C — Neutrophilic Asthma (Non-Eosinophilic):** Viral triggers cause a Th17-driven, IL-8-mediated neutrophilic airway inflammation that is steroid-resistant but responds to macrolides for their anti-inflammatory effect. Presents with normal FeNO, normal IgE, daytime-only cough, and poor steroid response. The key therapeutic test: azithromycin 500mg × 5 days.
