# Adolescent Male Vocal Training (Ages 12-16)

Voice training guidance for teenage boys with changing/cracking voices, specifically for Trinity/Rockschool graded exams.

## The Adolescent Voice Change ("Mutational Voice")

### What's happening physically
- The larynx grows and descends (about 1cm in males)
- Vocal folds lengthen and thicken — roughly a 1cm to 1.5cm increase
- This causes **unpredictable pitch breaks, cracks, and loss of control**
- The voice drop is typically 1 octave (from child's ≈C4-C5 to adult's ≈C3-C4)
- **Duration**: 6-18 months of active change, but the voice continues settling until ~20-22

### Don't panic — this is normal
Every male singer goes through this. The cracks, sudden octave drops, and inability to sustain notes are signs the voice is developing, not that the singer is "bad."

## Trinity Exam Preparation

### Grade-appropriate considerations

| Grade | Typical age | What examiner expects |
|-------|-------------|----------------------|
| Grade 1-2 | 10-12 | Simple songs, basic pitch accuracy, enthusiasm |
| Grade 3-4 | 12-14 | Steadier pitch, breath control, basic dynamics |
| Grade 5-6 | 14-16 | Wider range, stylistic awareness, consistent tone |
| Grade 7-8 | 16-18 | Technical proficiency, artistic interpretation |

**Important:** Trinity examiners at lower grades do NOT penalise adolescent voice cracks. They grade on potential, musicality, and technique within the student's current capability.

### Repertoire choices
- **Safe range**: D3-E4 (avoid songs requiring sustained chest voice above E4)
- **Ideal for 13-15yo males**: Pop ballads with moderate range
- **Strong choices**: "Talking to the Moon" (Bruno Mars), "Yesterday" (Beatles), "Hallelujah" (Cohen), "Perfect" (Ed Sheeran)
- **Avoid until post-change**: Songs requiring belting (Whitney Houston, high rock, musical theatre belt)

## Weekly Training Routine

### Daily Foundation (5-10 min)

**1. Breath (daily)**
- Lying down, hand on belly, slow breath in (4 counts), hold (4), out (4)
- Diaphragmatic breathing — shoulders should NOT rise
- Siren slides on "ng" — glide smoothly from comfortable low to high without pushing

**2. SOVT Exercises (Semi-Occluded Vocal Tract) — critical for changing voices**
- Lip trills / "motorboat" lips — any melody, including the song they're learning
- Straw phonation — hum/sing through a stirring straw (creates back-pressure that stabilises folds)
- Humming — gentle, forward, feeling vibration in lips/nose
- These are **the most important exercises for a cracking voice** — they keep the vocal folds adducted without strain

**3. Warm-up (5 min)**
- Glides on "oo" — comfortable low to comfortable high and back
- 5-note descending patterns (sol-fa-mi-re-do) on "mum"
- Octave slides on "waw" — helps bridge the passaggio (break zone)

### Specific Issue Work

| Issue | Exercise | Frequency |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Cracks | Lip trills through the break zone | Daily |
| Breath running out | Long sirens on "ah" — aim for 10-15 seconds | Daily |
| Pitch wobble | Long tones with piano/tuner — hold steady for 8 sec | 3x week |
| Thin voice | "Mum-mum-mum" patterns, descending | 3x week |
| Weak low notes | "Haw-haw-haw" (breathy, open) on low pitches | 2x week |

## What NOT to do

- ❌ **Do NOT push chest voice higher** — this creates strain and reinforces cracks
- ❌ **Do NOT try to "sing through" fatigue** — if the voice is tired, rest
- ❌ **Do NOT avoid the break zone** — gentle exploration with SOVT exercises builds coordination
- ❌ **Do NOT force vibrato** — natural vibrato emerges from relaxed, well-supported singing
- ❌ **Do NOT over-practice** — 20-30 minutes/day is enough for this age

## Nutritional / Health

- Hydration is critical — dry folds crack more
- Avoid dairy 1-2 hours before singing (mucus production)
- Adequate sleep directly affects vocal control
- Reflux / acidity damages folds — avoid spicy food before practice

## Communicating with Parents

When presenting this assessment to Nishant (or similar parent), frame it as:

1. **Strengths first** — always lead with what's working well
2. **Explain the biology** — "this is normal for a 14-year-old, here's what's happening in his throat"
3. **Concrete exercises** — not vague advice like "practice more", but specific daily drills
4. **Manage expectations** — Trinity examiners at his level don't penalise adolescent cracks
5. **Path forward** — show the progression: "at this age we build coordination, range stabilisation happens naturally over 18-24 months"
