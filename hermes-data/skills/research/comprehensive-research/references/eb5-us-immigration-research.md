## Source of Funds — India-Specific Challenges

The highest-scrutiny phase for Indian HNI applicants. USCIS demands a complete paper trail. The 7-year financial audit is standard.

### Key Documents (7-Year Trail)
- ITR-V (income tax returns with acknowledgments) — 7 years
- Form 26AS / Annual Information Statement (AIS) — the master tax transaction document
- Bank statements (savings, current, FD) — 7 years
- Sale deeds for property transactions
- Gift deeds (registered if immovable property)
- CA certification on letterhead — comprehensive "Network Statement" tracing the investment capital
- Form 16 / Form 16A (TDS certificates)
- Audited financials of owned companies

### LRS Remittance
- Limit: $250,000 per person per financial year
- Family of 4 = $1,000,000 capacity per year — sufficient for $800k
- Each member files Form A2 individually
- **Emigration remittance** (capital account, not subject to LRS cap) is an alternative if the family genuinely relocates — requires CA certificate confirming emigration

## US Tax Basics for Green Card Holders

### Worldwide Income
All global income is reportable to IRS from date of green card issuance. Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) available for foreign-source income.

**The Indian Stock Trap (IRC §865):** If you sell Indian stocks AFTER becoming a US resident, the gain is US-source income. The FTC cannot offset Indian capital gains tax paid because FTC only works for foreign-source income. **Solution: Sell all Indian stocks before the green card date.**

**PFIC (Passive Foreign Investment Company):** Indian mutual funds are PFICs under US tax law. Gains taxed at top marginal rate + interest charge. **Solution: Liquidate all Indian mutual funds before GC date.**

### FBAR (FinCEN 114)
- Trigger: $10,000 aggregate in foreign accounts at any time
- All Indian accounts: savings, current, FD, demat, NRE/NRO
- Penalties: Willful — greater of $100k or 50% of account value; Non-willful — up to $10k per violation

### FATCA (Form 8938)
- Thresholds (living in US): Single $50k/$75k, MFJ $100k/$150k
- Includes shares in private Indian companies (DRAAS, AHFL) if ≥10% ownership

### Form 5471
- Required if ≥10% owner of a foreign corporation (DRAAS, AHFL)
- Complex reporting of income, earnings, and profits

### US Estate Tax
- Exemption: $13,990,000 per person (2025) → SUNSETS to ~$7M after 31 Dec 2025
- Rate: 40% above exemption
- Portability: Yes (unused exemption passes to surviving spouse)
- QDOT needed if surviving spouse is non-citizen
- ALL worldwide assets (including Indian real estate, company shares) included in gross estate for US persons

### Gift Tax
- Annual exclusion (2025): $19,000 per donee
- Lifetime exemption: $13.99M (unified with estate tax)
- Split gifts: Married couples can double the annual exclusion
- Non-citizen spouse: $190,000 annual exclusion

## Foreign Trust (Indian Trust) — US Tax Treatment

### Core Classification
- An irrevocable Indian trust benefiting children is a **Foreign Non-Grantor Trust (FNGT)** for US purposes
- IRC §672(f): Only revocable trusts or trusts distributing only to grantor/spouse are grantor trusts for foreign trusts

### Key Rules
- **Settlor as trustee:** Allowed. Being trustee alone does NOT trigger grantor trust status under §672(f)
- **Trustee fees:** Reasonable compensation for actual services is permissible
- **Form 3520:** Filed by US beneficiary for EVERY distribution from a foreign trust. Penalty: 35% of gross reportable amount
- **Throwback tax:** Accumulation distributions taxed at beneficiary rate + interest charge. Punitive.
- **Form 3520-A:** Not filed by FNGT (only when there's a US owner of a foreign trust)

### Optimal Strategy
**Distribute trust assets to children BEFORE they become US persons.** This avoids:
- Form 3520 penalties (35% of gross)
- Throwback tax on accumulated income
- Annual compliance burden

If the trust must continue after beneficiaries become US persons: distribute ALL income currently (no accumulation) and file Form 3520 on time every year.

## Exit Tax — Green Card Surrender

### The 8-Year LTR Clock
- A green card holder becomes a Long-Term Resident (LTR) after holding the GC for **8 of the last 15 taxable years**
- Only LTRs who surrender are "expatriates" under IRC §877A
- **If you surrender before Year 8: No exit tax, no Section 2801 tax, no mark-to-market**
- **This is the single most important planning lever for the family**

### Covered Expatriate Tests
1. Net worth > $2M (LIKELY for Nishant as real estate CEO)
2. Avg annual US tax liability > ~$210k (possible)
3. Failure to certify tax compliance (avoidable)

### Consequences of Being a Covered Expatriate
1. **Mark-to-market exit tax** (IRC §877A): Deemed sale of all worldwide assets day before expatriation. Gains above ~$866k exemption taxed at up to 23.8%
2. **Section 2801 — Permanent 40% tax**: On the RECIPIENT (US person) of any gift/bequest from a Covered Expatriate. No statute of limitations. This is permanent.
3. **Trust on expatriation** (IRC §877A(f)): If you're a grantor of a foreign trust, the trust is deemed to have transferred all assets to you — deemed sale at FMV

### Surrender Process
- File Form I-407 (Record of Abandonment of LPR Status)
- Date of formal surrender = expatriation date for US tax purposes
- Always do it formally — never simply stop living in the US (creates ambiguous tax date)
